# Appendix A: The Dynasty, Set Down Plainly

*Being a register of the god-family of the Lands Between, with the exact degree of every kinship, and the exact weight of every claim.*

---

A register is a dull instrument, and I offer no apology for it. This history has watched what becomes of families whose kinships are recited loosely: stepchildren called sons until the sons believed it, rumors called mothers until the mothers were worshipped, a half-brother's corpse dressed as a bridegroom because the words *brother* and *lord* had been allowed to blur. The chapters of this book tell those stories at length; the present appendix exists so that no reader, closing the book, carries away a looser genealogy than the relics permit. Here every relation is given at its exact degree — step- where the relics say step-, half- where half-, consort where consort — and every claim is given in the grammar it has earned. When I write that a thing is *stated outright*, some surviving text states it. When I write *by inference*, I show the chain. When I write *rumored only*, I hand you a rumor, and you will kindly not hand it to your children as fact. And where the record refuses to speak, I write that it refuses, for in this family the silences are records too.

One warning before the roll is called. The whole dynasty hangs from a single fact so strange that the register cannot be read without it, and I place it first.

## The God

**MARIKA, called the Eternal, who is also RADAGON of the Golden Order.** One entry, two names, and the entry is two names because the being was two selves. That Marika, god of the Golden Order and vessel of its Ring, and Radagon, her second lord, were one being is stated outright — the capital's own stone yielded the confession, three words buried beneath a statue that name the two selves as one being, and nothing more is needed. What no relic supplies is the *mechanism* — whether Radagon was made, grown, or always latent; when the doubling began; which self, if either, has the better claim to be called first. The register therefore keeps two lines for one being, and must: Marika took two consorts, and Radagon, before he was her consort, was another woman's husband. Two marriages, three spouses, one god — the arithmetic is monstrous, and it is the family's foundation. Of Marika's own begetting: her girlhood among the shaman folk of their harvested village is mine by inference, several relics leaning together, chief among them the braid of her own hair sent home in her place; her blood among the Numen, that rare long-lived people said to come from beyond this world, is rumored only, on the print of an assassin's knife; and her father and mother are nothing at all — the record refuses.

## The Consorts

**GODFREY, first Elden Lord, first consort of Marika; in his first life and his last, HOARAH LOUX, chieftain of the badlands.** His station is stated outright, and I mark the direction of it, for the register's grammar depends on such things: the god takes a lord; the lord is the consort; no relic ever styles Marika *his* anything. That he fathered her first three children is not stated outright anywhere — the reader may be surprised, and should be — but comes by inference through the golden lineage, which the relics define as Godfrey and his offspring and into which those children are written. Stripped of grace and banished when his wars ran out, he returned with the tarnished exiles' last generation and fell before the Elden Throne, a chieftain again, having shed the lord.

**RENNALA of the Full Moon, queen of the Carian royal house of Liurnia.** Never Marika's consort, and never let a careless chart make her one: she enters this register as Radagon's first wife, stated outright, wedded when war turned to marriage beneath the full moon. She bore him three children, and when he left her for the god — that is, for himself — she was left mad with grief in her library, cradling an amber egg. She lives there yet, so far as my last reports run.

**RADAGON, as consort — the peculiar case.** The register must list him twice, once as god and once as lord, because the relics do. Second Elden Lord and second consort of Marika: stated outright, by the keeper of the church where the vows were sworn. That the marriage joined a being to itself is the consequence of the statue's three words, and I decline to soften it. Whether the union was policy, punishment, or something the Order's geometry required, the record refuses to say.

## The Children

Ten are set down here: seven of Marika's body, three of Radagon's by Rennala who were raised to demigod rank as stepchildren of the god. Every child of Marika is at least the half-sibling of every other — a derivation, not a quotation, but one the parentage lines compel.

**GODWYN, called the Golden.** Son of Marika, stated outright; son of Godfrey by inference through the golden lineage, no relic naming the father in so many words. First of the demigods to die — and the register must be exact even about dying: his *soul* was slain on the Night of the Black Knives, and his body, denied death, lives on beneath the earth as the Prince of Death, growing.

**MORGOTT, the Omen King, last defender of Leyndell.** Son of Marika, stated outright; son of Godfrey by the same golden-lineage inference as his brothers, and no more than that. Twin of Mohg — the twinship itself stated outright. Born horned, hidden beneath the capital he would spend his life defending; he fought the last Tarnished under a borrowed name and his own, and fell in the throne room of the dynasty that had never once owned him aloud.

**MOHG, Lord of Blood.** Son of Marika, stated outright; Godfrey's by inference only; Morgott's twin, stated outright. He fled the sewers for an outer god of blood, stole away the slumbering Miquella to found a dynasty upon, and wished himself that half-brother's consort — the wish stated outright, its requital nowhere. Slain by the last Tarnished; his corpse was then used as the flesh vessel for Radahn's return, a fate no register was ever built to phrase.

**RADAHN, called the Starscourge.** Son of Radagon and Rennala, both parents stated outright; stepson of Marika; half-brother of the twins Miquella and Malenia through the father — the "half" a derivation, though the relics do have him addressed as *lord brother*. The rot took his mind after Aeonia; he was slain at his own festival; and he was returned — in Mohg's body, by rite and theft — to stand as Miquella's Promised Consort in the Land of Shadow, where he fell a second time. The consort bond is stated outright; that it was chosen rests on a childhood wish of Miquella's whose answer the record never gives.

**RYKARD, Lord of Blasphemy.** Son of Radagon and Rennala, stated outright; stepson of Marika. He fed himself to the God-Devouring Serpent and became one flesh with it — stated outright, and I remind the reader that in this family such sentences are literal. Slain in his manor upon the volcano.

**RANNI, the Witch.** Daughter of Radagon and Rennala, stated outright; stepdaughter of Marika; Empyrean once, by the Fingers' choosing. On the Night of the Black Knives her own flesh was destroyed while her soul went on in a doll of her making — the inverse of Godwyn's death, and by every sign the two halves of one plot. She cut her ties to the Two Fingers utterly. Of her whereabouts after the burning, one of the six dawns bears her name, and I do not choose among dawns.

**MIQUELLA, called the Kind.** Child of Marika and Radagon — of a single god, both parents stated outright, and the strangeness of that sentence is the whole point of this register. Twin of Malenia, stated outright; Empyrean; cursed with eternal childhood. Stolen in his sleep by Mohg; abandoned his own flesh; shed, piece by piece, whatever of himself impeded godhood — a discarding this register meets again below, under Trina. His second ascension ended at the top of the tower in Shadow, before the gate, at the last Tarnished's hand.

**MALENIA, Blade of Miquella.** Child of the same single god, stated outright; twin of Miquella, stated outright; Empyrean; cursed from birth with the scarlet rot she spent a lifetime refusing. She fought Radahn to a standstill at Aeonia and bloomed, at what cost that region still testifies. She fell in the Haligtree's root, undefeated in her own accounting until the last.

**MESSMER, the Impaler.** Son of Marika, stated outright — his remembrance names her, and his dying breath cursed her. His father the record refuses, and the refusal is examined at this appendix's close. Elder brother to a fire-visioned sister his relics do not name. He waged the crusade against the horned folk, was sealed with his fire in the veiled land, and fell there to the last Tarnished.

**MELINA.** The register's faintest line, and I draw it faintly. Daughter of Marika by inference — her own words give her a mother inside the Erdtree, a birth at its foot, a purpose of kindling; no text closes the ring. That she is Messmer's unnamed sister is likewise inference, the kindling and the vision fitting her like a made glove. Her father is the same refusal as Messmer's. Some scholars venture that she was the Gloam-Eyed Queen besides; nothing in the record compels it, and it waits outside this register. Her fate stands at the fork the chronicle of the burning records: spent as kindling at the Forge, or living yet with a vow of Destined Death — the register cannot close what the accounts leave open.

## The Kin and Shadows

**MALIKETH, the Black Blade; in his hiding, GURRANQ, the Beast Clergyman.** Half-brother of Marika — stated outright in the relics as this history's tongue receives them, though I owe the reader a scruple: older renderings of the same texts can be read as *brother-by-oath* rather than by blood, and I record the kinship with that second reading standing in the margin. Beyond dispute is his office: shadowbound beast, given to Marika at her Empyrean choosing as the Fingers' leash upon their god, and keeper of Destined Death sealed within his flesh. He fell at Farum Azula, and death got out.

**RELLANA, the Twin Moon Knight.** Younger sister of Rennala, stated outright — thus aunt, by derivation, to Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni. She crossed into the Land of Shadow to stand as the Sword of Messmer, stated outright, and when she did so is one of the record's hardest knots (the history of the two courts unties what can be untied). No kinship binds her to Messmer but the sworn kind; the register notes that the sworn kind held.

**BLAIDD, the Half-Wolf.** Shadowbound companion of Ranni, stated outright — the same leash as Maliketh, one generation on, and the fail-safe the Fingers kept against exactly the betrayal Ranni committed. The leash was tried, and it broke him; he ended raving at her tower's foot.

**TRINA, called Saint.** The goddess of sleep is Miquella's discarded other half — by inference, several witnesses converging where no single text speaks: the devotee who called her his lord's other self, the fissure where the abandoned love was left, her own drowsing words. The claim is disputed at its edges, and I carry it as assembled, not as stated.

**MILLICENT.** Of Malenia's blood, born of the Aeonia bloom — a bud, an offshoot, a daughter, a sister; her own account keeps every one of those doors open, and I will not shut one for her. By inference she is set down here as Malenia's offshoot, degree unresolved. She died as she chose, whole.

## The Lineage After

**GODRICK, the Grafted.** Descendant of Godfrey and the golden lineage, stated outright — and the register stops precisely there, for the relics do: how many generations stand between the first lord and this least of his line, no text says, and every intervening name on the genealogists' charts is invention. He grafted the limbs of the dead onto himself in Stormveil, and died there.

**GODEFROY, the Grafted.** A member of the golden lineage by the naming of his prison alone — inference, and thin inference. That he is Godrick's ancestor rests on the resemblance of two names, and I decline it.

**NEPHELI LOUX.** Adopted daughter of Gideon Ofnir, stated outright — the one certain kinship she has. That she is Godfrey's blood carries the name and the warrior styling and not one relic more; rumored only, if that. Some accounts seat her afterward on Limgrave's throne at Stormveil; I set down the claim of the throne, not the throne.

**KENNETH HAIGHT.** Included to be excluded. He claims Limgrave's rule by right — the claim stated outright, loudly, from atop a ruin — but its basis is nowhere given, and no relic ties his blood to Godfrey's line at any degree. In the accounts that seat Nepheli, he judged himself unfit and bent the knee. The register records him as what he is: a claim without a kinship.

## The Cosmic House

Above the dynasty sits a stranger household, kept brief here because the account of the expedition into shadow keeps it at length. The **GREATER WILL**, outer god behind the golden Order, has a daughter stated outright: **METYR, Mother of Fingers**, the first star to fall, sent and then abandoned. Metyr is mother of all Two Fingers and Fingercreepers, stated outright; whether she mothered the Three Fingers as well her remembrance pointedly omits, and I let the omission stand. The **ELDEN BEAST**, the Will's vassal, became the living form of the Elden Ring — stated outright — and Marika its vessel, by inference every witness accepts and no line quite speaks. The **TWINBIRD** is said to be the envoy of a nameless outer god of death and mother of the Deathbirds — *said to be*, the shield that carries the account hedging its own words, and so do I.

## The Two Refusals

Last, the gaps — for this register would falsify the family if it tidied them away. Two of Marika's children have no father anywhere in the record: Messmer, and (if the inferences above hold) Melina. Understand that these are not ordinary losses. This land's relics are prodigal with lineage; it is the one subject on which they scarcely stop talking; the fathers of every other child in this appendix can be had at some stated or inferable degree. For these two — the two children of fire, the two the Erdtree's histories never mention — the sweep of every remembrance, every blade, every banner yields nothing. A gap that perfect is not an accident of time; it is workmanship. I have called it a redaction in the body of this history, and I repeat the judgment here in the register's flat voice: father, unstated; the record refuses; the refusal has an author. They are, I have come to think, this family's most eloquent records — for the dynasty that wrote down every golden begetting wrote down, twice, with equal care, a silence. The reader now knows the family exactly as the relics allow it to be known: no better, no worse, and no rounder.
