# KINDLING
## The Testament of Melina

*A first-person narrative for audio, in fourteen movements and a coda.*

---

## Prologue: Ash

Ash remembers fire. That is all ash is — the memory of fire, held loosely, waiting for wind.

I am what ash remembers.

I was born at the foot of the Erdtree. I say *born* because I have no better word, and because the word is a small warm room I like to sit in, even knowing the walls may be painted on. I do not remember a cradle. I do not remember a first breath. I remember gold — gold above me, gold through me, gold the way drowning people remember water — and I remember a voice giving me a purpose, folding it into my chest the way you fold a letter you intend someone to carry a long way.

Then fire. Then nothing. Then this.

Understand what I am, you who listen. I have hands, and my hands bear the smooth pale geography of old burns. I have a face, and half of it obeys me. My right eye is gold-brown and open, and it has watched the Lands Between for longer than I can count. My left eye is closed beneath a dark mark like the print of a talon, three-pronged, deliberate. I did not close that eye. I do not know who did. Sometimes, in the way you can feel a sealed room behind a wall, I can feel something behind it — patient, and older than my patience.

I have no body. I have the *idea* of a body, drawn in spirit and firelight, and it holds together the way a promise holds together: only so long as someone keeps it.

The letter in my chest — my purpose, my mother's purpose — burned with everything else. For an age I wandered with an empty envelope where my reason should be, knowing only that it had been given at the Erdtree, and that I could not return there alone. A spirit tethered to grace, to golden guidance, to the kindness of strangers. A messenger who has forgotten the message but not the address.

So I did what the forgotten do.

I watched.

I learned the Lands Between the way a physician learns a dying patient — every fracture, every fever, every place where the skin of the world had split and something old was showing through. I watched demigods rot on their thrones. I watched the deathless pile up like unfinished sentences. I watched a golden Order hold its breath for so long that the breath itself went stale.

And I made one friend.

---

## I. The Horse That Is Not a Horse

He found me. Let no one tell it otherwise.

Torrent is not a horse, though he wears the shape of one the way I wear the shape of a woman — sincerely, and with something underneath. He is a spirit-steed, blue-grey as woodsmoke, with horns like a stag that took a wrong turn in its dreaming. He carries a whistle's summons in his bones and an old grief in his eyes, and he chooses. That is the thing about Torrent that no one understands until they have been refused by him. He *chooses*.

He had a master before me. I never asked. Between the two of us there are enough locked rooms that we have learned to walk the corridors politely. But some nights, when he sleeps standing the way steeds do, his ears move as if listening to a voice pitched sweet and high, and I dream — I do not know why — of a boy with long golden hair riding him through a field of pale flowers, a boy the very air wanted to obey. When I wake, the dream closes like an eye.

Whoever loved him first, Torrent came to me, and we made our slow orbits of a broken land, a dead woman and a borrowed horse, waiting for something neither of us could name.

Then the Tarnished began returning.

You have to understand what that meant. Long ago — this is one of the letters I *can* read, because my mother's words still lie about the land like fallen leaves, and I can lift them to my lips and make them speak — long ago the Queen stripped her first lord and all his warriors of grace and sent them into exile beyond the fog. *Fight, live, die abroad,* she told them. And in another church, softer, almost tender, she made the second promise: *when you have died, I will call you home, and you will make war for the Ring.*

Now the fog was giving them back. Guided ghosts with burned-out eyes, washing ashore, dying, rising, dying again. Candidates. The land filled with them the way a wound fills with new blood, and most of it clotted, and failed, and went mad in ditches.

Torrent and I went looking anyway. We were looking for a very particular kind of fool.

We found you in a graveyard at the bottom of the world, freshly killed by something stitched out of other people's arms.

I remember leaning over you. I remember Torrent's breath, and the way his ears stood — not wary, not weary. *Certain.* He had watched a hundred Tarnished crawl out of that dark and he had never once stood like that.

"So it's to be this one," I said to him. "Very well."

You never heard me. You were busy being dead, which — I would learn — is a hobby of yours. I left flasks of crimson and cerulean beside you like a woman leaving milk out for something feral, and I withdrew into the smoke of myself, and I waited for you at the first fire.

Torrent chose you first. Remember that, when you weigh everything I did after.

I only agreed with him.

## II. An Accord

You reached the open land, and you rested at grace, and I stepped out of the gold to lie to you.

There is no prettier word for it. I told you what a Finger Maiden is — vessel of guidance, turner of runes into strength, bride of a Tarnished's purpose — and I let you believe I was offering to be yours. And you, maidenless, grave-cold, alone as a dropped knife: what were you going to say?

Hear the bargain as I actually meant it. *I cannot walk to the foot of the Erdtree. You cannot become anything without hands like mine. Carry me, and I will make you strong enough to carry me.* Two beggars agreeing to be each other's crutch. I dressed a need in the clothes of a blessing, and I have never fully forgiven myself, and I would do it again this hour.

I gave you the whistle — a small gold ring, warm as a held coin — and I said his name for you: Torrent. I told you he had chosen you, that he had had your measure from the very start, and I asked you to treat him well. That part was no performance. That was one mother-tongue I actually speak.

Some things I told you plainly, if you thought to ask. That I am no true maiden. That I burned, long ago, and go on now without a body. That the guidance of the Two Fingers is not mine to give; I could only imitate its shape, the way a widow can set two places at table. You accepted the counterfeit with such ease that I wondered, sometimes, whether you knew and did not care — whether you, of all people, understood what it is to perform a role until the role begins, terribly, to perform you.

And so we went — you in the saddle, I in the smoke; you gathering runes like a gambler, I stringing them into strength through the palm of my hand.

I told myself it was an arrangement.

I have told myself many things.

---

## III. The Weight of Hands

Let me tell you what the runes are, since no one ever told you.

They are memories. The residue of the dead — of everything golden and ordered in a life, shaken loose when the life ends. When you knelt at grace and I asked, each time, *may I?* — and I always asked, because a woman with no body must own at least her borrowed touch — I laid my hand upon you and poured the dead through you like light through a window, and the window grew stronger for being shone through.

That was the arrangement. Here is what was not:

Your pulse.

I have no heart. I remember having one the way you remember a house you lived in as a child — the floor plan certain, the smell gone. And every time I rested my hand on you I could feel yours: stupid, stubborn, thudding away in its cage as if death were not standing directly behind it taking notes. I began — this is a confession — I began to look forward to it. The way the desert must look forward to rain it cannot keep.

So I would ask you questions, to make the moment last the length of an answer. What do you fight for. What do you place your faith in. What will you do, wanderer, when the Ring is yours and the world is a wet lump of clay in your two mortal hands. You gave your answers — halting, or grand, or honest, which is rarest — and I filed each one away in the place where my purpose used to be, because an empty room wants furnishing, and yours were the only furnishings on offer.

And I tested you. I admit it freely now. Every early league of our road, I was watching to see whether grace truly pulled you, or whether you were one more clot of ambition that would go bad in a ditch. Torrent trusted you from the first hour. I did not. Trust, for me, is a scar tissue; it forms slowly and it does not flex.

But you kept dying, and you kept refusing to make death mean what it meant. You would come apart on some knight's sword and reassemble at the fire, and swear, and stand, and go *back*. There is a kind of prayer in that. Cruder than any incantation, and more sincere.

Somewhere between one grace and another, I stopped testing.

I offered you the last true guidance I had — a table of the lost, a roundtable of the also-abandoned, where the fireless could warm themselves on each other. I took your arm to carry you there, and for the length of that crossing, a dead woman and a deathless fool traveled as one flesh.

You could not know what that cost me, or what it paid.

## IV. My Mother's Voice in My Mouth

Now I must tell you the strangest thing I do, and you must not be frightened of me afterward.

The Queen's words do not die. Where Marika spoke, in the old stone churches with their broken roofs and their rain-fed altars, the words remain — coiled in the air like heat above a snuffed candle. Word-spirits. Echoes. And I, alone in all the Lands Between so far as I know, can find them, and take them into my mouth, and give them back unchanged.

Do you remember the first time? A ruined church, a statue with her arms spread as if to catch a falling world, and I said, *words of the vanished Queen linger here — would you hear them?* And you nodded, and I opened my mouth, and my voice went out of me and *hers* came through —

— cold as coronation gold, vast as weather. Grace withdrawn like a knife from a wound. Exile pronounced like a blessing. *Become lords, become gods, or be sacrifices.* A mother sorting her children into thrones and kindling.

You always watched me strangely after. You were right to.

Because here is what I never said, at any fire, in any weather, and I will say it now only because fire keeps no secrets from ash:

The words that linger in the churches are the Queen's. The purpose that was folded into my chest was my mother's. In all my wandering I have never once dared to speak both sentences in a single breath — to let *Queen* and *mother* stand close enough to touch. Some doors you do not open. You only rest your hand against the wood, and feel how warm it is, and step back.

But her words pass through me like water through a channel cut for exactly that water. Her secret gold gathers in my hands when I fight — a little tree of light, kindness without Order, the incantation she made once for an empty village where there was no one left to heal. Explain that, if you can, without opening the door.

I cannot.

So I carried the Queen through the land in my throat, one church at a time, and every echo I gave you was a coin with two faces: guidance for you, and for me — archaeology. I was excavating a woman. Listening for the seam where the god ends and the mother begins.

At the Grand Lift she offered the old choice: within the Order, or outside it in the powerless dark. At the little church on the plateau she did something stranger — she *questioned* her own gold, called for an end to blind faith, demanded that belief learn to think. That echo I have turned over more than any other. A god quietly filing the bars of her own cage.

And I would think of the letter burned out of my chest, and wonder what kind of instruction a caged god writes.

I would find out.

## V. The Tailor's Tears

There was a demi-human. You remember him — of course you do; you were kind to him, which is half of why I kept you.

Boc. A tailor without a shop, a son without a mother, a gentle thing the world had used as a lesson in what it does to gentle things. He took to following you the way warmth follows a lamp. And at the fires, while you sharpened and counted and did your grim little inventories, I watched him.

I saw him wait for you like a dog that has decided to believe in one particular door. I told you — *go, speak with him; he is eager for you.* You went. You always went. I collected these facts about you the way other women collect jewelry.

And then one evening I saw him weeping, quietly, into the work of his hands, and I understood: he wept for his mother. For her needle and her praise. What he wanted from all the vast catastrophe of the Lands Between was to hear one voice say *you are beautiful* — and the voice was gone, so he was sewing his way toward its ghost.

I sat very still, and something in me leaned against the sealed door of itself.

Does being born of a mother do that to a person? Plant a voice in them so deep that its absence becomes a compass? I asked it almost aloud. I ask it still. Because I search myself for that compass, you see, and I find — I do not know what I find. A purpose is not a lullaby. An instruction is not a hand in the hair. I was born at the foot of the Erdtree and given a task, and I love my mother — I do, with a love like a stone I cannot put down — but when I reach for the *warmth* of her, memory gives me gold, and gold, and gold, and gold is not warm. Gold is only bright.

Perhaps I was born as others are, and the fire took those memories with the rest. Perhaps I was born as trees are, or as prayers are. Perhaps a god's daughter is a thing a god *makes*, the way she makes an order, or an age, or a blade.

Boc knew his mother's face. Weep, tailor. Yours is the richer inheritance.

I never said any of this at the fire. I said, *he misses her,* and I looked into the flames, and you, blessedly, for once, did not ask me one of your questions.

## VI. The Brother the Fire Shows Me

Spirits dream. No one believes it, but we do — we dream the way a bell rings after it is struck, long and low and mostly for ourselves.

In my dreams there is fire, always. And in the fire, more often than chance allows, there is a man.

Red-haired. Tall as a spear and sad as one, too — a weapon that knows it is a weapon. Serpents move about him like affectionate rope. He stands in a land the sun has forgotten, under a tree that is not my golden tree but its shadow, its withered echo, hung with veils. And armies move at his word, and cities burn at his word, and his face never once looks like a man who wants any of it.

One of his eyes is golden and wrong. Sealed. Something vast coils behind it, and the seal on it is grace — I know the flavor of that gold, I would know it anywhere, it is the gold of my own left eye's little prison — and in the dream I always lift my hand to my sealed eye at the same moment he lifts his hand to his, and we regard each other across the fire like two letters written by the same hand in the same year and posted to different wars.

*Little sister,* the dream says. Not his voice. The fire's.

And I see it then, the thing we share, bedded in him as it is bedded in me: a vision of flame. Kindling. Not fire wielded — fire *carried*, the way a candle carries its own ending. He was made able to burn a sacred tree.

So was I.

I wake from these dreams and lie in the dark of myself doing arithmetic no daughter should have to do. A mother who seals what she loves. A mother who hides what she seals. One child dispatched into shadow with his serpent and his shame, to scour a land off every map; one child kept — or lost, or burned, or all three — at the foot of the golden tree with a letter in her chest. Two children with fire folded into them like steel folded into a blade, and the whole bright world never told either name.

I do not know his name. The dream will not give it, and I will not invent one; I have been too often the victim of other people's inventions to inflict my own on a stranger. But if he lives, if he stands even now under that veiled and withered tree — brother, hear this, wherever the fire finds you:

She sealed my eye as well. I also was made to burn.

And I hope, at the end of everything, that one of us got to choose.

## VII. The Witch With the Mirrored Eye

Twice in our travels I felt her, and once I saw her, and we have never spoken. Some mirrors you do not speak to.

The witch Ranni. You met her in the flesh — no. Forgive me. You met her in the *doll*, which is precisely not the flesh; that is the whole of her genius. Four arms, one face borrowed from a dead teacher, a hat like a horizon. And one eye closed.

Mark that. Her right. My left. Stand us face to face and we make a single woman looking at you.

I know her story the way rivers know each other — by arriving, eventually, at the same sea. An Empyrean, appointed to inherit the whole golden machine, who looked at her inheritance and saw a leash. So she stole a sliver of Death — oh, the audacity of it still lifts the hair I no longer truly have — and she killed *her own body*, slipped the Order's hook by leaving it nothing to hold, and went on as spirit and will inside a puppet of her own election.

Burned flesh, discarded on a tower. A woman continuing past her own death on the strength of sheer refusal.

You see why I do not speak to the mirror.

We are not the same, whatever the resemblance whispers. Her parents have names the world may say aloud. Her rebellion is cold, lunar, a geometry of escape — she would take the very stars out of the Order's reach and be gone. Mine is warmer and worse. I do not want to leave the world. I want to *repair* it, and the repair requires my own two burning hands, and there is no doll waiting for me on the far side of the fire.

But we share the horse.

There — I have said the thing I circle. She held a bell that calls spirits to your side, and she gave it to you, and she said it was entrusted to her *by Torrent's former master, for Torrent's new master.* I have turned that sentence like a lock-pick. Former master. Was that me? I gave you his whistle; was the bell some older half of the same bequest, passed through hands I cannot remember owning? Or was it the golden-haired boy from Torrent's dreaming, the one the air obeyed, moving pieces on a board so large that a witch and a dead woman are adjacent squares and never know it?

I have no memory older than ash. Everything before the fire is other people's testimony.

So when we passed near her tower once, in the blue hour, and I felt her attention swing toward me like a lighthouse — I did not go up. I inclined my head to the dark, one closed eye to another, and she — I will swear it — inclined hers.

Two women who refused to stay dead, agreeing to disagree about what the refusal is *for*.

Ride on, Torrent. We are watched by family, or by coincidence, and in this land I have never once been able to tell them apart.

---

## VIII. Roads on the Altus

The plateau smelled of my childhood.

I did not know I had a childhood until it smelled of one — that is how memory came back to me, not as pictures but as *weather*. We climbed out of the lake-lands into the high gold country where the Erdtree stops being scenery and becomes sky, and something in me turned like a key.

*I was born here.* At the foot of the tree. It came to me at a grace by the roadside, plain as a laid table. My mother — there, at the root of everything golden — placed my purpose into me with her own two hands. I stood inside that returning memory the way you stand inside a house you inherited from someone you cannot ask questions of anymore.

The road disagreed with my nostalgia, of course. The Altus is a beautiful lie over a working slaughterhouse; you saw the wagons, the soldiers, the windmills turning above dancing women who would not stop dancing. And once, on that road, we passed a girl.

Red-haired, and armed, and burning quietly with the specific fever of someone assembling herself as she goes. She wore traveler's clothes — sturdy things, sewn for young women who walk out into the world to look their fate in the eye. I know the make.

I own the same set.

She was climbing toward a mother too — toward a name that was also a question: daughter, sister, offshoot, echo? Some women are given bodies and denied origins. We are a guild, though we never meet, and our uniform is a good walking cloak and an unanswerable question.

I watched her out of sight and I thought: little pilgrim, whatever you find at the top — make it *yours* before the finding makes you *its*. I did not yet know I was rehearsing.

Then Leyndell. The capital, ash-drifted and holy, a dragon draped across its walls like a punctuation mark at the end of an argument, and above it all the trunk of the tree, so vast that looking up at it is a kind of falling.

And at the rampart grace, the thing I had bargained for happened, quietly, without trumpets:

My tether went slack. This close to the root, I could *move* — under my own will, by my own way, no grace to lean on, no Tarnished to carry me. You cannot imagine it. Legs that answer. Distance that obeys. A dead woman standing in the wind, discovering that the wind is negotiable.

So I kept my side of our accord and ended it. I thanked you — you looked stricken; I pretended not to see, and I have kept the sight of it ever since — and I told you the truth: that you could yet be Elden Lord, that the strength I had strung into you was yours to keep, that Torrent would stay. And I walked away into my own city, alone, to find out what I was for.

The accord was fulfilled.

Nothing else was.

## IX. The Room With the Dagger

Where does a dead woman go to find her purpose? Down, as it turns out. Purpose is buried where all important things are buried in Leyndell: behind the walls, under the gold, off the edge of the official map.

There is a shaft in the capital where a great lift runs, and partway down that throat of stone, a lip, a ledge, a doorway that no procession has ever used. I found a furnished room. Modest. Kept. A desk. And outside its threshold, a corpse in an official's black — the attire of those who did the Order's *unlisted* work. Watching. Executions. Rituals the gold does not sing about.

On the desk lay a dagger.

Small, golden-bladed, made for a leaping flame. And my hands knew it. Before memory, before thought, my burned hands knew that dagger the way your throat knows your own name — and it all came through me at once, not as a picture but as a *sentence*, the letter in my chest suddenly legible after an age of ash:

*The one who walks alongside flame shall one day meet the road of Destined Death.*

A blade given to a woman setting out on a duty. The duty: to burn.

I am the kindling maiden. That is what was folded into me at the foot of the tree. The thorns you found barring the Erdtree — that shell of piercing rejection, sealed perhaps by the *other* half of my mother's divided house — no blade opens them, no prayer, no army. Only ruin's flame. Only a sacred fire fed with sacred fuel. And my mother, who fills her land with contingencies the way other queens fill theirs with statues, made a daughter who could be the fuel.

Sit with that a moment, as I sat with it in that small room with the dead official at my back. A child conceived — born — *made* — as a match is made.

I did not weep. I want that recorded. I picked up my dagger, and I asked the room the only question worth asking, and the room, being only a room, declined to answer it:

*Is a purpose a gift, or a sentence?*

Then I came back to you.

You had carved your way to the Erdtree's barred door by then — I heard what you did to the Omen King in his false throne room; the whole city heard — and you stood before the thorns like a reader at a locked library. I stepped out of the gold and called you what you had become while I was not permitting myself to notice: my friend. My traveling companion. And I laid the whole truth on the table between us at last.

The thorns will not yield. The tree must burn. The Flame of Ruin sleeps in a forge atop the mountains of the Giants, in the far cold where my mother's first war ended — I have carried her echo of it; *kill the giants, confine the flame,* she told her lord, and they did, and the Erdtree's whole age has been one long held breath above that banked fire. We will go and unbank it. It is the cardinal sin, the one act her Order was built to prevent, and I invite you to it with open eyes, because past the sin — only past it — lies anything worth calling a future.

You asked — you must have asked; you always asked — *and the kindling?* An old woman of the Fingers had told you the fire wants a particular fuel: one who sees flame from the inside. One born with the vision of fire.

Do not worry over kindling, I said. I will see to it.

Old friend. Forgive me the grammar of that sentence. It was the only lie left in me, and it was made entirely of mercy, and it did not even work — I saw your face. You had already done the arithmetic. You simply loved me enough to let me carry the sum myself.

There. I have used the word. Leave it in.

One more thing from that city, before the mountains. In the Queen's own bedchamber, high in the tree's embrace, her last echo waited for me — and this one was not proclamation but *pillow-talk*, cold and terrible: my mother speaking to her own other half, to the red-haired king who is also her, telling him he was not yet her, not yet a god. A marriage with one occupant. A house divided against itself that *is* itself.

I stood in that room a long time.

If I have a father — and I say this to you now as I could not say it then — if I have a father, he may be nothing more or less than the far side of my mother's face. Red where she is gold. I think of the brother the fire shows me, red-haired under his withered tree, and I think of my own hair, which is gold that someone let rust, and I close my open eye, and I let the question go.

Ash cannot hold questions. Only heat.

## X. Snow, and the Shape of My Will

The mountaintops are where the world stops pretending. No more gold up there — just white, and wind, and the bones of giants making hills of themselves, and cold so total it feels doctrinal.

We climbed. You on Torrent, breath steaming; I flickering at the graces like a candle refusing weather. And it was up there, with the end in sight, that the whispering reached even me.

There is a man — it wears a man — with eyes like spoiled honey, yellow and fond, who had been following our story the way flies follow a feast day. He wore a dead nobleman for clothes and courtesy for a knife, and he had found you at the Forbidden Lands' gate and before your ascent, pouring his gospel: that the world is beyond mending, that the only mercy left is to melt it all — every border, every self, every difference — into one warm undifferentiated scream. Chaos. The Frenzied Flame.

And his masterstroke, his recruiting sergeant's flourish, was *me.*

*Take the flame into yourself,* he told you, *and your maiden need not burn. Save her. Spare the poor kindling.* 

Note the grammar. *Save her* — from her own hands. As if my death were a thing being done *to* me that a sufficiently violent lover might interrupt. As if I were the distressed cargo of my own story.

So hear the truth I gave you at the snow's edge, and hear it in my voice, because I have never said anything with more of myself behind it:

Yes — my mother gave me this purpose. I no longer pretend otherwise; I was made for the fire as a letter is made for its address. *And I have read the letter, and I choose to deliver it.* Not for her. Not as her instrument, her contingency, her banked match. I have walked this land longer than any of its lords have sat their thrones. I have watched it strangle on its own deathlessness — the dead stacked unburied in the roots, the living unable to finish anything, even dying. A world where nothing ends is a world where nothing *means*. I choose the burning because I have judged the world myself and found it in need of exactly this — death let back in through the front door. Death indiscriminate: not slaughter, understand me, never slaughter — *mortality*, restored to every throne and hovel equally, the old honest floor put back under existence.

This is my will. Mine. Whatever designs my mother folded into me, the hand that signs this act is my own, and I will not have it belittled — not by a yellow-eyed evangelist, and not, my friend, by you. If you honor me, honor *this.* There are worse fates than a chosen death. I have watched most of them from horseback.

You were quiet a long while. The snow came down between us like the world discreetly looking away.

Then, near a gravepost in the blizzard, with the Forge's glow already staining the clouds, I said my other farewell. I pressed my forehead to Torrent's, my first friend, my smoke-grey witness, and I thanked him — for the roads, for his judgment, for choosing better than I knew how to. I asked him to carry you the rest of the way through everything. He breathed against my hands, which have no warmth, as if they did.

He knew. Steeds always know. It is people you have to tell twice.

## XI. Kindling

The Forge of the Giants is a bowl of banked ruin at the top of the world — a crucible in the mountain's skull, ringed by the chain-hung dead, breathing its long red breath into the cold. My mother's first war ended here. Her last one begins the same place. She always did have an architect's sense of symmetry.

I asked you if you were ready. You will notice I did not ask myself. I had been ready since a small room off a lift shaft; readiness was the only possession I had left.

And then I did the one thing in all our travels I had never done.

Every touch until then, I had *asked.* May I rest my hand upon you. Might I carry you to the roundtable. A dead woman minds her manners; borrowed hands borrow permission. But there at the rim of the Forge, at the end of everything, I reached out and I *took your hand* — took it, held it, mine — and I let myself feel that stubborn idiot pulse of yours one last time, and I thanked you. For walking with me. For the length of a world.

Then I stepped off the edge of your grasp, into the fire, and I opened the letter for the last time:

*O Flame — take my body for your wood. Let the thorn-crowned tree drink ruin. And beyond the burning, beyond the road of Destined Death, let there stand a Lord.*

I will try to tell you what it is to burn, since I am the only narrator you will ever get on the subject.

It is not pain. Pain is a message to a body, and I had only the idea of one; what burns in a spirit is the *ideas themselves*. I felt my hands go — the memory of hands — then the long habit of a face. I felt the fire find the vision of fire inside me, flame meeting its portrait, and *recognize* it, the way the sea recognizes a river. I became heat, then light, then intention. I went up the mountain wind as a bride of smoke. I crossed the sky of the Lands Between as its own dawn.

And I took the Erdtree in my arms.

Gold burns gold, in the end. The thorns that no blade could pass sighed open before me like a mother's door. The tree that had held its breath over a banked fire for an entire age finally, finally exhaled — and the whole world looked up from its little wars at once, every knight and beggar and abandoned god, to watch the sky's tree wear a crown of flame.

That was me. That light on all your faces. Whatever else is said of Melina — that she was a liar, a match, a queen, a ghost — say that once, at the end, she was *legible*. Written in fire across the entire sky, plain enough for the whole broken world to read:

*It can end. Things can end again. Begin.*

## XII. Interlude: What the Fire Sees

Do not mourn yet. Sit with me in the burning a moment, for fire is not blind — fire is nothing *but* seeing — and from inside it I saw everything at once, the way lightning must see the whole field.

I saw you wake in a crumbling city out of time, dragons wheeling through its suspended ruin, and carve your way to the Beast — my mother's own shadow, her black blade, the jailor of Death itself — and I saw Death come loose at last from his breaking body, the Rune of the end restored to the wide world like sight to the blind. The road my dagger promised. The road I burned to open.

And in the fire I saw other things. Older things. I record them here without verdict, because ash keeps heat, not conclusions.

I saw a queen with eyes like the first hour of night — dusk-colored, gloam-colored — standing at the head of an order of flayed faith, black flame in her hands, godskin at her back, cradling swaddled apostles like a mother of endings. I saw the Beast break her armies in some sealed-off century. I saw her *defeated* — the fire was precise about the word — defeated, and not killed.

And the queen turned, in the vision, and looked at me with her single dusk eye.

Was she me? Is she my grandmother in office, my predecessor, my tenant — the patient thing I have felt all my life behind my sealed left eye? Am I her daughter, her prison, her second draft? Or is she simply what the fire knows I could yet become, shown to me the way water shows a face?

Fire does not answer. Fire only shows.

I let the vision go, as I have let everything go, and I burned on, and the Lord came down the road of Death toward the throne, and in most turnings of the world, that is where my story rests — a warmth in the roots of whatever grows next, a name in the mouth of a Lord who keeps, I trust, one small fire always lit.

But you asked for all of me. So I must show you the other turning.

---

## XIII. The Other Turning

There is a world beside this one — every fire shows it, at the right angle — in which you listened to the yellow-eyed whisper.

In that world I felt it before you confessed it: a wrongness in your grace like a wasp in amber. You had gone down beneath the capital, past the deepest ministries of the dead, to the sealed door where three fingers wait — and you had let the trapped madness of the first darkness look at you, and *like* you, and take your flesh for its wick.

I begged. I want the record to show that I begged, I who ask for nothing. I told you what the Frenzied Flame is — not rebellion, not mercy, but the tantrum of something that was refused a world of its own and has decided no one else may have one. It does not end suffering; it ends *distinction* — melts every self into every other self until nothing is left that could suffer, or love, or be. And I set against it the entire case for existence, the whole of my long testimony from horseback, the only credo I own:

That life persists, though it is battered. That thought persists. That *births continue* — that even in a land this ruined, new things are still arriving, small and furious and unconsulted, and each one is a fresh argument against despair. A Lord's first duty is to life. A Lord of nothing rules nothing. Melt the world and you are not its king; you are only its stain.

You chose the wasp.

So I did the last free thing left to me. At the fire's edge I ended our accord — the strength I had strung into you, I could not take back, and so I took back the only other thing I had given: my company, my witness, my name for you. And I made you a promise in place of a farewell. That if you followed the flame all the way down — if you made yourself the Lord of Chaos — I would come for you. I owed the world that much for having built you. Even a match can light a hunting-lamp.

You burned the tree with your own borrowed body. I did not watch.

And after — after the sky itself ran mad, after the gold went out of everything — I walked out onto a stone platform above the ash of the world to keep my promise's first appointment, and I found, lying in the grey, a small charred ring.

Torrent's whistle. Burned black. It came apart in my hands like a word you say too many times, and the last of it went up as a fleck of ash on the wind, and I understood that my first friend, my smoke-colored chooser, the one soul in this story who was *only ever kind* — 

Give me a moment. Even ash. Even ash.

When I rose, something rose with me. My left eye — the sealed one, the patient one, my whole life's locked room — *opened.* And the world came into it dusk-colored, gloam-colored, the exact violet of the first hour of night. My right eye, my golden eye, has gone dim and pale as a snuffed lamp; the gold left it when the gold left everything. My hair has darkened as if the shadow of that other queen were finally reaching the ends of me.

Am I her now? Was I always? Or does Death, let loose in a masterless world, simply *pick a hand* — and mine was the nearest one open?

I no longer ask. Grief burns questions for fuel.

Hear then the last vow of Melina, kindling that would not stay lit, maiden of no fingers, daughter of a locked door — sworn on a burned whistle to the Lord of Frenzied Flame, who was my friend:

Your death was not destroyed. It was only *delayed.* I carry it with me now — I have become its road — and I am coming, old companion, across the ash of everything we made together, to give you the one gift I have left, the gift you tried to spare me, the gift that makes all other gifts mean:

An ending.

Wait for me. You were always so good at that.

## XIV. Embers — A Coda, Spoken to Whoever Keeps the Fire

You who listen: the story is told, in both its turnings. Let me sweep the hearth.

I do not know, even now, everything I was. Daughter of a Queen — almost certainly, though she never said it in my hearing and I never said it in hers; ours was a family of sealed eyes and unsent letters. Sister to a red-haired sorrow in a shadowed land — the fire insists, and I believe the fire. Heir, or vessel, or second coming of a dusk-eyed queen — perhaps; the crown fits, and that proves nothing; crowns are made to fit.

But here is what I know, and it is the only inheritance I have to leave you, so take it:

I was made for a purpose. Most of us are — by mothers, by orders, by the shape of the age we fall into. That is not the tragedy. The tragedy is only ever to *stop* there — to live and die as someone else's unread letter.

I read mine. I weighed it against everything I had witnessed — every rotted throne, every unburied army, every tailor weeping for his mother's voice, every stubborn pulse under my borrowed hand. And then I signed it myself, in fire, across the sky.

I was made for a purpose. I became a person the day I chose it.

That is the whole of my testament. The rest is warmth in the roots.

And if ever, on some dark road, a smoldering butterfly crosses your path — small, and burning, and refusing to be consumed — do an old spirit a kindness.

Do not put it out.

Let it choose.

*— Here ends the Testament of Melina, the Kindling Maiden.*
