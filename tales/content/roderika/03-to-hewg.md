## Part Three: To Hewg

### 1.
You won't hear this. That's all right. I've never once in my life said anything to anyone who could hear it, and I've got rather good at it, and I'd not know what to do with an answer if one came.

The house is burning.

I want to say that plainly, the way I said the road, so it keeps its size. I woke, or came back from wherever I go, and the fire had got out of the hearth. Not spread. Got out. It was in the hall, and up the walls, and along the beams, and through the long room with the table, and in the wing, and at the counter where the two waiting women stood in it and went on waiting. And it didn't burn anything down. That's the thing I can't make you see, since you can't see. It burns and burns and the house stays. It's a fire that's decided what the house is now, and the house has agreed.

You didn't move. I looked at you first. I always look at you first. You were at the anvil, with the hammer up, and the forge going, and the fire from the hall coming in through the arch to meet it, and you brought the hammer down as if nothing were different, and I suppose to you nothing was. It's been fire in here since before I came.

Then I listened. And I'll tell you what I heard, so you'll have it, since you were working.

The man behind the door had stopped talking to himself. He was talking to the house. Learning it, he said, once, to no one. Searing it into my memory. And then, a while later, the door, and his step across the hall, and the entrance, and gone.

The fingers in the deep room. I'd got so I could hear them the way I hear you, their dry spelling in the air, always going, all mysteries. In the fire they stopped spelling. They went a different way. Slapping. Two fingers on a stone, over and over, like something that's forgotten what fingers are for. Mad, I said to the stranger, when they came. The Two Fingers went mad. The old woman who reads them says they're only asleep, consulting something. I've listened, and I say mad, and I'm the one who hears in this house, now.

The Tarnished went. One by one. I heard the doors. I'd never heard so many go and I've never heard one come back.

The two waiting women waited.

And your chains.

I'd listened to them for so long that I could have drawn them. The drag, the settle, the old turning in them that I'd never once got a string on. And in the fire, in the first hour of it, with the hall going up and the fingers slapping and the doors, there was a sound I'd not heard before. Like a rope giving. Like a thing that's held for a very long time finding it doesn't have to.

You didn't look down. You didn't even stop the hammer. Your chains broke, Master Hewg, on the floor at your feet, and you brought the hammer down on the next blow as if they'd been noise.

### 2.
I told the stranger we were the only ones. Take a look around, I said, when they came, from the entrance, from wherever they'd been to do what they'd done. The Roundtable Hold is burned. Razed to the ground. The Two Fingers went mad, and the Tarnished have all left. Myself and Master Hewg are the only ones who remain.

That's not true, and I've got to say so somewhere. The old woman's still in the deep room, with the slapping. She's said she'll see it to the end. The two at the counter are still at the counter. I said the only ones and I meant it and I was wrong the way you're wrong when you say everyone's gone about a party and mean everyone who was yours. I don't count her and she doesn't count me. We're each the last of a different thing. I wanted you to know I'd noticed. You'd have noticed. You notice what's true even when it's not what's kind.

Don't worry, I told the stranger. I don't blame you. I'd said it before. I said it again so it'd be said in the fire.

And then I said the thing I'd been building since the rope gave. I'll continue spirit tuning just the same as before. Which is why I need your help persuading Master Hewg to leave.

I'd tried. I want that plain too. Before the stranger, I'd tried. I'd sat at your left with the fire coming in and I'd said it the way we say things at home when they matter, without the question at the end, for once, straight. I'd said, The house is going. You'll go with it. I can hear it in you, Master Hewg, the same turning that's in the walls, you're knotted into this place like a root into stone, and when the stone goes the root goes. I said, Your shackles are broken. You're a free man, now. It's high time you put the Roundtable behind you.

And you said, What use have I for freedom now?

Not angry. That's what I couldn't bear. You said it the way you'd say the weather. I smith weapons to slay a god. I have lived, and will die, doing so, upon this spot. Is there any other way? And you looked at me, and you meant it as a question, you wanted to know if I had one, and I didn't, and I sat down.

So I sent the stranger. They went in and I heard them not say anything and I heard you say the same to them, I've upset the girl, you said, she says that now that my chains are broken, I'm free, and I heard the shape of you shaking your head. And they came out and I said, Oh, is that right... So Master Hewg won't listen to you, either.

You have my thanks, regardless. I'll try and talk him round next time. I said next time. I've said it every time. I'm saying it now, to you, who can't hear.

And then I said the other thing, and I said the name, the once I'd promised myself, because it was needed.

I know he was given this great entreaty; To craft a weapon which could slay a god. Though I can't help but think of it as a curse. A fearsome curse, put on him by Queen Marika.

There. I've said it. The one on the wind, the one you pray to and fear, the one who gave you the hammer. I'd heard you say it a hundred nights and I'd never let it in my mouth. I let it in for this. Because you call it a promise, and a duty, and a wish, and I've heard all three in you, and I've heard what's under them, and what's under them is a cloak. It's a thing put on a person to make them go, and feel honoured going. I know it because I've worn one. I told the stranger, And if that's the case, I'm not sure there's anything we can do. I'm not sure. I've never been sure of anything. But I know a cloak.

### 3.
You finished it.

I want to tell you about that, because you were happy, and I've never once heard you happy and I'd like it written down somewhere by someone who was there.

The stranger had brought you something. A stone, I think, the kind you'd been waiting for, I could hear you wanting it before they'd got it out. And you worked, and the fire came in through the arch, and I sat with a bell in my lap not tuning anything, just listening to you, and the hammer changed. It got lighter. Faster. Like the singing in the hull. And then it stopped, and there was a silence with a shape to it, and you said:

Oh, finally! It's complete! I've done it! A weapon to slay a god!

I'd never heard your voice go up. It went up like a boy's.

Oh, you have my deepest gratitude, you said to them. Thanks to you, my wish is granted. Now wield it, and kill them. The demigods. And their god. Queen Marika herself wishes it so.

Her wish. There it was again, the thing I can't fit together. She wants a weapon to kill what she is, and you've made it, and you're glad. I sat at your left in the fire and I gave up. I gave up on her. I've decided she's a thing I'll never get a string on, like your chains, like the road, and I've stopped trying, and it's a relief, the way it was a relief to stop waiting for the gold. I've kept you instead. You I can hear.

Use my masterpiece to slay a god, you said, later, when the stranger came again. That is all that I have lived for. And then, and I heard it catch, And my promise to Q-queen Marika.

The catch. I want to tell you I didn't notice. I noticed. Your voice snagged on her name like wool on a nail, and it had never done that, and I felt it in my chest, where the ship is, and I thought, no.

And then you said, But do me a favor, and do look after the girl.

To them. Not to me. You've never once said a thing to me that you meant, Master Hewg, you say it all to the people who lay out their arms and let me get it through the wall, and I've stopped minding, I've decided it's the way you're made, the way a bell's made with no clapper. But I heard that. Look after the girl. In the same breath as the god and the promise, as if the three were one size. As if I were a thing on the anvil you'd not quite finished and wanted someone to see to.

I'll look after her. I've told the stranger, since. I've said, I'll look after her myself, thank you, to the fire, in your voice, to see how it felt. It felt like a coat.

### 4.
Then the stutter came for all of it.

I'll smith...as long as you like, you said. Now...lay out your...arms. With the gaps in it. Where there'd never been gaps. I heard the stranger hear it. I heard them stand there a moment too long.

I'll tell you what it's like from the floor. It's like listening to a string go. Not snap. Go. Slacken, a note at a time, the way the sea does when the tide's turning and you can't say which wave was the one that turned it. I'd listen to you in the nights, after the prayer, which came less, and I'd hear the turning in you, the old one, the one I'd never got a string on, and I'd hear it loosening, and I understood, finally, what it had been holding. Not you to the anvil. You to you. The chains had been the frame. And the frame was going the way of the house.

I can't hold on much longer, you said to them. I'm going the way of the R-roundtable.

The R. Where there'd never been an R. Where there'd been a door slamming.

It was a great honor to smith your weapons during my time here. Allow me to c-call you this, just once, before it ends. And you stopped, and I could hear you getting your mouth round it, a word you'd never used for anyone, a word you'd spat at the very idea of. My lord, you said. F-for that is what you are.

I hated them. For a moment. The stranger, who'd never said a word to you, who'd come and laid out their arms and gone and come back and gone, and had that from you, my lord, with the last whole piece of your voice, and I who'd sat at your left through all of it had the girl. I hated them for the length of a breath. Then I didn't. Because you'd meant it. You've never once said a thing you didn't mean, it's the one thing about you I'd take to the grave, and if you called them lord then they were one, to you, and I could hear that they were, and I could hear why. They do things. They walked in from a road and finished your work with you. I'd only sat. So I let it go. It's on the list, oddly. Things to be grateful for. That you had someone to say it to.

Then you didn't know me.

I'll tell it once and not again. It was the morning, if it's morning, and I'd come back from wherever I go, and I was at your left, sideways, where I'd been since you said sit there, and you turned from the anvil and you looked at me, the way you'd looked at my eyes that first time, and there was nothing in it. You said, Who are you?

Not cruel. That was the worst of it. You'd have been kinder cruel. You said it the way you'd say it to anyone, a woman on your floor in the morning that you'd no memory of, and then you said, Oh, I must be a blacksmith. Now, let's get smithing... and you turned back, and you picked up the hammer, and you didn't know why your hands knew how.

I didn't say anything. I've had practice. I sat there through the morning and the next, and each morning you turned and found me and asked, and each morning it frightened you a little, I could hear it, a strange woman where somebody used to sit. So I moved.

I sit by the fire now. The hearth, the first one, the rug, where I sat when I came. I can hear you from here. Every blow. You can't see me, and you don't turn and find anyone, and it's kinder. I've told the stranger. I said, I'll continue spirit tuning just the same as before. It doesn't need a room. It never did. It needs a bell and an ash and someone to sit, and I've got all three, and the fire's warm on my back the way it was the first night.

You asked them. The stranger. I heard it from the rug. Could you tell me what happened? Why is the Roundtable burning, in ruins? And then: Why does that girl weep for me? Have I forgotten something of dire importance?

You have. It's me. It's all right. I've got it. I'm keeping it for both of us, the way I keep the names. It's what I'm for.

### 5.
The stranger came back and you didn't know them either.

Ah, I said. So it wasn't just me this happened to... To think he'd forget who you were, as well. I don't know why that helped. It did. It was a smaller thing to be forgotten by you with someone. I knew he'd burn himself out, old Hewg.

Old Hewg. I heard myself. Not Master. The first time. I've not said it since and I've not gone back, either, I've let it stand, because you were old that morning, and you were Hewg, and the Master had gone the way of the R.

And then I said the thing I'd been deciding since I moved to the fire, and I'll say it to you now so it's been said to the one it's about.

I think we could talk him into leaving the Roundtable Hold, now.

We could. I know we could. A man who doesn't know what he's holding on to can be led out by the hand. You'd go. You'd say Who are you, and I'd say Never mind, and I'd take your hand, and you'd come, because there'd be nothing in you any more saying upon this spot. I could do it tonight. I've thought about it every night on the rug with the fire on my back and the bell in my lap.

But maybe it's better that we don't. It's only proper that we respect his choice to stay.

It was yours. That's the whole of it. You made it when you knew what it was, in your own voice, with no gaps in it, I have lived, and will die, doing so, upon this spot, and you asked me if there was another way and I hadn't one. A person who's made a choice like that, knowing, and then forgets it, hasn't unmade it. They've only put it down. And I'll not pick it up and carry it out the door because it's got lighter. I know what it is to have someone decide for you because you couldn't. My brother did it, in the long hall, with his hands shaking on the clasp. I'll not do it to you.

I'll remain with Hewg, I told the stranger. He made me who I am today. I'd like to return the kindness, in whatever small way I can.

I'll say it plainer to you, since you can't hear it and so I can. Made me who I am is what I said. What I meant was smaller and bigger both. You gave me something to do with my hands. That's all. A person with something to do with her hands has a reason to be in a room in the morning, and I'd not had one since the boat, since the point where the land was gone and you were all mine and there was no more choosing. You put a bell in my hand and said listen to that and I had a reason. You gave me that. It's not a small thing to be given. I've decided it's the largest thing I own, and I've a ship and a seed and a strip of velvet with nine men's blood on it, so I know what I'm measuring against.

### 6.
I sent them off. The stranger. I'll tell you what I said, because it was about you, and because I said her name a second time, and I want you to have heard it once from a mouth that wasn't afraid.

Please, become Elden Lord.

That's what the man behind the door said everyone was here for. A lord. A throne. I don't know what it means and I've stopped pretending I do. But you'd called them one, and I've decided that's what makes a thing true here, in this house, with the fire that doesn't burn down. Hewg was always saying that you were a Lord, to him. You were. Once. It was enough.

So slay her, with the weapons he smithed. Slay the god, Marika, who cursed us all.

Us. There it is again. I say it. I've said it about the guidance and I've said it about the Tarnished and now I've said it about her, the one on the wind, and I'll tell you what I meant, since I'd not have dared say it to your face when you had one that knew me.

She cursed you with a hammer and a promise. She cursed me with a cloak, or somebody did in her name, since it's her tree my family stood at the window looking for and her gold I couldn't see. She cursed the stranger with whatever it is that makes a person walk in from a road and do the thing, and never sit, and never say. She cursed nine men with a spider, since the spider's hers too, her blood gone wrong in a hall. I don't know that she meant any of it. I've heard you pray to her and I've heard her name catch in your throat and I think she's a thing that things happen in the name of, the way weather is. And I've stopped needing her to have meant it. A curse doesn't need meaning. It needs someone to carry it up a road. We've all done that. That's the us.

They went. The stranger. Out through the entrance where people go, with your weapon, to do whatever it is a lord does, and I said thank you to the fire, since I couldn't say it to their back, and I've put it on the list, at the top, and drawn a line under it.

### 7.
So here I am. On the rug. Sideways.

I've a glovewort in my lap. One of the last of the box under your bench, that I took when I moved, since you'd not miss it and I would. A little white bell that's never rung and is ringing. And I've wrapped it. I took the strip of velvet out from over my heart, the blood one, the one I cut on the boat, and I've wrapped the flower in it the way I wrapped the ship, and I hold the two together, the bell and the blood, and I've found it's the only way I've got to hold both. The dead who can be heard and the dead who can't. I sit with them. I've told you what tuning is. It's sitting until they're more themselves. I'll sit.

The fire's on my back. Your hammer's coming through the arch, slow, patient, the way it did the first night, when I lay on this rug and thought, there's a person the road won't take anywhere either. It won't. It won't take either of us. I've decided that's not the worst thing a road can do.

I'll tell you what I hear, Master Hewg, since you're the one I'm telling now, and since you can't tell me I'm wrong, and since I'm the one who hears in this house.

I hear the hammer. I hear the two at the counter, waiting, with the fire on the shelves. I hear the old woman in the deep room, and the slapping, which has slowed. I hear the field of the house's dead, every ash in every room, turned each its own way, the way I've learned to hear them, wolves and soldiers and champions and the edges of people who sat very straight at a table. I hear the walls. They're thin, now. The fire's made them thin. This was always a place a long way from everywhere, the man behind the door used to say so, and I think the fire's burning the far out of it, a little, every night, so that the everywhere gets closer.

And under all of it. Very far. In a place that rains.

I've told you I'd not touch this. I'll touch it once.

There's something singing. Under its breath. The way a thing sings in a hull when it's been told to give it a rest and can't.

I might be making it. I've made a great deal, sitting. I've made a lady with burned hands who brought me here and a saying about cloaks and a soldier I hope the giants had. But I'm the one who hears, in this house, and there's nobody left to tell me I'm wrong, and I've decided, the way I've decided things since the road stopped answering, that it's him. The one who sang. Under the wrappings, in the heap, in the rain, with my hood on top of him keeping the wet off, singing the same songs over, because he never learned any new ones and he never once, in all that crossing, gave it a rest.

I've a bell. I've a strip of velvet with his blood in it. I've a string to listen for, a long way off, and the rest of whatever this is to listen. And if there's a way to reach him, a real one, I'll find it, or I won't, and I'll have sat here trying either way, which is more than I did in the hut.

That's the message. It's the same one. It was always the same one. I've just found more people who can't hear it.

There.

Do you hear it, Master Hewg? There. Just there. Under the hammer.

I'll sit with it. You keep on.
