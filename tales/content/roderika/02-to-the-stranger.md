## Part Two: To the Stranger

### 1.
You'll want to know about the road, and I can't tell you.

I put my foot on it. I said the thing I'd decided to say, up toward the fog, and I took the second step before I'd finished, the way I'd planned, and then the third, and the fourth. I remember the fourth. The road went soft under it, the way sand does, and I thought, that's wrong, it's stone, and I looked down.

And then there was a fire.

Not a wrong fire. A hearth. A great stone hearth with a good fire in it, and a rug before it, and a smell of woodsmoke and old stone and, under those, something I'd learn the name of later and had no name for then. The smell of a place that's been a long time without a window. I was on my knees on the rug. My cloak was on. The ship and the seed were knocking together over my heart. And I had walked, I know I had walked, my legs had that ache in them, but I couldn't have told you one thing I'd walked past.

I've thought about it since. I've decided I was brought. I don't know by what. It's been said, here, that there's a lady who does it, a burned one, who takes a person's hand and brings them, and it's been said there's no such lady and you simply arrive, the way you arrive in a dream. I've decided it doesn't matter which. Something took a craven off a road and put her by a fire. I'll thank it if I ever meet it. I've a great deal of thanking to do and I've started keeping a list.

So I sat. Sideways, on the rug, my back to the warm. I'd got up off the floor of a hut to go and find a table and I'd found a floor. I laughed about that. The cough. Nobody heard it.

Then I listened, because it's what I do now, and I'll tell you what I heard, so you'll know the house the way I knew it before I knew any of the people in it.

There was a door, and behind the door a man who talked to himself in a voice that's used to being listened to. Long sentences with a shape to them, as if he were reading them off something. He never came out. When people went in he said house guest and remember your place and I have high hopes for you, and I could hear that all three were the same sentence in him.

There was a corridor where a man in black armour stood so still that at first I took him for the armour on its own, and then he moved, and I nearly went back up the road.

There was a room, a long one, off the hall, with a table in it, round, and chairs, and no one in the chairs. I never once heard anyone sit at it. I've thought about that. A house built around a table nobody sits at.

There was a side room where a woman on a bed asked people, very gently, if she might hold them. I heard the asking, and I heard them say yes, and I heard, under both, something being taken, and I didn't know what, and I stayed away from that room and I've never been sorry.

There were two women at a counter, side by side, who sold things and who were not alive. I'd learn the word for it later. Then I only knew the sound of something that had stopped and kept going anyway, the way a wheel does after the cart's turned over.

There was a balcony, and on it a young man in a hat who'd been hurt in a way that hadn't finished with him.

And under all of it, from a room off the great hall, iron on iron. A hammer. Slow. Patient. The sound of someone who'd done one thing so long that it had stopped being a thing he did and become a thing he was. I didn't go to it. Not for days. I sat by the fire and listened to the hammer and I thought, there's a person the road won't take anywhere either.

### 2.
Then you.

I heard the step before I saw you, the careful one, the one that wants to be sure of the ground. It came from the entrance, where people arrive who don't remember arriving, and it stopped at the edge of the hall, and I knew you the way I'd know rain. I was on my feet. I don't remember standing.

And I said the thing I should have said in the hut and never had. I said, Greetings. Nice to see you again. My name is Roderika. I should have told you sooner.

I'll tell you why I hadn't. It wasn't manners. I'd stopped being somebody who had one. A name is for answering to, and there'd been nobody to answer to since the road turned. Then you came down that road with a ship in your hand, and I stood on the road and said a message to a fog, and somewhere between that and the fire I'd become somebody who could be called. So I told you what to call me. I'd like to say it felt like a great thing. It felt like taking a coat off.

Then I told you about the house. Isn't this place impressive, though? I said. The Roundtable Hold, covert quarters of the Two Fingers, and gathering place of champions who vie to become Elden Lord. I'd had it explained. Through a door, mostly. The man with the voice explains it to everyone who goes in, and I'd sat by the fire and had it come through the wood. Two Fingers. I hadn't seen them then. I'd only heard them, from the deep room past the old woman, a dry sound, like someone spelling in the air. And I said, I never knew the guidance bestowed upon us Tarnished had such fantastic roots.

Us. You'll have noticed. I say it. I said it to you and I've said it since and I've decided I'm allowed. Nobody in this house has told me otherwise. I've stood at the same windows as the rest of them, and I've stopped waiting for the gold, and I've found that once you stop waiting for a thing you can go ahead and say you're one of the people who have it, and nobody checks. That's a small mean thing to have learned. I've learned it anyway.

Although, I said, it's all a bit much for me, in truth. I'm still looking for my own purpose. That word again. I keep reaching for it and it keeps being too large, like my brother's boots.

Then I did the thing I'd been holding since the hut, and I want to tell you about it properly, because you took it without a word and I don't know that you knew what it was.

I said, Oh, and… Please, allow me to express my thanks. For giving me that keepsake from my men. It was as if I was frozen with self-pity. That's true. That's exactly what it was. Frozen, in a shape, and you put something warm in my hand and the shape gave. I failed them at every turn, I said, because I did, and because I wanted you to have heard me say it once without the laugh. You have my gratitude. Please, take this.

And I gave you the seed.

My brother's. The gold one, from the pocket over my heart, warm, humming. I'd carried it across the sea and down a road and up whatever it was that brought me here, waiting to see the thing it wanted to be planted toward. I never saw it. I never will. But you do. You go where the threads go, I've watched you, you turn toward things I can't see the way the little one turned toward you. So I put it in your hand, and it hummed all the way into your palm and then it hummed at you instead, and I felt it stop being mine and become a thing that would grow somewhere, and I thought, there. That's where it goes. It goes with the person who can see.

You put it away. You didn't know what it was. It's all right. It knew.

### 3.
I'll tell you about him now. Master Hewg. You've talked to him more than I have, I think, in words, and you know less about him than I do, and that's not your fault, it's the way he's made. He talks to the people who lay out their arms. He talks about the people who don't. I'm the second kind. I've had to learn him the way I learn everything, from the next room.

I went to the alcove because you did. I'd listened to the hammer for days and never gone, and then you went, straight across the hall as if you knew the way, and I heard him say You're a new face. No matter, it's all the same. Lay out your arms, and I heard you lay them out, whatever that is, the clank of it, and I got up off the rug and I followed the sound, and I stood in the arch.

He's big. You know that. Big the way a boulder's big, as if he'd been there before the room was built round him, and scaled, grey-green, with the scales going small and fine round the eyes. Chains on his legs. Thick ones, old, bolted to the floor. A forge going that turns the whole alcove the colour of the inside of a mouth. An anvil worn to a shine in one place, where the hammer's come down however many thousand times. And him, bent over your sword, not looking up.

He never looked up. Not at me. You paid him, and he did something to the blade that I felt more than saw, a thing going into the metal, and he handed it back and said Back already? Can't get enough, eh, to the air, and you went, and I stayed in the arch, and he went back to the hammer as if I were a draught.

I stayed anyway. I found a place on the hall side of the arch where the floor's warm from the forge, and I sat there, sideways, and I listened to him work. Days. He never said a word to me. I'll tell you what I heard.

I heard him call you fools. All of you, every Tarnished that came, smithing for you fools, in a voice like a door that's been slammed so often it's given up on latching. And I heard him, every time, hand you back a thing that was better than the thing you'd brought. And once, when you'd stood there a moment too long looking at a little knife on his bench, I heard him say, Like the look of that whetstone knife, do you? I've no need for it. You're welcome to take it. Go ahead. Gruff. As if he'd been caught at something.

And I heard him pray.

Not to you. Not when anyone was there. At what passes for night, here, when the house goes quiet in the way a house does when it's decided it's night, he'd stop the hammer, and I'd hear the chains shift, and he'd say it very low, to the forge. Your divinity, have mercy, and grant me forgiveness. The road is yet long. A god is not easily felled. But one day, without fail, you will have your wish. So please, grant me forgiveness. And a name. A woman's. The one everyone in this house says, the queen's, the one on the wind. I won't say it yet. I'll say it once, later, when it's needed.

I'd heard fear before. I make the sound myself, about the road. But I'd never heard a man afraid of the thing he was praying to. That's a different sound. It's the sound of a man asking the fire not to burn him while he holds his hands in it because it's warm.

And I heard him say, once, to you, when you'd asked about the chains, it helps me forget. The sheer terror of her. And I sat in the warm on the hall side of the arch and I thought, her. And I thought, there's someone who's had a cloak put on them too.

So when you came to me after and I said what I said about him, I want you to know it wasn't guessing. I said, Certainly, he does appear intimidating, and holds no love for us Tarnished... But I know he's trapped here, at the Roundtable Hold... So... I can tell. He's a gentle soul, underneath it all. I can tell. I told you I could. Because he calls you fools and hands you the knife anyway. Because he prays to what frightens him. Because a man who wasn't gentle underneath wouldn't need chains, he'd just be what the chains are for.

### 4.
I heard him say it before you told me. I'd like you to know that, because I lied to you about it, a little, and I don't want to have.

You'd gone in to him, and laid out your arms, and then you'd said something, I couldn't hear what, you never say anything I can hear, and there was a pause, and then his voice, lower than the hammer. The girl you brought here… She's crestfallen, and can scarcely swing a blade, but she has a gift for spirit tuning. And then, lower still, so that I nearly didn't get it through the wall: I saw another one like her, long ago. Their eyes share the same hue.

I sat in the warm with that. Both halves of it. A gift. And another one like her. I've kept the second half the way I kept the soldier's words, without touching it. Another one. Long ago. Her eyes. I've never asked him. I've come close, in the nights, and I've stopped, because I've learned from him that a person's prayers are their own.

Then you came out and across the hall and stood over me and did the thing with your hands that you do, and I understood you the way I understand everything, and I said what I'd have said if I hadn't already heard. I said, You're telling me I possess some kind of gift? I don't believe you.

That part was true. I didn't. I sat there and I turned the word over and it was a word for other people. My brother had a gift. He could see gold in the air. My sister could sing so that the dogs came in from the yard. I could tie a bowline and sit sideways and hear things through walls, and nobody at home would have called any of that a gift, they'd have called it what they called it, which was odd.

But. If I do have this talent, I said, and goodness knows it would be my first... I suppose I should try to hone it. Shouldn't I?

Shouldn't I. Listen to me. Asking you. Asking the floor. I'd never in my life decided a thing without asking it as a question first, in case the answer was no and I could go back to sitting. You didn't answer. You never do. And I found that when nobody answers, a question turns into a decision on its own, if you leave it long enough. That's another thing I've learned here. I'm collecting them.

I'll ask Master Hewg to teach me, I said.

Master. I'd called him that before he'd agreed to be anything. It's what we say at home to anyone with a craft and hands, and it came out of me the way little'un had, from the kitchens, from before. He didn't like it. He's got used to it. I've never once said his name without it, and I've decided I never will, whatever else changes.

I went to the arch and I didn't sit down. I stood in it. He didn't look up. I said it the way we say it at home when we're asking someone for their craft, which is old and stiff and I've never used it before, because I'd never had anything to ask for. I said, Master Hewg. I beg your instruction.

The hammer stopped.

He didn't look up even then. He said, to the anvil, I told the Tarnished you've a gift. That's all I said. I'm not a teacher. I'm a smith.

I said, I know. I heard you.

That got him. Not the asking. The heard. He put the hammer down and he turned, chains and all, and he looked at me for the first time, properly, and I watched his face do the thing yours never does, I watched it decide something about me. And he said, Take that off. I can't see your eyes.

The hood. I'd had it up since the road. I'd had it up in the hut through however many rains, and by the fire, and I hadn't thought about it once, because a hood is a thing you're inside, not a thing you wear. I put my hands up and I pushed it back. And I stood in the arch with my face out and the forge on it, and he looked at my eyes for a long time. Longer than anyone ever has. Longer than my brother at the window. And then he made a sound in his throat, I don't know what it was, it wasn't a word, and he turned back to the anvil.

He said, Sit there. Don't touch anything.

I sat there. To his left, on the floor, where the warm is. I haven't moved since, except the once, and I'll tell you about that when we come to it.

I've never put the hood back up. I've found I don't need to hide a face that somebody's looked at.

### 5.
I'll tell you what it is. The tuning. You've had it done, you've brought me your ashes and paid and taken them back stronger, and you've never once asked what happens on the floor between the taking and the giving, and I don't think you'd ask if you could. So I'll tell you anyway, because it's the one thing in the world I know how to do, and a person should say what she knows.

It's not putting strength in.

He told me that first, so I'd not get it wrong. He's a smith, he puts strength into things all day, he knows what it looks like, and he said, That's not what this is. Don't go at it like a hammer. He said that whatever else he'd forgotten, and there's a great deal, he'd not forgotten that.

There are flowers. You've seen them, you bring them me, you find them in the dark places where the dead are put, white, small, on a stem, with the head hung down. Gloveworts, they're called here. He had a few, dried, in a box under the bench, kept from before, and the first thing he did was open the box and put one in my hand and say Listen to that.

I listened. I'll tell you what a glovewort is. It's a bell. A little white bell with no clapper, that's never once rung, and that's ringing all the time. You hold it and the ringing goes up your arm. It's the sound the dead make when they're near. It's the sound the little one made in the bag, only made by a flower, on purpose, the way a bell's made on purpose. I sat on the floor with it in my hand and I cried, and he let me, and then he said, Right. Now the ash.

You put the flower and the ash together. In your lap, in your hands, it doesn't matter, it's not the hands. And you listen to the ash the way I'd listened to the little one, in the next room, only now there's a bell in the room with you and the bell is on your side. And what you hear is a string. That's the nearest I can get. A string that's been strung too long, or not long enough, and wants to be somewhere it isn't. And you don't pull it. You listen for where it wants to be, and you lean, the way the little one leaned at you, and you help it get there. And when it gets there, something goes into it, out of you, the runes you've brought him, I don't understand that part, he says it's the way of it. And something comes back. Into you. That's the part nobody warned me about.

He said, It's a conversation. Only quiet. Both parties enriched. He said the one who taught him to say that had said it just so, and he'd never found better words, and neither have I.

That's tuning. It's sitting with the dead until they're more themselves, and being more yourself for having sat.

The first one I did was the little one.

You brought her back. I'd not thought you would. You came in and laid out your arms for him and then you came round to my side of the anvil and you took the bag out from inside the armour and you put it in my lap. And I said the words, Greetings. Are you here for spirit tuning?, as if I'd been saying them for years, and my hands were shaking so that I dropped the flower.

She knew me. That's the first thing. The ash went still in my lap the way it had gone still for you, and I thought, she's gone quiet for me, and I nearly couldn't go on. Then I put the bell against her and listened for the string.

A girl. I'd known that. Small, and far from home. I'd known that too. But now I could hear the shape of the home, a little, or the shape of the missing it, which is the same thing from inside. Somewhere high. Somewhere with a great deal of sky, more sky than ground. And someone in it. Someone she'd left, or been taken from, someone with the same string as hers only a note off, the way two sisters' voices are.

And she told me her name.

I won't say it. It's hers. I've kept the nine's names in my mouth like stones and I've room for one more. But I'll tell you this: it was a real name. A person's. Not a thing you'd call a jellyfish. Somebody had stood over her once and said it, and she'd answered to it, and she'd carried it into the ash the way the captain carried my ship. So I said it back to her. Very low, so he wouldn't hear. And the string went where it wanted, and she came up out of the tuning a little bigger than she'd gone in, and so did I.

I gave her back to you. Can you take this little one along with you? The same words as the hut. They can't be summoned here. But the spirits look rather fondly upon you. And you took her, and I said, before you'd gone, because I thought you should know and I couldn't think how else to say it, She's looking for somebody. Somewhere with a lot of sky. You didn't nod. You put her away. And I thought, he's got it. Or she has. Whichever you are. You'll go where the sky is, you go where things are.

### 6.
I got better.

I don't know how else to say it and I've never said it about anything. I did the little one, and then you brought me others, wolves and soldiers and a thing that was three things, and I did them, and each one I heard sooner and lost less of myself in, and one day I found I'd done one without him looking over, and he'd not looked over because he'd known he didn't need to.

I told you so, when you came. I'd worked out what to say. Good to see you again. Thank you very much! I have you to thank, don't I? For persuading Master Hewg? You had. Whatever you'd said to him, in there, in the words I can't hear, it had gone in. I can happily announce that he has taught me the noble toil of spirit tuning. Noble toil. I was pleased with that. I'm as yet unsure of what I might be able to accomplish, but if I might be any help to you all, I'd certainly like to try.

And then I said the thing I'd carried from the hut, and I want you to have heard it, because it's the reason for all of this and I only got to say it once. And if there's any chance to ease the suffering of my dear men who were grafted... Well, I certainly must try.

Roderika the spirit tuner prentice, I said. Pleased to make your acquaintance. And I did a little bow, from the floor, which is a foolish thing to do and I did it anyway.

Then, later, I don't know how much later, the house doesn't do days, I stopped being a prentice.

He said so. Not to me. To you, of course, in there, with the hammer going, so that I got it through the wall the way I get everything of his that matters. The girl has come a long way. As ever, time and technique have made her stronger. Tis good to see. And then something else, lower, that I only got the edge of, something about what an imprisoned man does and doesn't deserve, and I didn't catch the rest, and I've decided I don't want to, because I heard the shape of it, and the shape was a man putting something down very carefully so as not to break it.

So when you came round the anvil I told you what I'd found. I feel like I'm really coming to grips with spirit tuning of late. I can see how and why immortal essence exists as spirit under the Golden Order. That's not quite it. I'll say it plainer, for you, since you never say anything back. The dead here don't all go where they're meant to. The tree takes most of them, the great one, the gold one, everyone goes back into it the way water goes back into the sea. But some don't. Some stay in their ash. And I'd thought, in the hut, that it was because they were stuck, and it isn't, or it isn't only. It's because they've a yearning. Each one. A direction. A thing they were turned toward when they stopped, that they're still turned toward. The little one's sister. The wolves' hunting. The soldiers' war. That's what I hear, when I hear them. Not the dead. The turning. I can understand their yearnings, what they become drawn to.

And I'll tell you a thing I've told nobody, and you can do what you like with it. I think that's what the threads were. At home. In the east passage. I think my brother stood at the window and saw the tree's turning, gold, going west, and everyone with our blood saw it because our blood is turned that way. And I couldn't, and I've never known why, and I've stopped asking. But I can hear the dead's. It isn't the same. It isn't nothing. It's a turning, and I'm the one who hears it, and I'll take that. I've decided to take it.

Master Hewg said it himself, actually, that I'm no mere prentice any longer, I told you. Once again, I'm pleased to make your acquaintance. Roderika the certified spirit tuner.

And I stood up. I don't stand up. I stood, and I did the thing we do at home, the proper one, the one my mother made me do a thousand times in the long hall until my knees knew it without me. I curtsied to you. Skirt and all. In a burning smith's alcove with chains on the floor. It's the one thing from home that's still any use, and I've given it to you, so you can do it to whoever needs one.

I'll tell you what else changed, when I could hear.

The two women at the counter. They're dead. I'd known that the way you know a wheel's stopped turning. Now I could hear what they were. They're not unhappy. They're waiting, the way a shop waits, with the door open and the things on the shelves. Someone put them there to wait and they're doing it, and they'll do it after the shelves are gone. I say good morning to them now. It's not nothing, to be said good morning to.

The table. There've been people at it. Not now, before. I can hear the edges of them the way you can see where a picture hung. Champions, the man with the voice calls them. What I hear is a lot of men sitting very straight and being very sure, and then not being anywhere.

And him. His chains. I'd listened to them for however long, the drag and the settle, and I'd thought, iron. They're not. They sound like iron. They're something older and it's holding him the way the road held me, and there's a turning in it too, I can just get it, something about a promise, and I don't know how you tune a road. I've sat at his left and tried, in the nights, very quietly, and the string won't come. Some things aren't strung. Some things are the frame.

I'll tell you the last thing I did, in that time, and then I'll tell you about the wing.

I gave you the hood.

Not to keep. I folded it, the crimson, with the lining out, and I put it in your hands, and I said, Will you take this up there. To the courtyard. To the heap. You know the one. You've been. Put it on top. I don't mind which of them. So there's something of mine up there that's warm.

You did. I know you did. I felt it go. There's a place that rains, a long way off, and something of mine went into it and lay down, and I've been a little colder since and I don't mind.

And the little one. I'll tell you now, while I've got the nerve. One day you didn't bring her. You came in and laid out your arms and came round to me with a wolf and not with her, and I listened for her, the way I always do when you come in, and she wasn't there. Not gone the way the dead go. Gone somewhere with a great deal of sky.

I think you found the sister. I think you took her up to wherever the sky is and there was someone waiting with the same string a note off, and they went. Together. To look at whatever it is you look at from up there. I'd like to think I helped. Don't tell me if I'm wrong. You wouldn't anyway. It's the kindest thing about you, that I can decide what you'd have said.

### 7.
I need to warn you about something.

I said that to you the moment you came in, before the greeting, before anything, and I watched your face not change and I wanted to shake you, I who've never shaken anyone. Listen. A little while ago someone started lurking in the wing on the opposite side of the roundtable.

You know the wing. Past the counter, past the two waiting women, the far side of the house from the fire. Nobody goes there. There's a room at the end of it that was always shut and always quiet, quiet the way the loft in the hut was quiet, and then one day it wasn't.

And I can hear, from all the way over there... The howling and wailing of spirits in fear of a curse.

I'll tell you what it was like, since you can't hear it and you should know what's in your house. It was as if every dead thing in the walls had turned at once. Not toward a yearning. Away from something. Like a field of grass when the wind gets under it. And the something. I can even hear the repulsive, twisted malison itself. That's a word from home and I'd never had cause to use it. A malison is a curse said on purpose by someone who knows how. This one was in the room at the end of the wing, and it was making a sound like a mouth that's never once closed, and every spirit in the house could hear it and none of them could get away, because they're in their ash, and ash doesn't run. A plethora of spirits in an unceasing cacophony. I'm sorry for the words. They're the only ones that were big enough.

I can't even imagine, I said. How much suffering, inflicted to who knows how many souls. And then the thing that frightened me more than the sound. Not even the grafting caused anything like this to happen.

I'd thought the spider was the worst thing. I'd sat in a hut for however many rains being sure of it. Arms, and legs, and heads, and the heap. And here was something in the same house as me that had done a thing to the dead that made the heap sound like a kindness, and I couldn't get at the shape of it, only the size.

You should keep your distance, I said. I know you're strong, but please...

Please. I don't say please to you. I say it to the road. I said it to you and you went to the wing anyway, because you go where things are.

Then it stopped.

I woke, if it's waking, and the field of grass had settled, and the mouth was gone, and I sat very still and listened to be sure, and the house was only the house, the hammer and the counter and the man behind the door. I can't hear them any more, I told you. The voices of the spirits cowering from the curse. You'd done something. You'd gone down the wing and come back and now the room at the end of it was a room. I suppose the Dung Eater must have left the Roundtable. That's what he's called. The thing in the room. The two waiting women say it as if it were a name and I suppose it is. I just pray nothing ill comes of this. I did pray. Not to anyone. I've no one to pray to. I said it to the fire, the way he does, and I meant it.

Ill came of it.

Be on your guard, I beg you. He's back. The Dung Eater. Again. The field turned. The mouth opened. And worse than ever, the reverberations of the twisted malison itself. Worse, because I could hear it better. That's the cruelty of getting good at a thing. I'd been a prentice the first time and heard a noise. Now I was what I am and I heard what the noise was.

But after all this time, I've started to grasp it now. I can hear, in the malison, another fearsome Order.

Order. I'll say what I mean. The tree's got one. The gold, the threads, the way everything with our blood turns toward it, the way the dead go back into it. That's an Order, that's what the man behind the door means when he says the word. And the thing in the wing had one too. A whole other one. A whole other way for everything to be turned, only turned the other way, toward a mouth. And I could hear it. I could hear its grammar.
