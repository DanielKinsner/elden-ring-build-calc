## IV. The Night of the Conspiracy

You will want to know how it was done.

So did I. So do I. I have had a very long time, and I will give you what I have, which is less than you want and more than I can carry.

It was the golden age. That is what it is called now; then it was only the age, and the tree was full, and nothing had died in a thousand years that had not come back through the roots. I stood where I stood. The black blade was on my back, and the coal was in the blade, and I had held it so long that I had forgotten it was a thing that could be taken, the way you forget that a wall can fall.

They came in the way her own people came.

The rare ones. Women, with her light in the hair and her scent on them, and I took them for hers. I had seen such women all my life in her house; they came and went; they had the run of the inner rooms. These came in silver, under cloaks that fooled the eye. I have heard it said that the cloaks were what fooled me, and it is true that I could not see them clearly, but it was not the cloaks. It was what was under the cloaks, and what was under the cloaks was her.

Her seal. Her scent. Her kind, or wearing her kind.

A shadow does not lift its blade against the god's own. That is not an order. Nobody gave it to me. It is the shape that was pressed into the clay: that the thing in front of me is not to be struck, and that what belongs to the thing in front of me is not to be struck, and that when what belongs to her comes toward me in her house wearing her light, I hold. I held. I stood with the black blade on my back and the short blade in my hand and they came toward me the way her women had always come toward me and I let them come.

And while I held, something was cut.

I did not feel it go.

That is my shame, and I will say it once, plainly, so that you have it: the thing I was made to hold was cut from me while I held it, and I did not feel the cut. A fragment. A piece of the coal. Taken from the blade with a rite I did not see and put into a knife that had been twisted so it could hold it. I have seen the knives since; they turn on themselves like a root looking for water. They took the piece out into a bitter night, the coldest I have ever felt in that land of no seasons, and before the morning the golden one, the eldest, was dead.

Not dead. Understand. Nothing under the gold could die. What I felt, that night, was wrongness in the coal, a socket where there had been none; and then the mark that death carves in the world when it takes a god's child, the mark that should have been a circle, broke in half. Half a wheel. I felt it break the way you feel a bone go in a hand that is not yours. Two had died at once, somewhere, in some manner. That is all a seal feels. The rest I had from men afterward, men in the capital who had never seen my face: that his body lay where it fell at the foot of his mother's tree and did not rot and did not return, because the thing that would have carried it back through the roots was the thing they had put in the knife; that only his soul went out of him; that the other who died that night died in the flesh alone, and the soul went on. I believe them. I have no way to know. The body stayed, and the roots came into it, and I will come to what the roots did with it.

Someone stole a fragment of Death from Maliketh.

That is how it is said. I have heard it said by those same men, and it is true, and I have had a very long time to look at the first word of it. Someone. I do not know who. I have never known who. I know the women had her light. I know they had the run of her rooms. I know that the seal I would not strike was her seal.

I have never known if she sent them.

That is the stone in the gap. I have asked her. I am asking her still, in the only way I have left, which is to say her name into the dark and wait; and she has never answered, and the tree she is in does not pass messages, and I have stopped expecting one. But I want you to know that I am still asking. When you find me on the floor of this hall with the black gone out of me, I will still be asking.

Why wouldst thou gull me.

There is the question. Not why did you take it. Why did you let me stand there and hold the door while your own hand reached past me. If it was your hand. If it was your hand.
