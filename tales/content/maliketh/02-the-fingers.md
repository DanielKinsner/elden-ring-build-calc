## II. The Fingers

Two of them came down into the storm.

I do not know how. I have stopped asking. Something sent them, and it did not consult the weather. They were tall as the temple door and pale as the inside of a shell, and they did not speak. They pressed.

That is the only word I have. They pressed against what I was, and what I was gave, the way wet clay gives under a thumb, and when the pressure came away there was a shape in me that had not been there before. The shape was a following. I do not mean I was made to serve. Beasts serve; I had served the memory of the gift all my life. I mean that I was made to be *behind*. There was, after the pressing, a place in front of me where someone would stand, and the place was empty, and every part of me leaned toward the emptiness the way roots lean toward water.

I learned the words for it later, from people who had words for everything. Fingers, they are called, and they serve something I have never seen. A vassal tailored for an Empyrean. A shadow. Shadowbound, in your tongue, as though I had been tied to a shadow. No. I was the shadow. The tying went the other way.

I do not know whether I was chosen. A priest of the gift would like to think he was chosen; it would make the pressing a kind of honour. But they did not look at me before they pressed, and they did not look at me after, and I think the truth is that they needed a beast who could hold a thing without dropping it, and I was the one whose hand was nearest.

They took me out of the storm.

You cannot imagine what that is, if you were born under a sky. The storm is not weather to us. It is the wall of the world. Beyond it is the falling. To be carried out through it is to be carried out of the falling into a place where things stay where they are put, and it made me sick for a year, the stillness of the ground.

They brought me to her.

Her name was Marika. I will not say it again until I have to. She was standing under no tree, because there was no tree. That is a thing I can tell you that almost no one alive can tell you: I saw her before the tree. She was of the people from outside, the rare ones, the ones with the light in the hair. Her hair had been cut once, close to the skull, and grown back; I could see where. I do not know why I remember that. I have kept it the way I keep the stone.

She looked at me the way you look at a thing you have been told you are to keep. And then she did the thing that no one had told her to do, and that is the thing I have never been able to put down.

She named me her brother.

Not of the blood. Understand me. She had no blood in her that was mine, and I no blood in me that was hers, and if anyone tells you otherwise they are telling you in a tongue that has only one word for brother and has to make do. In her tongue there is a word for exactly what she made me. A brother by the oath. The younger one. The one who comes after and follows. She said it, and I felt the following in me turn toward her and lock, and she watched it lock, and she said the word again, gently, as if to a thing that had just been born. Little brother. Come.

And I came.

I will not tell you I loved her. I do not have the word. They pressed a following into me and she named the following brother, and whether a thing that is made to follow can also choose to, I have had the whole of time to ask and I have not found the answer, and I am not going to find it on the bridge with you. I will tell you what I know. When she walked, I was behind her. When she stopped, I stopped. When she slept, I did not, and I stood in the door of the place where she slept with the short blade of the five fingers in my hand, and nothing came through the door. That was the first work. It was not hard. It is the only work I have ever done that was not hard.

She asked me once, in those years, as I stood in the door, what my first name had been. I could not tell her. I had already lost it. She looked at me for a long moment and said: then that is a thing we have in common, and went in to sleep, and did not say what she meant, and I did not ask. I have that too. I keep it with the hair.

She gave me the second name in those years. I do not know if she gave it or the pale ones gave it and she only said it aloud. It is said the name meant the Death of the Demigods. I do not know in what tongue it is said. There were no demigods yet when I first heard it. It was a name that was said about me more than to me, and I wore it the way a beast wears a mark burned into the hide: the shape of someone else's intention, and after a while the fur grows over it and you forget.

Maliketh.

There. You have two of them. The third I will give you when we come to it. It is the one you know.
