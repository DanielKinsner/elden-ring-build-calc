## V. Within

I took the sword into myself.

I will tell you how, because no one else can, and because you will be holding the husk of it in your hand before the storm has turned twice, and you should know what you hold.

I went out of her house. Not far. To the place under the tree where the roots come up through the stone like knuckles. I laid the black blade across my knees and I looked at it for the length of a night, the coal still in it, dimmer by one piece, and I understood that a blade is a thing that can be reached. A blade is *outside*. Anything outside can be taken. I had been made to hold, and I had held a thing in my hands, and hands can be opened by anyone who knows where the fingers are.

So I set the point of it against my own breast, at the place where the pale ones had pressed the following into me, and I pushed.

It went in as it had gone into the blade. As a spirit goes into a house that has been chosen for it. There was no wound; I want that understood; I did not bleed; the steel did not part the flesh. The steel went *into* the flesh the way a word goes into a listener, and the coal went with it, and when I took my hands away there was nothing in them. Nothing. The sword was in me and Death was in the sword and I was the door and the room and the lock, all at once, with no outside left for a hand to reach into.

Never again. No one. That was the whole of the thought. A beast's thought. Not this, not again.

It was not heavy. I have said that about the blade and it was true of the blade. It was not true of this. When Death is in a blade it is a thing you hold. When it is in your body it is a thing you *are*, and the world is not built for a thing that is Death to walk in it; the world leans away. The stone under my feet leaned away. The roots drew back from me the way a hand draws back from a coal. I have never since stood on ground that wanted me.

And there was the piece.

I could feel it now. I had not felt it go, and now I could feel nothing else: the place in the coal where the fragment had been cut, a socket, a gap, and out beyond the socket, somewhere in the land, the fragment itself, alive, moving, put to use. A tooth feels cold. That is how I felt the fragment. Every time it was used, every time the twisted knives went into something, I felt the cold of it in the gap where it belonged.

What was left in my hands, after, was a husk.

The sword. It is the same steel; it will cut you; I will cut you with it. But it is the skin a thing leaves on a rock when the thing has gone on. Her tongue has the word: the shed casing, the shell a cicada leaves clinging to the bark. That is what I carry on my back now. That is what you will take from me. It is the only thing I own that is not inside me, and it is empty, and I have kept it because a priest keeps the first thing.

*...*

*I smell it.*

*Death. Feed it me.*

Do you hear that?

That is also me.

I told you on the bridge that you would hear it before I explained it. There is a temple in the east of her land, at the edge of the red country, built in the manner of my city. I am there. I am on my knees in it, now, while I speak to you here in the storm. Do not ask me how. Time here does not run; it pools; it stands in the corners like water in the dark under stone; and a thing that has been in it as long as I have can be in the storm and in the east at once, the way a sound can be in two ears. I will tell you about the temple when we come to it. For now, only know: when you hear that voice, it is not another. It is the part of me that is eating.
