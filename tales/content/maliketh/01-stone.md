## I. Stone

The first thing I remember is a stone in my hand, and that I had a hand.

That is not a small thing to remember. There was a time, and the storm remembers it even where I do not, when what I am had no hands. There were beasts. There was the city, already falling, because this city has been falling since before falling had a name. There were the dragons that made the city their roost and wore the lightning like a second skin. And then there was a gift.

The priests of my kind called it a gift and would not say from whom. Intelligence, they called it. They carved five fingers on the hilts of their short blades to remember it by, because before the gift a beast had nothing to count with, and after it we could count to five and know that five was the number of a hand. That was the whole of what we taught. We were given the hand. Everything we did with it afterward was ours, and everything we did with it afterward was also, somehow, the gift's, and no priest of mine ever settled which. I was a priest of that. A clergyman, your kind would say, if your kind had a word for a beast who keeps a memory. I carried the short blade with the five fingers on it, and I taught the young ones to throw stones, because stones were the first weapons of the beasts who gained intelligence, and a priest who forgets the first thing forgets everything after.

I had a name then. It was the first thing I was ever given and the first thing I lost, and I have looked for it since the way a tongue looks for a tooth after the tooth is gone. Not because you think it will be there. Because the gap wants to be touched.

Death, in that time, was a fire.

The birds kept it. When one of us died, the birds came, the great grey ones with the pokers in their beaks, and raked the body into the ghostflame, and the body went to cinders, and the cinders went nowhere. No tree. No gold. No returning. A beast does not think about dying. That was the last of my not thinking about it.

There was a lord in the city.

He had heads enough that a young beast could not count them, though the priests said there had been more. His god had gone. Not died. Gone. The priests said it, and the lord did not deny it, and he sat at the heart of the storm where the lightning is born and waited for it to come back. I saw him once, when the city was young enough that a young beast could still walk to the centre of it without the floor going out from under him. He did not look at me. He was looking for something a great deal larger than me, and it was not there, and he went on looking.

He is there still. I have not gone in to him. What would I say? He waits for what has gone. I wait for what approaches. Nothing between the two of us is not waiting.

That was the world. Beasts, a hand, a fire, a lord with an empty place in front of him. I want you to have it before I go on, because everything I am about to tell you was done to that world by people who did not know it was there.
