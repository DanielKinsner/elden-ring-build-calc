## III. The Seal

She became a god the way the storm becomes lightning: it was in her the whole time, and then it was out.

I was behind her when it happened, because I was always behind her, and so I will tell you that I saw it and you will believe me, and the truth is that I saw her back. I saw her shoulders. I saw the light come up through her hair from underneath, the way light comes up through water, and I saw the Ring.

I do not have a way to tell you what the Ring is. Your people have a name for it; you say it as if it were a thing you could hold. The ones who write about it say it is an Order, and it is; they say it is a law, and it is; but I saw it, and what I saw was a ring of runes, made of them the way a body is made of bones, and every rune was a way that the world was allowed to be. There was a rune for growing. There was a rune for gold. And there was one that was black, and it was the way the world was allowed to end.

She reached in and took it out.

I watched her do it. I watched her put her hand in among the runes the way you put a hand into a fire for a coal you have decided you need, and close her fingers, and draw it out, and the Ring closed over the place where it had been, and was gold all the way through. That was the Order. That is what the Order is, if you want it in one sentence from the only one who stood close enough to say: it is the Ring with its death taken out. Everything after was gold. Nothing under the gold would ever truly end. The tree came up out of the ground behind her the way a held breath comes out, and its roots went down into every grave in the land and offered every dead thing a way back, and there was no ghostflame any more, and the birds went into the dark to wait.

And she turned to me holding the coal.

She wished me to be the seal. I do not say she ordered it. I have turned that moment over so many times that the edges are gone off it, and I cannot find an order in it anywhere. She wished. She stood with the black rune in her hand and it was burning her, I could see it burning her, and she said: Brother, there must be a place for this. It cannot be in the tree. It cannot be in me.

And I opened my sword.

I had a sword by then. Not the short blade of the five fingers; that I kept, and keep. A sword for the door of a god's chamber, black, long as I am tall, and I laid it across my hands and held it out to her, and she set the coal against the steel, and it went in.

It went in the way a spirit is said to go into a house that has been chosen for it. That is her tongue's way of saying it and it is the right way. Death did not rest on the blade. It lodged. It made a house of the steel. And I will tell you the thing that no one believes: it was not heavy. It was the opposite of heavy. It was the thing that made everything else heavy, gathered into one place so that the rest of the world could float. I carried the whole weight of the world's dying so that the world would not have to, and for a long time I did not know that was what I was carrying, because it did not feel like weight. It felt like being awake.

That was the seal. Her shadow, holding the Order's shadow. A dark thing inside a dark thing, so that the gold could be gold.

Then she had children.

I will not tell you their names; you know their names; they are carved on every wall in the capital, and the capital is burning now, or will be, or has, depending on which side of the storm you are standing. I will tell you what they were to me. They were gods in halves. They had her light in them and something else, and the something else could be killed, and there was only one thing in the whole of the Order that could kill it, and I was carrying it behind their mother's chair.

There was not one of them who did not fear me.

That was the work. Understand it. I did not hunt them. I never drew the black blade against a child of hers; I never had to. I stood behind her, and I held the only thing under the gold that could end them, and they knew it, and knowing it, they were brave. They say, the ones who say things about me, that champions knew what was at stake and that is what made them champions. In her tongue it is plainer: because they knew dread, they were heroes. I was the dread. I was the reason their courage was courage and not just gold. A brave thing that cannot die is not brave; it is only loud. I made them quiet.

One of her daughters, small then, stopped at the door of her mother's hall and would not come through it. Her mother called her. She did not come. She stood in the door and looked at me, past her mother, at the black hilt over my shoulder, and looked, and looked, until she had looked her fill; and then she came in, walking straight, and did not look at me again all that day, and she was never afraid of anything after that I saw. That is what I did for them. I was the thing they looked at first, so that everything after was smaller. None of them thanked me, and I did not want thanks. I wanted them to look, and they did.

The eldest was golden.

He killed dragons. My dragons; the storm's; the ones I had grown under. He went into the old places where the ancient ones still roosted and he fought the last of them, and he made a friend of the one he could not kill, which is a thing only a boy who has never been afraid enough can do. I did not hate him for the dragons. I want that said before the rest. He was the best of them. He looked at me across the hall of his mother's house with the fear that made him golden, and he did not look away, and I gave him that, and it was the only thing I ever gave him. I want that said so that you know what was taken when it was taken.

Her lord had a beast on his back.

I will tell you this because you will not hear it anywhere else. The first lord, the one with the axe, the one who could not stop killing: he carried a lion on his shoulders, golden once, gone grey at the muzzle, old, with a face like a priest's, and the lion's whole work was to hold him down. To sit on the back of the lord's neck and keep the war in him from getting out. So there were two beasts in her house. One on the back of the lord, to keep him from killing everything. One at the heel of the god, to keep everything from dying. We never spoke, the lion and I. What would two such beasts say to each other? Yours is the one who wants to kill. Mine is the one who wants nothing to die. Between us, the house stood.

Then she sent the lord away. I was behind her when she did it. She took the light out of his eyes and out of the eyes of all his warriors, and they went out through the fog, and the lion went with them, and the house had one beast in it after that.

And there was another lord, red-haired. I heard her say to him, once, in the chamber whose door I kept, that he was yet to become her, and that they would be shattered, both; she called him her other self. I did not understand it then and I do not understand it now, and I did not have to. A seal does not understand. It holds.

She said, once, where I could hear it, that the early days of blind belief were long past.

I did not know what she meant. I was the seal. A seal does not have beliefs, blind or otherwise. I remember that she said it looking at the tree, and that the tree was very tall by then, and that her hands were closed at her sides the way they had been closed around the coal, and that I thought, for the first time in all those years, that she was standing in front of something I could not see.

I should have asked what it was.

I did not ask. I was behind her. That is where the questions are not.
