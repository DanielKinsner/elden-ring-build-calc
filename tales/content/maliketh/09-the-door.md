## IX. The Door

You are through the door.

I have to stop telling now and say the sentence, because you are here, and it is time, and the sentence is what I have.

Thou, who approacheth Destined Death.

I stop. I have never stopped in the middle of it before. I stop because you have come through the door with your hand at your side, hanging, the way it hung in my door in the east with the empty bag, not knowing what to do with itself; and I know the hand.

I know the hand.

I have watched that hand hold a root out to my mouth nine times. I have watched it not know what to do with itself. I have taken from it and not touched it and I have thanked it and I have told it that the thing it held should never be touched by a hand and I have gone for it with my claws and it has put me down with the flat of the steel and waited. It held a scale of the storm and closed on it.

My own hand goes to my chest. To the place. I do not decide that it should.

Tarnished.

Surely not.

You.

Why.

I do not say the rest. The rest is in me and the storm fills the silence where it would be. Why did you feed me. Why did you carry the roots up out of the graves for me if you were going to carry yourself up the bridge for this. Why did you wait, when I was down in my own temple with your steel in me. Why did you lower it.

It is no matter.

I say that. I hear myself say it. It is no matter. Because it is not; because the question I had for you was never that one, and you have not come to answer questions, you have come for the thing in my chest, and I have vowed, and the vow does not care who the hand belongs to.

I vow it again. I say it to you across the hall: never again shall Destined Death be stolen. By anyone. By you.

And I fight you as a priest fights.

I want you to know that this is a choice. I could go into my chest now. I do not. You fed me, and I will not open the other thing on you first; I will meet you with the gift. Stones; the first weapon; I throw them the way I taught the young ones to throw, the way I showed you in my own door, and they come apart in the air and cut. Then the claws. Then the short blade, the five fingers on the hilt, the blade the storm gives to the ones who keep the gift; and I keep the gift, still, with the whole of the world's dying in my chest, I keep it; I can still count to five. I come at you low and fast in the robe I have worn in the hall of the dead and I do not draw the other thing. The other thing is inside. I would rather kill you with what I was given first than with what I was given after.

You are good. I say that as one who made heroes.

You take the stones. You take the claws. You take the short blade across the shield and you come in under it and you put steel into me, not to stop, this time; you have not come to stop me; and I go down on one knee on the floor of the hall, and my blood is in the dust that does not settle. The gift is not enough. I knew it would not be. I wanted to have offered it.

And I do the last thing.

I put my hand into my own breast. Into the place where the pale ones pressed and the point went in and the sword has been lodged since the night after the knives. I put my hand in up to the wrist, and it does not bleed, it has never bled, and I say the prayer. It is not a prayer of my priests. It is not hers. It is mine. It is the shortest prayer there is, and I have said it once before, under the tree, when I opened the blade to the coal, and I say it now the other way.

O Death.

Once more.

Into my sword.

And it comes.

It comes up out of me the way it went in, as a spirit comes out of a house that is burning. The blade comes out with it. The black blade, the whole length of it, with the coal in the steel and the steel in my hand, and it is not a husk now; it is not a shed skin; it is the thing itself, awake, the way it was awake behind her chair when there was not one of her children who did not fear it. The robe of the hall of the dead comes off me. The iron I wore behind her, black and gold, is on me again; I do not know how; it is what I am wearing when I am this. And the hall changes. Everything the blade touches forgets how to be whole. That is what it does. It does not cut; it reminds. It reminds the thing it strikes that it was always going to end, and the thing shrinks toward its ending, and cannot come back to the size it was.

Fear me.

I say it. I say the second name. Maliketh, the Black Blade. I say it the way it was said about me, and for the first time it is said *by* me, and I hear how it sounds, and I understand at last what it means, in whatever tongue it is said in: it means the one who stands in front of the god's children and reminds them. I told you I would give you my names in the order I lost them, and I have kept to it: the first is lost, and the third is on its knees in the east and will stay there, and I am losing it now by leaving it where it is; and the second I have put back on for the length of this, the way you put on a dead man's iron to be buried in. It is the name you will hew into the tree. It was never the one I answered to.

And I fight you as that.

I will not tell you the fight. You are in it. You know what it is to be reminded; I can see you shrinking under the blade and swelling back and shrinking; and there is something in your edge that twists the time around it, so that it can touch a thing like me. The scale, I think. I gave you that. I gave you everything; I have given you three names and a stone and an eye and both my claws and the prayer and the scale, and now you have the blade too, or will. I fight you with the whole of the world's dying in my hand and you do not stop coming, because nothing has ever stopped, and because you have decided, and I made the ones who decide.

You put the steel in me the last time.

I feel the blade go out of my hand. I feel it hit the floor of the hall. I feel my knees, and then the stone, and then the dust that does not settle, settling on me at last.

I had a question ready.

I carried it up the bridge for you; I have carried it through all of this; it is the one I told you I would ask, the one question I had left. Here it is, so that you have it, since I find I am not going to say it.

Why covet Destined Death? To kill what?

What is there, under the gold, that you needed the ending of? Whose child? Whose tree? What did she build that you could not leave standing until you had a knife that could remind it? I wanted to ask. I wanted to hear you say it, so that I would know, for once, what the thing I held was *for*, in someone's hands other than hers.

I do not ask it.

Because it is you, and you fed me, and I know what you needed the ending of: you needed the ending of *this*. Of the beast in the hall with the root in its mouth. You went and pulled up every grave in her land so that I could eat, and you carried the last one to me, and then you came up the bridge with the scale I gave you in your steel so that the eating would stop. There is no question in that. There is only the hand.

What comes out of me instead is not a question.

I am sorry, Marika.

The gold will not come back.

That is what I say. Plainly, in her tongue; not forgive me, only sorry, the way you say it to someone in the next room. I did not decide to say it. It is what was in the place where the question had been.

And Death goes out of me.

Once more. The other way. It goes out through the place where my hand went in, and it does not go into a sword this time; the sword is on the floor of the hall and it is a husk again, it is a skin; it goes *out*. Into the hall. Into the storm. Out over the wall of the world and down onto her land, onto the red country and the drowned villages and the capital with the tree standing over it, and the tree is not gold any more where it touches it; it is a thing that can burn. Everything it touches is reminded. The land goes under it like grass under a shadow.

For the first time since she took the coal out of the fire, the world can end.

That is what you killed.

Not me. I am not so proud. A shadow is not a thing that can be killed; it only goes where the light goes, and the light is on the floor of this hall in a husk of black steel and you will pick it up. You killed the keeping. You killed the one room that was still whole in the house she broke. You opened the hand.

They will hew me into the tree. It will say she betrayed me; it will say that a seal was all she needed of me, as if she had not named me. Those are not my words. My words were the ones I said, and I have said them.

Take the sword. Go and burn the tree. I made the ones who decide, and I will not unmake you on the floor with my last breath by asking you what you are for. The lord behind his door is still waiting. Do not tell him. Let one of us have it.

Thou, who approached Destined Death.

Thou hast it.

And in the hall in the east, on its knees, with its mouth open, waiting for the door, the other of me says it too. It has not stopped. It will not stop; time pools. Go and look, if you want, when the tree is ash. You will find the wax on the floor and the dead with their faces to the centre and the gargoyle in the door looking at nothing, and you will not be able to say whether I left it or was never in it, and that is the right answer, and it is the only one I have ever had.

*...*

*I smell it.*

*Feed it me.*

*Farewell.*
