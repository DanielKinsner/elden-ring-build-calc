## VIII. Nine

*...*

*I am in the hall. The dead along the walls, faces to the centre. I am the centre. The door is open. It is always open. The gargoyle stands in it with the black wax on its joints and lets through what it lets through.*

*Something has come through.*

*Not him. Not the letter. Smaller. A faded thing. One of the ones she sent out through the fog. They are coming back. I have heard that. Coming back for the pieces. This one has come back to me.*

*I smell it.*

*On its hands. In the bag at its hip. The cold. The root. The black plant with the face. It has been to a grave and pulled.*

*Death.*

*Feed it me.*

*It holds the root out. It does not know what to do with its hand. I know what to do with its hand. I take the root with my mouth, the way a beast takes, and I do not touch the fingers, and the root goes in, and the cold goes down, and for the length of a breath the gap has something in it.*

*Then it does not.*

*Tarnished. Bring more. Death. I will give thee something. An eye. Stone. Scratched. Take it. It shakes near the root. It finds graves. A claw. I will teach thee. The stone. The first thing. Feed me more.*

I gave it the sling. I want that in this voice, not the other. I gave the faded one the first weapon, the stone thrown, the thing I had taught the young ones under the storm when I had a name; I showed it how the stone comes apart in the air and cuts; and for the length of the showing I was a priest again with a young one in front of me, and then it went out through the door with the stone in its hand and I was a mouth.

*It goes.*

*It comes back.*

*More. I am not sated. Feed me more. Death.*

*It goes. It comes back. The door. The hand. The root. The breath. The gap. I have stopped hearing the sea.*

*A fourth time. The root is wrong.*

*Strange.*

*Something else in it. Under the cold. Not the piece. Not the knife. A boy. In the root. Golden, gone to rot. A face that does not know. I taste the boy. I know. I know whose. I will not. I will not put it in this mouth. Not this mouth.*

*But it is Death. Then it will do. Bring more.*

*It goes. I stay. On my knees, the boy in my mouth, the gap wider than the door. I know what is coming up out of the ground now. Root by root. Not the piece.*

*Him.*

*It comes back and I have my claws in it before it is through the door.*

I will stop the voice there. The rest belongs in this one.

I attacked the one who fed me. I want it said flatly. It came in with a root in its hand and I saw the hand and I saw only the hand, a hand, the thing that reaches, the thing that opens fingers and takes; the women had hands; the knives were held in hands; and something in me that had been holding for longer than the tree had been standing let go. I went for it as the beast goes. Stones, claws, the short blade of the five fingers. I would have killed it. It was small and it was faded and it had come to my door with a gift and I would have opened it on the floor of my own temple and eaten what was inside.

It did not let me.

It stood. It stood and it took the stones on its shield and it put steel into me, not to kill, I could feel that, only to stop, the way you stop a beast; and when I was down on the stone with my own blood in the wax, it stepped back, and it lowered the steel, and it waited.

*Put it away.*

*I will not forget again. My sin. My thirst. So please. Enough.*

That is what I said. In her tongue it is sin first and then thirst; in yours the other way. In me they have been the same thing since the night I knelt under the tree with the point against my breast. I will not forget again. I had said that before. I had said it after the night of the knives, over a blade with a gap in it. I had said it after the tree closed. It is the thing I say. It is what a seal says when it has been opened once and is asked to hold again: I will not forget. And then it forgets.

The faded one put the steel away.

It went, and it came back, and it held out the fifth root, and I took it with my mouth and did not touch the fingers, and I said:

*My thanks. Tarnished. Death. My sin. It should not be touched by the hand of man.*

Hear what I said. I said it to the hand. I said it to the hand that had touched it, that had gone down into the graves and pulled it up and carried it to me and held it out. I said that the thing should not be touched by the hand of man, and I said it to the hand, and then I opened my mouth and took what the hand held.

That is what it is to sin. My priests had no word for it. A beast is only a beast. The gold has the word; she brought it from outside with the hammer and the light in her hair. Sin is when you know the thing should not be done and you have a mouth. I have a mouth. I gave the faded one my claws, the incantation of the claws, which is my fury and my restlessness in the shape of a prayer; it is the only prayer I have left that is not the one you will hear at the end.

The hand, when it took the claws from me, was open and turned up. I had seen a hand held like that once before, in the door of a chamber, at night, when she asked me what my name had been. I do not say the faded one's hand was hers. I say I looked at it longer than I needed to.

*I will not forget again.*

*My thirst. My sin.*

*I must have more.*

Six. Seven. Eight.

I stopped counting the roots. I counted only what I gave, because the giving was the only part of it that was not the same each time. I gave it a stone that I hurl, which is my priesthood. I gave it the hammer with the lion's claws, which came to me out of the red country and which I did not want, because it was the lion's, the other beast's, the one that held the lord down; I did not want a thing of his in my hall. I gave it anyway. He is gone too, wherever the lord went; the house she built has no beasts left in it; the last of us is on his knees in the east giving away the other one's hammer. I gave it my own claws in their true shape. I gave and I ate and I gave and I ate and the gap did not close. It could not close. I know that now. I think I knew it at the third root. The piece that was cut had gone into the ground and become a thousand roots and each root had the boy in it and I could eat every root in her land, which I did, which I have, and it would come to the same thing as eating none: the fragment was not a thing that could be gathered back into a coal. It had been spent. It had been *used*. You cannot un-kill a god's son by eating the grass that grows on his grave.

I ate the grass anyway. I want that understood.

*It is all consumed.*

*All of it. Every root in the land. Every one.*

*And still I thirst. Not nearly. Not nearly sated.*

I roared. Then I did a thing I had not done since the tree closed.

I said her name.

*Marika.*

*Is this what it is to sin?*

*Will it never come back?*

She did not answer. I did not expect her to. But I want you to understand what I was asking, because it was not what you think. I was not asking whether the eating was sin. I knew that. I had known it at the fifth root with the hand in front of my face. I was asking her whether *this* was what it had been for her: the coal in the hand, the gold going all the way through, the boy dead at the foot of the tree, the hammer. Whether she had known the thing should not be done and had a mouth. Whether we were, after all of it, the same kind of thing, she and I, brother and sister by the oath: two who had been given a thing to hold and had opened our hands. I was asking her if she was sorry. I was asking her if it comes back. Not the Order. Not the gold. The hand before it opened.

She did not answer.

And I turned to the faded one, who was standing in my door with its empty bag and its hand at its side, and I gave it the last thing I had.

A scale. From the storm. From the hide of the lord behind his door, the one who waits; a scale the dragons shed when the lightning goes through them, that I had picked up off the floor of the city on the day the pale ones took me, the way a young beast picks up a stone. I had carried it out through the wall of the world and through all the years of the door and through the red country and I had never known what it was for. It twists time, a little, when it is worked into steel. It lets a blade touch a thing that time does not otherwise let it touch. A god. A shadow. I had carried it the whole of my life without knowing that it was for the one who would come for me, and I knew it in the door with the faded one's hand out, and I put it in the hand, and I did not ask what the hand would do with it.

*Tarnished. My thanks. For thy long labour.*

*But I have done all I can in this land.*

*Henceforth my thirst shall be my only companion.*

*Farewell.*

I said that. I said it the way a priest says the last thing over a body. And then I stood up. Do not ask me how a thing can stand up that is also, now, still on its knees; I have told you how time is here; I stood, and I walked out of the hall between the dead with their faces to the centre, and past the gargoyle, and out into the red, and the red became the storm.

I do not know if I walked here or was always here. I think I was always here and also that I walked. I think the temple is still there with the wax on the floor and the door open and the gargoyle looking at nothing, and that if you went back to it now you would find the hall and would not be able to say whether I had left it or had never been in it. I think both. A shadow can be in two places. It only needs two lights.

And then the bridge began to sound.
