## VIII. The Body I Chose

Thou hast slept beside this body. Thou knowest, then, what the world does not: that it is cool to the touch, always, like the shaded side of stone; that it does not breathe, and that in the first nights thou wouldst wake and watch for a breath anyway, old habits of the living being what they are; and that it sleeps — oh, it sleeps — at hours no court could schedule and no war would excuse. Thou hast never once asked me to apologize for what it is. I notice everything, my dear one. I noticed that.

So let me tell thee how it was to wake in it, the first morning of my freedom, with my former address still cooling on a tower and the world about to break.

The histories, where they touch this at all, imagine transference — a soul decanted, wine to a new bottle. It was not so smooth, and I will not prettify it. What woke on my crone's shelf-shape was a will with its habits amputated. I reached for breath and found the reach itself was the relic — there was no lung to fill and no need of one; only the *gesture* of breathing, printed on me by a body I no longer kept. I reached for hunger: gone. For the little tidal weathers of the blood, the flutters and heats by which flesh keeps a woman informed of her own existence: gone, all gone, and in their place a stillness so entire that for one long unpriced moment I mistook it for annihilation and thought: *so the rite failed, and this is the far side of failure.*

Then I moved a finger. Painted, jointed, cold. It obeyed.

And the joy that took me then, thou must hear it named, because it is the most scandalous sentence in this testament: I have known no pleasure in this life — not my mother's gardens, not the Night's grim success, not, forgive me, even thee — that has matched the first knuckle-creak of that little painted hand obeying me *and nothing else*. No grace routed through it. No fate written on it. No Order holding its lease. A hand that answered to one law in all creation, and the law was Ranni.

My crone had asked: what is the least a body must be, for a will to keep house in it?

Answer, my teacher, from inside the answer: less than they fear. So much less than they built their whole gold heaven on.

Now the inventory of my new estate, since a sovereign should audit what she reigns over.

Item: four arms. Two great, two small, and the ballads have spent more wonder on them than on my treason, which tells thee what ballads are for. I will disappoint thee: I do not know why she carved four. I have theories the way a shelf has dust, and I have never liked any of them well enough to state. What I will say is that I grew into them as one grows into an inheritance — awkwardly, then gratefully. A woman with four hands can hold a question and its answer apart. I have found uses.

Item: a second face. Yes — say it, thou hast traced it by lamplight with thy breath held, as though it might wake. There rides above this painted countenance another: vast, faint, spectral, one eye closed, cowled in the dark the way mine was cowled in the woods. Is it her? Is it me — some cast shadow of the woman on the tower, refusing to file itself under the discarded? Is it neither, a smith's blessing, a rune's residue, the doll's own dream of being larger? I told thee in her movement: the paint is all either of us was given. I live behind two faces and can vouch for the tenancy of one. The door stays shut because I have never found its handle, love. Not every mystery in this account is my manufacturing. Some of it I merely wear.

Item: one eye of this face, closed. It was carved so. I have left it so. A woman who has seen what I have seen owes the dark at least one window with the shutter drawn.

Item — and here the audit turns, as audits do, to liabilities.

The sleep. My crone's shape keeps its own hours, and I learned early that this was no flaw of craft but the bill of the miracle itself: a will keeping house without flesh does not rest as flesh rests, in nightly installments; it *founders*, at intervals, wholly — dragged under to some cold shelf of itself for days, seasons, once for what I am told approached a decade. Thou hast seen the small deaths of my sleeping. In the years I am telling of, they were terror. To be the age's most hunted treason and to know that at any hour the tide might take thee, schedule-less, defenseless, thy mirrored smith and thy sworn wolf standing guard over a shelf-doll that will not wake for burning—

This body is not without its hindrances. I have said that to thee before, in another tower, in a smaller voice. It was the largest understatement I ever fit through a doll's mouth.

Item: the size. I will not dwell; thou hast carried me in a pack and the indignity has a movement of its own coming. But mark it in the audit: I traded a demigod's stature for a shelf-child's, and there are doors in this world I now need a stranger's kindness to open. The heavens make nothing free. I bought sovereignty with scale. On the whole — I say this evenly, having had an age to check the arithmetic — on the whole, sovereignty is the better currency. But do not let any teller sing thee the doll as pure triumph. Freedom that is painless is freedom that has not yet been used.

And one item more, which no audit of mine may skip, though it is the strangest entry: somewhere in the world's ledgers I ceased, that morning, to be collateral.

Thou knowest what the shard-bearing children of Marika became, when the Ring broke and each ran home with a piece of the law in their fists. Great Runes: the age's whole inheritance, cut up like a carcass at a hunt. One such fell, by every right of blood, to me. And I — the record uses a gentle verb, and for once I will keep it — I *cast it aside*. Let the scholars dispute what it was and where it lies; I have heard the theories, and I find them touching, the way one finds children's maps of the sea touching. What I owned of that inheritance I dropped where I dropped the flesh, and for the same reason. A Great Rune is a title-deed, my dear one. And a title-deed binds two ways: what thou ownest by law, the law owns a road into. They mistook my flesh for a leash. I would not hand them my sovereignty back on parchment.

Let the fragment of their broken law lie wherever discarded things lie. I kept no piece of the machine. That was the whole doctrine, entire: keep no piece of the machine.

There were, before the provisioning was done, the small comedies of the learning, and because this testament has promised thee the unsung verses, here are two.

I had to learn to walk. Set aside thy image of the witch descending stairs like cold silk; the first hour of my second life was spent discovering that a century of flesh-habits does not translate to joints of another gauge, and the discovering was done at floor level, repeatedly, in the dignified silence of a woman refusing to be observed failing by her own furniture. My balance assumed a height I no longer kept; my stride assumed a weight that was gone; I reached for a shelf my arms now ended well short of, and stood there, arm raised, recalculating my entire relationship with architecture. Sovereignty, love, is won in such hours as much as on any Night: alone, small, ridiculous, and electing — this is the whole discipline — electing to find the ridiculousness *funny* rather than fatal. I laughed in that tower, by myself, more than I had laughed in the last forty years of my first body. No chronicle will credit it. Chronicles never credit the laughing.

And Iji, when first he saw me — I had prepared statements, love; contingencies; a whole protocol of queenly precedent for the unveiling of a transmigrated liege — Iji looked down at the doll in his doorway for one long bedrock moment, and then knelt, as he had always knelt, precisely as though nothing whatever had changed, and asked whether my ladyship would take the morning report. The morning report. I could have knighted him for it. It was the first hour I understood that the household would hold — that what they served had never been the height of me.

So provisioned — four hands, two faces, one drawn shutter, a tide of sleep, and no deed of title anywhere upon me — I stood at my tower's window that first season and watched the age end.

It ended the way ice comes off a spring roof: one crack, then all at once, then slower than anyone believes, for years. The Ring was broken. The mother of the law had — I gave thee this in sequence and keep it in sequence — struck it herself and vanished from the telling. Her children, my kin among them, fell upon the pieces and then upon one another. Gold made war on gold. My mother's lake-country filled with soldiers; the Academy sealed its own doors with the raya of its lucaria turned inward like a snail's horns; my red brother rode to a wasteland fame that I will spend a festival's movement on; my serpent brother went into his mountain and became a thing I decline to describe at table. The Shattering, the historians call it, as though a single word could hold a world discovering that its law had been optional all along.

And through every year of it, no herald came up my stair. No Finger crooked itself in my direction. No fate-reader found my line of light — there was no line to find; the woman was discarded and the doll was deedless. The wars raged around the Three Sisters like weather around a held breath, and mark it: not one power of the age thought the little towers worth the taking.

I had built the one shelter the age could not read: irrelevance. From inside it, patient as a debt, I watched the machine that had priced my whole existence claw itself apart over the pieces of its own broken title.

The heavens had lost my address.

They would not receive it again, my dear one, until I chose the hour of the delivery — and I would spend one wolf, one smith, one buried city, and thee, before that hour struck. The next movement begins the spending.
