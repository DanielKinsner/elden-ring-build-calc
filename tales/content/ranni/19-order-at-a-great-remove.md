## XIX. An Order at a Great Remove

Before the throne, before the summons, thou camest back.

I mark it again because it governs everything after: with the beast of gold still unfought and a world's crown within reach of thy two hands, thou tookest a detour — back to a chamber in a tower in a juniper garden, to a doll thou hadst carried, to ask her not *how do I win* but *what is it I would be winning for thee*. What is this Order of thine, thou askedst — or thy silence asked; by then we conversed mostly in my answering of what thou wast too courteous to say.

No counselor, no priest, no power of the age thought to ask me that question. They asked what I had done, and what I wanted, and what I would destroy. Thou askedst what I would *build*. So I told thee — the only soul I have ever told — and because the telling that day was compressed, doll-small, spoken half into sleep, I will give it here at testament-scale: the whole of my law, and the doubt inside it, and the one instruction I need thee to carry longer than love lasts.

Upon the order I envision, then. Attend.

Mine will be an order not of gold, but the stars and moon of the chill night.

Begin where the difference begins: with where a law *lives*. The gold lived here. That was its whole ingenuity and its whole atrocity — an Order resident in the world's own flesh. Its law was a tree you could touch; grace ran in the blood; fate was written on the body; the divine had an address, and the address was *you*. And because law and life were bound so tightly — one weave, no seam — there was no gesture of living that was not also, at once, an act of law. To eat was doctrine. To die was jurisprudence. To be born marked was to be conscripted at the first breath. I have told thee eighteen movements of what that architecture does: it makes a girl's flesh a leash. It makes a wolf's love a mechanism. It makes death itself a locked cupboard with a warden. A resident law, my dear one, is a law that never stops touching thee. Whatever its kindness on any given morning, its *hand never lifts*.

Now hear my law, and hear it in the grammar I actually swore, for I have learned — we will come to it — how the words are already being mishandled.

I would keep the order far from the earth beneath our feet. Not abolish it. Mark that first, against the wilder tellings: I am no annihilator; a world without law is merely the frenzy by another road, and I have seen what waits down that road, and I sealed my own eye rather than look at it long. There must be law; life and souls and order are bound together — that much of the gold's insight was true. My quarrel was never with the *existence* of the weave. It was with the tightness. So: an order removed. Law, lifted out of the flesh of the world — out of the blood, out of the bodies, out of the touchable — and set at the distance of the stars and moon of the chill night. Present, as the moon is present: real, exact, governing the tides of what may be. And far, as the moon is far: past grasping, past bargaining, past the reach of any hand — mine included; write *mine included* twice — that would close it round a throat.

An order thou canst live under but never be handled by. Law as light arrives: enough to steer by. Never enough to hold.

And the certainties — sight, emotion, faith, touch. Here I must slow down, for here is where my own words have been carried crooked, and a testament that exists to correct records may begin with its author's.

When I said that the certainties of sight, emotion, faith, and touch should become impossibilities, there are two ways to hear it, and I have heard the wrong one walking abroad wearing my name. The wrong one: that I would strike the senses from the living — unmake feeling, blind the eye, hollow the heart, an age of numb souls under a numb sky. Love, look at me. I am a woman who crossed a world's ending for the feel of one pulse through a painted hand. The certainties I sentenced were never *yours*. They were certainties *about the law*. Under the gold, the divine could be seen — there stood its tree, gold to the horizon. Felt — grace in the veins, warm as complicity. Believed with the ease of breathing, for what effort is faith when the god's hand is visibly on thy shoulder? Touched — rings, runes, fingers, thrones. And that seen, felt, certain, touchable god-law is precisely what I unmade. In my night, no one will *see* the order and know it for the order. No one will feel its hand and be sure whose hand it was. Faith will cost again — will be a walking-out under a dark sky toward lights that do not descend, as my mother's people walked before ever a golden tree stood. And no hand — no priest's, no tyrant's, no witch's — will touch the law and turn it, for the law will be where the stars are, and the stars do not come down for anyone. I am the proof of what it costs when they do.

That is the whole of the sentence I swore. Ages will shorten it, as ages shorten every oath until the part most useful to them is all that remains. Thou heardst what I meant before the voyage began: not the murder of sight, feeling, faith, or touch, but the removal of the hand that would rule them. Remember the distinction when my words no longer can.

And that thou mayest never wonder in some far century what thy wife's law left to the living, let me inventory what remains below, under my remove — for the mistellings will say I took things, and the record should hold what I refused to take.

Grief stays. I would not touch it if I could; grief is love's bookkeeping, and I have built this whole testament out of mine. Joy stays — the leaping-at-snow kind and every other; no clause of my night reaches it. Faith stays, though its furniture changes: what leaves is the *certainty* that made faith cheap, the god-hand on the shoulder; what remains is the older, harder, better thing — the walking-out under an unanswering sky that my mother's people practiced before gold was ever law. Birth and death stay, both, at last, in their honest order, neither locked in a cupboard nor loosed as a weapon. Kingdoms stay, if the living want kingdoms; guilds and gardens and academies; wars too, I fear — I do not pretend my distance pacifies anyone; I only promise the wars will be the living's own, fought over the living's own quarrels, with no heaven putting its thumb on either scale. The whole warm terrible business of being finite stays exactly where it has always been: in the world's own hands.

What leaves is one thing, and one only. The management. The moon does not manage the tide, whatever the tide believes. It moves it, and lets it be sea.

Which is why I would abandon this soil, with mine order.

The going matters; it is not flourish. A law kept at a distance requires a lawkeeper who keeps the distance — first of all from herself. Had I stayed — throned in some tower, moon-marked and adored, the visible witch of the invisible law — then within three generations I *am* the address again: petitioned, touched, bargained with, a golden order in silver paint. The remove must include the remover. So: abandonment. The soil, the towers, the graves I love, the mother in her library — left, with the law, for the far cold. I wrote it on a ring before I had a bearer: the solitude beyond the night. I costed the voyage for one.

And now the doubt. I promised it thee whole, and here it is — the question I turn in the dark of this ship while thou sleepest, the flaw-hunt I run on my own architecture, as I once ran it on the gold's.

*Is the remove itself a leash?* An order past touching is also an order past appeal. A law no hand can turn is also a law no wronged soul can petition. I have built a sky that cannot be corrupted, and the price of incorruptible is *unanswerable* — and I know a woman who once stood before an unanswerable power that trembled and repeated its three assurances, and I know what she did about it. What if my night breeds her? Some cold clear child, three ages hence, who looks up at my distant law and finds it a locked door, and forges her own knife? I have sat with that girl in my mind's dark. I know her face. It is not unlike mine.

Here is the answer I have, and it is not a proof, it is a wager. The gold's crime was never that it could be appealed to. It was that it *answered* — reached down, touched, chose, leashed. My law will not answer, and so it will never leash; the girl with the knife will find no fingers in her cradle, no shadow at her heel, no rune in her flesh. She will find only distance — and distance, my dear one, is the one tyranny that cannot hold what walks away from it. If she rejects my night, she need only build her own lamp and live under that. I will not descend to stop her. *That* is the whole difference, and I stake the age on it: an order that cannot come down is an order that cannot come *for* thee.

And if I am wrong — if remove rots into neglect, if the far law fails some need I could not foresee from the near dark of one small history — then hear the last provision of this movement, spoken while the witness is my own Lord: my order is not exempt from my method. What I did to the gold may be done to the night. I do not intend to be unanswerable *and* unaccountable, love. I intend to be far away, and correctable, and I have deliberately married the one soul in creation with a proven record of walking past my warnings.

Thou art not only my consort. Thou art my contingency. Thou hast been the Blasphemous Claw of better hands than Rykard's since the hour thou didst nod to a crone on a wall — the counter-key I leave in the world against my own worst outcome. No order I build gets to be a cage, love. Not even mine. *Especially* not mine.

That is the Age of Stars, said whole, once, for the record that matters.

The rest is the going. Come — the last movement is the two of us. I have kept it until every other account was paid.
