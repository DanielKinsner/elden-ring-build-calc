## II. The Shadow Given to Me

Blaidd.

I have arranged this entire telling — its order, its pauses, the architecture of what comes before what — so that I might say that name to thee once, plainly, before the history gets its hands on it. Blaidd. There. In every account the world keeps, he enters as mine: my shadow, my sword, my half-wolf. Let him enter this one as he entered my life, which is to say, as himself, and let me stand aside a moment while he does it.

He came to Caria when we were both young — he perhaps younger than I, though who can age a wolf that walks like a man. I will tell thee honestly what I was told at the time: that he was a foundling, a marvel, a gift for the young princess. A child with a beast's head and a knight's heart, to be companion to a girl the heavens had begun to circle. And I will tell thee with equal honesty what I understood only long after, when Iji at last taught me the words for it: that in the grammar of the Fingers, *gift* is a term of accounting. An Empyrean is issued a shadow the way a prisoner is issued a window — something given, that the given thing may be watched through.

He was given to me as a brother. And they kept the receipt.

But hear me carefully, for this is the part the tragedians flatten. The receipt was real, and so was the brother. Both things were true the whole length of his life, and neither ever cancelled the other, and if thou canst not hold both at once then put this testament down, for the whole of it is made of such holdings.

He was four days in my house before I forgot to watch him back. I had been warned, of course — a wolf, they whispered, a beast in the nursery — and I was a careful child, appraising as a habit of survival. Four days. Then I came upon him in the west court in the first snow of the year, and the great grim marvel of the Carian court was leaping — there is no statelier word and he deserves none — leaping at the falling flakes, snapping at them, delighted by each one precisely as though it were the first. He saw me watching. Any courtier would have composed himself. Blaidd grinned — that terrible wonderful grin, all honesty and teeth — and said the snow was getting away, and would I help.

I was a princess of the moon's own house. I helped.

Thou hast met him, so thou knowest what he became: a warrior to empty a field, courteous as old iron, with that low voice that made strangers stand straighter. But I knew the creature under the accolades. He carried me on his shoulders past my mother's knights, and the knights pretended not to see, because his dignity was the kind that spreads to bystanders. He was clever — cleverer than the tale allows a loyal beast to be; he could take a scheme apart with three questions, and the third was always *who pays?* — he had, thou seest, a better conscience than his mistress, and earlier. And he laughed. At nothing. At snow. At me, when I was grand, which in those years was often. There are people whose laughter is a door out of whatever room thou art trapped in. I have known exactly one.

I should give thee, while we are in the good years, the household triangle that made them good — for Blaidd did not grow up against me alone, and the record of the faithful should show them faithful to *each other* before any of it was tragic. Iji was there first: the manor's smith since my mother's rise, older than the royalty he served, slow as bedrock and about as movable. And the wolf-pup marvel and the mountain of a smith took one long look at each other, in the year of Blaidd's arriving, and struck up the sort of friendship that needs no maintenance and survives no explanation — the enormous and the quick, the cautious and the leaping. Blaidd haunted that forge. He fetched and hauled and ruined his hearing happily at the anvil's side; Iji forged him, over the years, a training sword, then a true sword, then the great royal blade this testament has already knelt with; and when Blaidd earned some scrape too stupid to bring to the household's notice, it was Iji who mended and Iji who kept the counsel. A hundred years of shared bread and mended steel, I called it once, and will again, movements hence, where it will cost thee more to hear.

I tell thee this now for a reason of craft, love. When this account arrives at what those two did to each other at the end — the cage, the apology owed and the apology carried — thou wilt be tempted, as every hearer is, to read them as jailer and prisoner. Read them as this movement shows them first: two friends at one forge, neither of whom would have believed, under any sky then imaginable, that the day could come. The tragedy is not that they were enemies. The tragedy is that they never were.

In time he swore to me. Not the nursery loyalty of children — a knight's oath, made when we were grown enough to mean words. He knelt in the Carian manner with the great sword laid across his palms, and he swore to serve no master but Ranni. Mark the phrasing, for he chose it: no master *but* Ranni. It was not devotion's flourish. It was a jurisdictional claim. There existed, somewhere above us both, an authority that considered him already spoken for — a fate he had been born to, fitted to, *made* for — and my wolf looked up past all of it and named the one sovereignty he would accept, in defiance of the rest. The old smiths say that at the moment the oath left him, a cold magic entered the blade unbidden and never after left it. I was there. They say true. I felt the frost come off my own heart's country and take up residence in his steel, and I did not summon it, and I could not have stopped it.

My house seals what it loves. I have never decided whether that is a blessing we bestow or a habit we cannot help.

Now the riddle. I promised thee locked doors would be pointed at, so here is one, small and black and shaped like a wolf's head.

There exists a mask. Thou hast perhaps held it — it lay near the towers, where such things wash up. A black wolf's visage, finely made, and the accounts that travel with it say this: that it is the relic of an assassin, one who assumed the guise of Ranni the Witch's loyal shadow. The likeness, the accounts note, is striking.

An assassin in the guise of my shadow. Sit with the sentence as I have sat with it. Does it mean some killer once wore my wolf's face as a costume, and walked where he walked, and did work that was debited to his name? Or does it mean — say it slowly — that the guise came first? That what was sent to a moon-marked child in Caria was an *assassin*, fitted with a brother's shape the way a blade is fitted with a grip, and that everything I have just told thee — the snow, the shoulders, the laughter — was the long unplanned overgrowing of a weapon by a soul?

I do not know. Hear it plainly: I, who have made a career of knowing, do not know, and both readings have kept me from sleep in a body that scarcely sleeps. But I will tell thee which reading I fear, and in fearing, credit. The heavens do not send toys to dangerous children. They send instruments and call them gifts. If my brother-wolf began as a knife pointed at my future disobedience — then his oath, his defiance-of-born-fate, his *no master but Ranni*, was not loyalty overthrowing nothing. It was a made thing turning in the maker's hand to choose its own edge. And that would make him not the tale's faithful dog, but the first free creature I ever met — freer than I, and earlier, for he chose his chain while I was still pretending mine was jewelry.

Much later — years and a rebellion later — Iji would sit me down and teach me the doctrine entire, in the flat voice he saved for load-bearing truths. That such beasts are shadowbound: bound to an Empyrean the way the cast shadow is bound to the caster. That the binding is not affection but function; the shadow is the Fingers' surety upon their candidate. And that there is a mechanism in it, sunk deeper than the soul it wears — a mechanism I will not name yet, because in the year I am telling thee of, no one had named it to me, and I have sworn to give thee this history in the order I paid for it. Let it stand for now as I first heard it: *given, that the given thing may be watched through.*

What I would learn of the mechanism's other work — what it does, what it is *for*, when the watched one slips the watch — that belongs to a later movement, and to a colder page of the bill.

Here is what I want kept, whatever else is lost of this movement.

There was a girl who had been regarded by a dark moon, in a house already half heresy, with a future being drafted for her in a language of light she had not consented to learn. By every design of the powers that arranged her life, she should have grown up guarded, handled, and alone — sovereignty's usual childhood. Instead, for reasons that appear in no design, she had a brother who leapt at snow.

They gave him to me to be my leash, if my fear reads the mask aright. I am no longer able to regret the gift, and I have tried, for regretting it would be tidier. He is the argument I cannot close: that a thing made for a purpose may love past it; that the instrument may outgrow the intent; that what the heavens send to bind thee may become the very reason thou knowest binding is not love.

I was a child with a wolf and a dark moon. The heavens took notes.

They were, as usual, taking the wrong ones.
