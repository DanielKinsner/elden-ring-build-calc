## XVIII. The Faithful Who Paid

I promised thee this movement in the prologue. I have promised it to myself far longer. Every telling has one chapter that is the reason the teller kept putting off the telling; thou hast been married to this one for years, and watched me not write it, and said nothing, and kept the lamp filled.

Here is the bill, then. I will read it as bills are read: aloud, in order, unrounded. No arch questions in this movement. No craft. I have spent seventeen movements earning the right to be plain, and plainness is what the dead are owed.

Iji first — not because he fell first; the order of the dying ran otherwise, and this bill will keep even that straight. He outlived the wolf. He heard of Blaidd's end from thy own mouth, made his last accounting, and only then did his forge go dark. But a reckoning is not a chronology, and I will read him first regardless, for he was the household's first stone and I will not let him be its afterthought. I was gone by then; understand what that means and what it cost. After the cathedral I went where I told thee: to the night sky, ahead, to make ready. My household I left as I had always left it — standing, faithful, at their posts. There was no farewell tour. I did not know it was the last time. One never does; that is what last times are.

They found him at his forge. Thou foundest him — say it true; thou art the *they* of half my sorrows, the finder of my dead. At his anvil, where he had stood a hundred years being braver than anyone every single day. Around him, on the ground his customers had haggled on, lay dead the silver-armored sisterhood — black knives, the very craft of my own Night, come for my household while its lady sailed ahead making a sky ready. And on him, burning yet, a black flame.

He had not run. There was nowhere he would have counted as away.

Who sent them, whose flame, whether he broke their sortie and died of the winning or was simply overwhelmed at the last — I do not know. I was not there. Write it three times into the record, for it is the whole of my account of his death: I was not there. I was not there. The woman he spent a century of terror serving was, at the hour the bill came due, precisely where her design required her to be, which was elsewhere, which was always, for every one of them, elsewhere.

His last words I have only as thou broughtest them: that his purpose was nearing its end. That I was in thy hands now. That he prayed thee serve me well, unto the very end. A war counselor to the last syllable: the situation assessed, the command transferred, the flank covered. He used his dying to *staff my future*. I have sat with that sentence on nights when the voyage is at its darkest, and I will tell thee what I always come to. Not guilt — guilt is cheap, and he would wave it off with one great grey hand. What I come to is the mirror-helm. He was terrified, and he stayed. A hundred years, terrified, staying. And at the end the fear was even correct. Everything the mirror dreaded came, exactly as dreaded, and found him at his post.

The age remembers a merchant with a strange helmet. This account remembers the bravest soul in the treason of the epoch. I have said it twice now. When we raise our own halls at the far end of this night, his name goes over the first forge.

Now the wolf. Give me a moment, love.

Thou knowest the mechanism by now; I taught it to thee across three movements the way Iji taught it to me: slowly, with the flat voice for load-bearing truths. A shadow is given to an Empyrean. The gift is a surety. And in the gift there is sunk, deeper than the soul it wears, a purpose that is not the wearer's — and when the Empyrean strays, the purpose wakes. The doctrine has a plain word for what the waking does to the faithful thing it happens inside of. The doctrine says the shadow goes mad.

The doctrine does not say — because doctrines are written by the watchers, never the watched — what the madness *is*. So thy wife will write it, here, and the Fingers' own archive may sue me under whatever law survives them. The madness is not the purpose winning. The madness is the war. Two absolute things, wearing one body: the made purpose, which says *thy lady has strayed; recall her; thou wast built for this hour* — and the sworn love, which says *no master but Ranni*, and will not yield, and cannot kill the purpose, for the purpose is the marrow of the frame it was sunk in. A lesser fidelity would have broken clean and come for me, and been the Fingers' good hound, and died at thy hand with an easy epitaph. Blaidd's fidelity did not break. It held. It held against the marrow of his own making, and the holding is what tore him, and that — write it in the record in a plain hand — that is not madness. That is a soul refusing its design at a cost no design can price. I know exactly one other creature in this age who refused her design.

She, at least, got to choose the body it happened in.

Iji saw it coming before any of us. That is the misjudgment he begged pardon for — not blindness; foresight, wrongly spent. He watched my wolf after the Festival, after the blade came up out of Nokron in another's hands, and he read the signs a war counselor reads, and he did the arithmetic of a mind that had kept a treason safe for a century: the shadow will turn; the shadow is the mightiest blade in the household; the shadow must be contained. For Lady Ranni's sake — his own words, and they were true, and they were the exact truth that damned the deed. He lured his friend — they were friends; a hundred years of shared bread and mended steel; do not let the tragedy eat the friendship out of the record — he lured his friend to an evergaol and sealed him in it. Old Iji trapped me here, my wolf told thee through the cell's cold light, bewildered as a child locked out of his own house. Told me I'd bring nought but bale to Lady Ranni.

Bale. He could not even say the word without asking it.

And thou — merciful past all strategy, thou — thou letst him out. And he thanked thee, and said he did not know what had come over old Iji, and went — where does a shadow go, love, when the war inside it is being lost? Home. He went home. To the foot of my tower, where I was not; where I would never, in the design's whole remaining length, be again; and there the purpose and the love closed for the last time, and there was no lady left on the plateau to be recalled or defended, and so he stood in the juniper garden howling at an empty rise — guarding, attacking, guarding, the two verbs by then indistinguishable — until thou camest back.

He drew on thee. Thou hadst freed him; he drew on thee at my door, and what thou sawest in him thou hast told me once and I did not ask twice: nothing of the Festival's grinning comrade. A creature at war with its own marrow, and losing, and knowing.

Thou didst what was left to do. I have never thanked thee for it, because there is no grammar for that thanks; I thank thee now, in the only sentence I have found that holds: thou wast the last thing he saw, and thou wast not the Fingers' and not mine — thou wast the one soul in his whole sworn world who had only ever been kind to him for nothing. If it had to be a blade — and by then it had to be a blade — the design owed him at least that hand on it.

His last words were a question. Thou gavest them to me whole, because I asked for them whole: *Why. Why am I bale to Ranni.*

I am going to answer him now. On the record. Late — everything I have ever paid, I have paid late — but not softened.

Thou wast never bale to me, Blaidd. Bale was done *through* thee, and to thee, by powers that called thee a gift when they meant a hold — and before I set my rebellion in motion, I knew enough. There is the sentence, love; seventeen movements, and here it is, plain. Not the whole of the Night — that door remains shut. This door: I understood what refusal might wake in his design. Iji's doctrine was no secret to me by then; I built my whole rebellion in a house that contained my leash's other end, wearing my brother's face, and I told myself the thing commanders tell themselves — that love would be stronger than making; that his oath would hold; that what I could not bear to test I was permitted to hope. I did not let myself learn what my freedom would cost the one creature who loved me before I was worth loving. He paid for my liberty with the deed to his own mind, and he paid it still swearing — thou heardst him — *I could never betray her. No matter what might happen.*

He never did. Not once. Not mad, not torn, not dying at my locked door. The record of the age will say Ranni the Witch was betrayed by no one.

Say it true, record: she was betrayed by no one, and she let the price of that land on the faithful.

Forgive me. No — strike that; I struck it in the prologue. He would forgive me, love; that is the exact shape of the wound. He would forgive me before I finished asking, with the snow still getting away, and would I help.

I would. I would.

The rest of the bill I read quickly — not because it weighs less, but because it never stops being weighed. It is the entry that has no total.

Under the gold, in the wet dark where the roots of the world's tree tangle the oldest dead, the prince who paid the Night's other half lies yet. His body would not die — I built the rite that made sure of it, and I will keep saying *I built* until the words wear through — and what a body becomes when its soul is subtracted and its death is withheld, the age has learned in its roots and its graves. A seeping. A wrongness in the world's deep water. Those who live in death, rising in the old barrows with their eyes full of nothing, hunted by the Order's hounds, dug out root by root by golden-masked men who call the work holy. Every one of them, love — every shambling grave-cold one of them, and every hunter's torch lit against them, and every village that learned to fear its own churchyard — is a line in this movement's bill, accruing, world without end, from a rite I signed.

Fia dreams her mending. I ceded thee the mark; perhaps her new law gives his broken continuance a place within an Order of Death restored; I have told thee why I had no right to vote. But dreams are not payments, and until some order — hers, mine, any — closes that account, it stands open in my name. It will stand open, I think, longer than this voyage. I sail under it. It does not darken the stars, love. It just never sets.

That is the ledger of the faithful, read aloud, unrounded: one smith, one wolf, one prince, one world's slow wound. Set against it, on the far page, what the spending bought: a leash cut; a night begun; a sky no power owns; and a woman free upon it who is — I will say the whole of it — worth less than what freed her, if souls are weighed one against one, and no one may so weigh them, and I have, every night of this voyage, and thou knowest I have, and that is why thou keepest the lamp filled and sayest nothing.

Freedom, I find, is purchased in a currency one's friends are made of. I knew the price going in. I paid it anyway, and — here is the sentence I have never said even to the dark — I would pay it again. That is what I am, love. That is the woman on the far side of every door in this testament: not one who did not know, and not one who cannot grieve.

One who knew, and grieved, and chose it anyway, and calls the choosing sovereignty, and lies awake beside thee testing whether the word holds.

It holds. It costs, and it holds. Like everything my house has ever forged.
