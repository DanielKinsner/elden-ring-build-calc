## III. The Crone in the Snow

Thou hast been looking at her face this whole voyage.

I watch thee decline to ask, and it is courteously done, and I am done permitting the courtesy. This face — the one I wear, the one thou hast learned; painted, still, imperfectly mine — is a dead woman's. I took it knowingly. I would take it again. And since no history holds her, since the golden record never once condescended to learn her name, her chapter will exist in exactly one place in all the world: here, in the testimony of her last student. Attend, then. This movement is a grave-good.

I found her — or was found; with her the grammar never resolved — deep in the woods, in the year my restlessness outgrew my tutors. Understand what I was by then: a royal child already remarked by the sky, drilled in glintstone by the Academy's finest, and bored the particular hungry boredom of a mind that has noticed all its teachers are afraid of it. I took to riding out. Riding became walking; walking became trespass; trespass, one grey afternoon with snow coming on, became a clearing I have never since found on any map, and a low house breathing smoke, and an old woman in the doorway who looked at me — at *me*, whom the whole lake-land looked at — with the mild interest one gives weather.

A crone, snowy-white, in robes the color of the season. She did not bow. She did not name me. She said — I have kept this, too, with the jewels — she said: *Thou'rt letting the cold in. Decide a side of the door.*

I decided. Understand this of me, whatever else the record claims: every leash I have ever slipped, I walked into that house of my own feet.

What did she teach me? Sorcery, yes — and I will come to the sorcery — but first she taught me the thing under it, without which the sorcery is just cleverness with a temperature.

The Academy taught warmth's arts, whatever it believed it taught: the full moon's grace, light bent to spectacle, power as a court wears power. My crone taught cold, and cold, she said, is not the absence of heat any more than silence is the absence of speech. Cold is a *discipline*. Heat spends; cold keeps. Heat declares; cold attends. All the golden order of the world, she said, every tree and law and singing sword of it, is a fire's opinion — and fires, child, are institutions with one argument, which they make until they die of it. What does not burn does not need to argue. Frost has no cathedral. Winter files no claims. It arrives, it preserves, it outlasts. She would set a cup of water on the sill of an evening, and in the morning hold up the ice to the light: *see*, she would say, *the shape of the vessel, kept — after the vessel's whole ambition has cracked off it.*

I was a child raised among people who mistook her for a hedge-witch. She was the first entire philosophy I ever met wearing wool.

The sorceries came as the doctrine's handwriting. Icecrag and frost-blade; the shaping of cold into edge and wall and lattice; arts the Academy's registers would file, with a clerk's little shudder, under *heresy*. She was well versed in every one, and she taught with a terrible economy — no praise, no punishment, only the assignment repeated until the assignment was beneath me, and then, without ceremony, the next. And she taught me her other craft, the one I have never seen practiced since and do not expect to see again this side of the night's far shore.

Understand also what the apprenticeship required of me at home, for the doubleness was itself a schooling. No one knew. Not my mother, whose own secret meetings had taught me the family art of the unmentioned; not my brothers; not, in those years, even Iji. A princess's days are watched the way coasts are watched — idly, constantly — and I learned to move through my own life as through hostile country: the false errand, the lamed horse, the tutor flattered into believing the missed lesson was his own idea. I was nine — ten? — when I first understood that I was running two selves on one face, and that the running was not exhausting me. It was *feeding* me. Some children are born double, love; the honest ones merely wait all their lives for a second life worth the keeping. The gold would one day call my whole existence a deception, and I have let the word stand in their records, because correcting it would require explaining something the gold has no organ to understand: that the girl in the manor was not a mask over the girl in the woods. Both were true. I have never in my life worn a mask that was not also a face — Renna will teach thee that again, movements hence — and I learned the craft of it there, on the snow-road between my two educations, before I had words for what I was practicing.

She made dolls.

Jointed, painted, patient things, ranged on her shelves like a quiet congregation. I thought them toys the first winter. The second winter I thought them art. The third winter I watched her set a finished one at the table across from her and regard it through an evening, and I understood, with the slow vertigo of true instruction, that they were *studies*. Essays in the question her whole life bent around: what is the least a body must be, for a will to keep house in it? What of flesh is essential, and what is merely — she loved this word, she said it the way priests say their god's name — merely *habit*.

Thou wilt have arithmetic running already; thou always hast. A secret mentor, versed in cold, hostile in her bones to the burning orders of the world, keeping a private academy of vessels — and her final student a girl the heavens had marked for a throne. Say the sum, then: was I found, or was I recruited? Did a bored princess stumble on a hedge-witch, or did something older and colder arrange a clearing in a wood for the one child in the world who would need, one day, to know exactly how much of a body is habit?

I have said I would rest my hand on locked doors. This is a door. I do not know. She never told me her purposes, or her origin, or her allegiances, or — hear it — her *name*. I asked once, in my third winter, grown bold. She looked at me the way she looked at held ice, and said that names were the first habit, and that she had cracked off hers long ago, and that if I lived as long as she intended — she said *intended*, I did not miss it, I filed it — I would understand.

There was a tower in the Three Sisters, in after years, that the maps called Renna's Rise. I left her garments in it. I took the name Renna once myself, for a night's work I will tell thee of in its place, and I could not say — I could not say under oath, my dear one, and this from a woman who has slain rather than perjure herself — whether I was borrowing her name, or the tower's, or no one's at all. A name with no owner is a room with no lock. I have made use of several.

One more winter is owed thee. The last.

I came to her — I was near grown by then, and the Fingers' attention had begun to settle on me in earnest, a weight I will give its own movement — and I found her seated among her congregation of dolls with one upon her lap, unfinished, its face as yet unpainted. And she looked at me longer than her economy had ever spent on looking, and she said the sentence I have carried further than any other freight in this life.

*Cold preserves, child. Remember what thou hast loved in the shape of me.*

I did not understand it as instruction. I understood it — later, thou wilt see how much later, and over what — as bequest. She was telling me, in the grammar of her discipline, what she was for. What the dolls had always been for. That somewhere in the long essay of her shelves there was a shape being kept against a winter she would not see, the way ice keeps the vessel's shape after the vessel's whole ambition has cracked away.

The histories, where they deign to notice the woman at all, will tell thee that the doll which came to house the soul of Ranni the Witch was modeled after her secret mentor. It is so. Every joint of these hands, this stillness I wear for a face — hers. And the world, when it troubles to think of it, thinks it either homage or convenience.

Let the world think. Thou, at least, wilt know: it was neither. It was a shape *kept for me* — or a shape I chose to believe was kept, which, I have found, the cold does not distinguish. When the hour came to decide what of a body is habit, I did not have to guess. I had been shown, three winters and a bequest, by the only teacher who never once asked me for anything.

I know now what such teachers cost. They ask for nothing so that thou wilt owe them everything, and be glad of the debt, and pay it across a hundred years to their painted face. That is not an accusation. It is the sincerest sentence in this movement.

She was gone before the Night of the Knives. How, I will not say, because I do not know, and I have grown into a woman who says *I do not know* with a straight spine. The house, the clearing, the congregation — I sought them once more, after, with all a demigod's resource. Snow, and standing wood, and no door to decide a side of.

Cold preserves. She never said what it preserves *from*. I have come to believe the answer is: from ending badly. Fires end badly — ask any kingdom. Ice merely — she would have loved this — ice merely *continues*.

So does she. Thou hast been looking at her face this whole voyage.

Now thou knowest whose. Ask thy questions of the paint; the paint is all either of us was given. And when this telling turns, movements hence, to the matter of my second body — remember the cup on the sill, and the morning ice held up to the light, and judge me then as her student.

For that is what I was. It is the one title I have never once thought of discarding.
