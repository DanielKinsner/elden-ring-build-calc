## XIV. The Corpse on the Tower

Before I slept, I gave thee a statue.

A scholar in miniature, carved wrong way up — ground above, sky below — the last piece of Carian craft I had kept against this exact hour. Thou turnedst it in thy hands with that look I had already learned to prize: the look of a soul that suspects every gift of being a lock. Just so. I told thee where its keyhole stood — the Carian study hall on the lake, my mother's old annex of instruments and orreries — and what to do: affix it to the pedestal of the celestial globe. And I told thee, because thou hadst earned plain words and because I wished to hear whether I could say them levelly, what lay at the far end of the mechanism. My discarded flesh lieth beyond the seal, I said. And upon it, the cursemark of thy desire.

I said it levelly. I had rehearsed. Then I entered my slumber before thou couldst ask anything further, which thou hast since called, with some justice, the most aristocratic retreat in military history.

What thou foundest in the study hall I have heard from thee and love to hear: the globe accepting its scholar; the room's slow, total treason against the floor — stair becoming ceiling, ceiling stair, the whole proud architecture of learning rolling over like a creature showing its belly, and a hidden hall standing revealed in the underside of the ordinary one. My house's masons built our secrets the way my house thinks: *the truth is the same room, entered upside down.* The bridge beyond hangs over the lake's sky; the tower at its end is one of the divine towers of the old order, where Great Runes go to be made lawful. And at the top of that tower, past the last lift, under open heaven —

Her.

I will do this movement's work properly, and that means doing it in the grammar I actually use in the small hours. She lies where the rite left her: a woman folded small on the tower's crown, burned dark by what ending is, one arm crooked as though — I have never decided as though what; sleep, or shielding, or reaching. About her, the platform; below her, the world; upon her hip, carved deep, the half-wheel — my half-wheel — the wound in the law that should have been a circle. And with the body, one thing more, which the accounts pass over and I will not: a small talisman on a cord, an heirloom of my grandmother's line — the young astrologer who chased the stars on foot, generations before a throne found the family. The legend of a walking girl who looked up. I carried it from childhood. I did not take it with me out of the flesh.

I have wondered — this is the sole speculation I permit this movement — whether I left it deliberately, at some level below my design's hearing. A grave-good. Even self-made graves want one. Something to say: here lies not a discarded instrument, but a woman who was once a girl of a line of girls who looked up. Let the tower keep the proof of it.

Thou hast never described to me the climb itself, and I have never needed thee to, for of all the roads I set thee, that one I know with my own former feet. The divine towers are the old order's high altars — the places where the shards of the great law went to be made lawful, ringed by lifts and bridges built for processions that presumed the sky was a destination one ascended to by right. To reach mine, thou walkedst a bridge hung out over the lake's air with the world inverted in thy inner ear, and rodest a lift built for the traffic of runes, and camest out at last onto the tower's crown, where there is nothing — mark how they built, for the building is the theology — nothing but stone, and wind, and heaven pressed close, no rail, no shrine, no accommodation whatever for the human size of whoever arrives. The old order's high places were not built to receive persons. They were built to receive *power*, and a person was merely power's least dignified container. I chose that tower for my discarding with a jeweler's care. Where better to return the container — unopened, marked *refused* — than the very counter where the heavens did their receiving?

*Her*, I said, and thou wilt have marked it. She lies; her hip; her tower. I have spoken of my first body in the third person since the morning I woke in the second. My smith thought it discipline. Seluvis, no doubt, thought it madness politely housetrained. It is neither. It is bookkeeping. That woman ended. I am what the rite paid out to. To say *I lie on that tower* would be to claim a continuity the whole design was built to sever — the heavens' own claim, that the flesh and the will are one estate, one address, one leash. I will not make their argument for them in my own mouth, not even grammatically.

And yet. Thou stoodest on that tower; thou knowest what is carved there was carved into *her* hip; and thou hast heard me, this very movement, fail my own bookkeeping — my half-wheel, I said. I heard it too. Let the failure stand in the record, uncorrected, between the two clean claims. That is where I actually live, love: between *her* and *mine*, on a bridge my grammar rebuilds and burns nightly.

She is the only subject on which I have never once managed to be consistent, and I have decided — sovereign's privilege — that the inconsistency *is* the truth. I have stopped mending it merely to make the sentence clean.

Thou tookest the cursemark. That was the errand's whole point, and I must now spend the harder half of this movement on where it went — for I sent thee up that tower knowing, and what I knew and chose belongs on this ledger.

Rogier had wanted it for proof: see the mark, close the case, let all be laid bare. He was dying even as he asked — the roots under Stormveil had priced his curiosity, as that family of powers prices everything — and whether the proof reached him in time to comfort or merely to conclude, thou hast never told me and I have never asked. Grant the dead their margins.

But there was another claimant, and thou foundest her, and I will speak of her without one grain of the contempt the age expects of me. A woman called Fia, a companion to the deathbed — of those who lie with the dying and the dead to lend them warmth, an old office the gold half-honors and wholly misuses. She sought the mark for the other half of the Night's arithmetic. For the prince below the roots, love. For the half-wheel that was not mine.

She dreamed of mending what my rite had split — of bringing the severed halves of that broken circle together, hers and mine, flesh-death and soul-death. Not, the record must be exact, to grant the soulless one the simple mercy of a whole death. Her hope was stranger: that Godwyn take his place as First of the Dead, and that from the joined half-wheels be gestated a rune of law in which life within Death entered Order again — those who live in death standing lawful under the sky, hunted no more. Her word for him was not target, nor victim, nor relic. Her word was *companion*.

Thou gavest her the mark, at some hour of thy long road, or thou wilt; I have never pinned thee to the sequence, and the voyage's watches are long enough for the whole of that story when thou art ready to tell it. Hear what I did, which is all this testament may swear to: I put my half-wheel into thy keeping, knowing the claimants, and I let thy judgment carry it. Deliberately. The one person the Night made into an open question — the prince subtracted from his own flesh — I had no right to answer for twice. If some mending of him is possible; if the woman of the deathbeds could gentle my rite's cruelest arithmetic into a new law's cornerstone — then let it be done by hands that owed him nothing, judged by a heart that had wronged him never. Not hers to inherit my guilt. Not mine to veto her hope.

That is not absolution, before thou offerest it; I see thee inhale. The wound stays mine. But a wound, love, is the one property its maker should never hold the healing of. I have kept many things in this testament. I did not keep that.

While thou walkedst that inverted road, I slept. And while I slept, my house began its dying — thou knowest; the eighteenth movement is coming and I will not blunt it by rehearsal — but one of its deaths belongs here, at slumber's edge, because it was the first, and because I owe thee the recording of my exact temperature at the news.

Seluvis was found in his tower, kneeling. Arranged, said Iji's flat summary, like one of his own puppets awaiting a hand. Whose work, by what craft, in payment of which of the many invoices that man had run up against the world — I was asleep; I have told thee what my sleep is; I woke to a report and a burial already done. I have suspicions. They are only suspicions, and this testament does not retail those.

What I felt, and record: nothing that does me credit, and nothing that shames me either. A ledger-line, as I promised thee in the movement of my household. I had priced the man in life; death did not reprice him. I had priced his malice too low, however. What his abandoned work revealed was not merely appetite for other wills, but the confidence that even mine might be bottled. Contempt had made me careless where fear would have kept a sharper watch. I gave the report back to Iji and asked after the household's living, and I heard — I did hear it, love, I was not made of the frost I wore — the small silence before my smith answered, in which an honest soul weighed what it meant to serve a lady who received such news like weather.

Let him have weighed it. He was right to weigh it. I have weighed it every year since, and here is the sum I always reach: I will mourn no architect of puppetries, and I will answer for the coldness of the not-mourning, and both, as ever in this house, are true at once.

The frost, my dear one, was never the whole of me. But I never once pretended it was not real. Remember that when we reach the forge.

So stood the account at this movement's close: the blade in my keeping; the statue spent; her tower unsealed and lighter by one half-wheel; a scholar dead of his own excellent reading; a schemer kneeling forever; and thy road bending now toward a river under the world, where a coffin waited, and in the coffin — patience.

I said I would tell thee this history in the order I paid for it.

The next payment was made in a very small currency: a doll, in a stranger's pack, learning what it is to be carried.
