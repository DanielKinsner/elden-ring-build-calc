## XVII. Beneath Manus Celes

Give me thy hand again. The left one — the one the ring went past, on its way to mine. This movement I will tell holding it, because this is the one movement of the twenty-one with nothing withheld in it. No doors, love. No hand resting on locks. For one movement of this testament, thou shalt have the thing itself, whole, which is the only wedding-gift a woman like me has ever had to give.

First, what lay dead under the cathedral, and how it came to be dead, for the record must hold my last deed of arms before it holds my marriage.

My Two Fingers. The very ones. The hand raised over my candidacy in the years of the shine on the word Empyrean; the old voice repeating itself beneath its cathedral; the instrument that had chosen me a throne, issued me a shadow, priced my flesh at leash. All the age of my design it had waited there — for the Fingers do not pursue; why should they? property returns, or is returned — waited under Manus Celes for its candidate to tire of straying.

I returned. With the treasure of the Eternal City in my keeping.

Of the deed itself I will give thee what I possess — I told thee: nothing withheld — and what I possess is less than the ballads suppose. The blade knew its work. That is the plainest way I can put a thing I have no craft-words for. The Nox forged — inherited — were bequeathed — a thing whose whole nature is that one wound: the harm of the Greater Will's vassals, kept whetted through an age of dark like a promise. I brought edge and purpose to the appointed place, and what happened under the cathedral happened as much *through* me as by me — the debt of a banished people discharging itself along my arm, an age of stolen sky arriving, all at once, at a reckoning the size of two fingers.

It did not cry out. I do not know that it could. It trembled — as it had always trembled; I had taken that trembling, girlhood-long, for the palsy of age, and I wondered, in the last of it, whether it had been fear the whole time; whether the old voice repeating itself had been repeating itself the way the cornered do; whether something in that chamber had known for a century what its candidate was becoming, and had gone on rendering its three assurances anyway, to the end, because assurance was the only craft it had —

Thou seest. Nothing withheld includes the doubt. I slew the instrument of my leash, and I do not repent it, and there was a moment, after, in the stillness of that low stone room, when I stood over the great dead hand of my whole captivity and found that what I felt was not triumph.

It was quiet. Only quiet. The particular quiet of a house, after; thou knowest it. I had expected freedom to have a sound. It has instead an *absence*, exactly the size of the thing removed, and a woman stands in it and hears, for the first time in her life, nothing at all above her — and must decide, in that terrible new roominess, whether what she has made is an open sky or merely a larger cell.

I sat down upon the corpse of my jailer to think about it. That is where thou foundest me. Now thou knowest what the doll was doing there, all those hours, with her four hands folded. She was listening to the quiet. She was deciding what it was.

And then the quiet ended — with footsteps on the sealed road. And I knew what the sky was, because something I wanted was moving under it, toward me, of its own free will.

I sat with the dead hand a long while before thy footsteps came — days, by the altar-plateau's slow light; the doll does not count hours and the newly freed do not wish to — and I will give thee one more furnishing of that waiting, because it belongs to the movement's honesty. The cathedral above my head was a ruin. Manus Celes: a chapel the size of a modest barn, roof long fallen, standing alone on the high grass with its saints worn blank — and beneath it, reached by a ragged hole in the very floor, the chamber of the power that had presumed to own me. That is where they had kept my leash's far end, all those years. Not in a citadel. Not behind armies. In a hole under a small ruined church at the top of a sealed mountain — because the Order's whole security, I understood at last, sitting there, had never been walls. It had been *belief*. No wall was needed around a thing no one could imagine harming. The Fingers slept unguarded for the same reason the sky goes unguarded: assurance, bottomless assurance, that reaching them was a thought no creature under heaven would finish thinking.

I finished it. And the quiet I sat in afterward was the sound of an entire cosmology discovering it had never once locked its own door. Let every order to come — mine first — keep that ruin in its catechism: what cannot be questioned does not build defenses. And what does not build defenses has forgotten that it can bleed.

Thou camest down into the cathedral's underroom with the ring.

I will set the scene exactly, for our grandchildren in the far cold — whatever shape grandchildren take, for such as we. A small stone chamber under a small stone church, at the top of a sealed world. The great hand dead in the middle of it like a felled idol. On the hand, a doll, sitting, as queens have never once sat anywhere. And before the doll: a Tarnished of no house — grave-dust, lake-water, rot-script, void- ash, dragon-frost, every station of my warning worn openly like campaign ribbons — holding out, in silence, in an open palm, a little leaden ring.

Thou didst kneel. I have never told thee what that meant, so hear it now: posture is not fealty. The age was one entire genuflection — everything knelt, to trees, to Fingers, to flames, to fates — but thou didst lower thyself only to where a small painted hand rested, and simply *offered*. As one equal stoops to meet another where she is. As thou laidst Rogier's accusation on my table, a lifetime of movements ago: a thing that plainly belonged to me, returned to its owner.

And I said — thou knowest what I said; the record keeps it and I would not change a grain: *So, it was thee, who would become my Lord. Perhaps I needn't have warned thee. I am pleased, however.*

Perhaps I needn't have warned thee. Under the arch of it — hear the confession sixteen movements bought — under the arch, that sentence was the white flag of my entire fortified heart. The warning was the last wall I had. Thou hadst walked through it as through weather. *I am pleased, however*: four words, love, doing the work my whole vocabulary refused — and thou didst hear them for what they were, and had the grace not to smile more than slightly.

Then the ring went onto this painted hand, and the oldest cold tradition of my house closed over us both.

Let me teach it to thee properly, the tradition into which thou didst marry — thou hadst it that day only as ceremony; have it now as law.

In Caria, the moon is matrilineal. Queen to princess, hand to hand, garden to garden: the meeting is arranged by the mother, but the moon does its own choosing, and what it chooses, it marks. And when a chosen daughter takes a spouse, the house does not receive the newcomer with lands or seals. It gives — this is the whole of it — a sword. A greatsword of the moon, bestowed by the Carian queen upon her spouse, in honor of a tradition older than the royalty itself. My mother gave one so, once, to a red-haired champion out of the gold; thou hast seen where that giving ended, and so had I, and I gave anyway. Weigh that as thou wilt.

The blade I bestowed on thee that day thou hast carried since: the Dark Moon Greatsword. My sigil is in it — a full moon, cold and leaden, the same that rides my seal and my ring — and the sword, the smiths say truly, is but a beam of it. Attend to the grammar of the gift, for it is the grammar of everything I am able to promise thee. A beam is not the moon. The moon I could not give thee — the moon is not mine to give; I am its marked one, not its mistress. What my house gives its consorts is the *light as it arrives*: cold, exact, faithful, drawn out long and given an edge. That is the covenant of the Carian marriage, said in steel because my family has never once managed to say anything important in words: *I cannot give thee the source of me. I give thee what of it can be held.*

And now the word the whole movement has been circling. Lord. The ring's own inscription grants it: Ranni is an Empyrean, so runs the old law, and her consort would by rights earn the title of lord. Thou hast never once asked me what thou art to me — champion, servant, consort, lord — in all the years since. So I will rule on it here, in the open record, with nothing withheld, and the ruling is final.

A champion fights for a cause and is spent by it. Thou wast that, and I spent thee, and that account is closed and its shame is mine. A servant obeys. Thou never wast that — thou didst disobey me at the one hour it mattered most, with a ring in thy hand. A consort — the old law's word — is a title granted *by* a throne, and it smells of the granting; everything the Fingers ever offered me was a consort's bargain, sovereignty on a leash of gratitude, and I will not hand down what I would not wear.

Lord, then. But hear which lordship, for I am rewriting this word as we use it. Not lord *over* — the night has no over. Not lord *under* — I did not slay the fingers of one hierarchy to found another with a wedding. Lord *beside*. The old law says my consort earns the title by rights. I say — and mine is now the law that says — that thou didst earn it walking: past a warning, across a lake of the world's illness, through the void's bailiff, up a sealed road, into a quiet the size of my whole former captivity, where thou didst kneel before the last free demigod of the age and offer, without yielding one grain of equality, a found thing to its owner.

That is what a Lord is, in the order not of gold. One who comes on, steady, past every posted argument. One who can kneel without becoming less.

The wedding was in a stone room the size of a pantry, love, on the corpse of a god's hand, with a dragon bleeding on the road above and not one witness of woman born. My mother's was under the full moon before the massed towers of an academy. I have seen the pictures.

I would not trade.
