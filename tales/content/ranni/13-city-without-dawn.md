## XIII. A City Without Dawn

What does a false sky look like, when it is *home*? Thou hast walked under Nokron's night — I never have; mark the strangeness of that; the city my whole design orbited, I know only by report and treasure. Its people I know another way: by inheritance. For the Nox, my dear one, are the elder firm of the family business. Everything I am was, by them, attempted first — the refusal, the treason, the price. This movement is their chapter, and I tell it as debtor, not historian.

Hear their history as my house kept it, under the versions the gold permits.

Long ago — before the Erdtree's account of the world begins; the Nox are among the items that account was written to omit — there was a people of the night. Mistresses of silver, readers of the dark sky, keepers of arts the later Order would file, as it filed my crone, under heresy. They served — or kept; the grammar is worn away — a black moon of their own, and their creed survives on the armor of their descendants in one long patient sentence: that they await their liege, and the coming age of the stars, and their Lord of Night.

And they *invoked the ire of the Greater Will*. So the record states it, flat as a tombstone, and like a tombstone it withholds the cause. What did they do? I have heard every guess the underworld sells. I will not retail them. I know what my house knows: that whatever it was, it was called treason by the power that punishes with skies — and that the sentence, when it came, came *down*. A star was loosed upon them — no metaphor, love; thou hast fought the very animal — a malformed thing of the outer void that fell upon an Eternal City and devoured its heaven. Took away their sky. Sit with the sentence. Not their walls, which stood; not their people, who lived. Their *sky*. The Order above understood, as I have always granted it understands, exactly how to price a punishment: kill a rebel and you make a martyr; take a rebel's sky and every generation after is born already sentenced.

They went under. Down past Siofra and Ainsel with what silver and craft could be carried, and there, in the great vaulted dark, they did the thing for which I honor them above every people in this world's history.

They built the night again. By hand.

Thou hast seen it — the false stars thou couldst not describe. Lanterns and craft and longing, hung in courses over Nokron and Nokstella, wheeling on their makers' law. An entire banished civilization, condemned to dark by heaven's own verdict, that looked at its sentence and said: *very well. We will make a better sky than the one you stole.* The gold calls it blasphemy's monument. I call it the most dignified act of defiance in the record of the world, and I will hear no appeal.

Two relics of theirs govern my story, and thou broughtest me one of each. Take them in order of their sadness.

The first: the silver tears. Thou hast met them — the gleaming mimic-things of the deep cities, quicksilver that studies a shape and wears it. Toys, the surface-folk suppose, or vermin. Neither. They were an *attempt*, love. The Nox, awaiting their Lord of Night through generation after skyless generation, at some hour lost patience with waiting — and I of all souls will not fault them — and undertook to *forge* their lord. To grow, from their mirror-craft, a being that could wear lordship's shape: a manufactured deliverer. The mimic tears are what came of it. Perfect imitation; no sovereign under the imitation. A shape that fights, that walks, that wears thee better than thy own reflection does — and hollow at the crown, every one, forever. The enterprise failed as it had to fail, and I want its epitaph in this testament because the whole age of gold should be made to read it: *a lord cannot be forged. Neither by silver, nor — patience, we are coming to the cathedral — by Fingers.*

The second relic they kept in their deepest vault, and it is the reason thy road and mine ever crossed. A blade. Born — the record says, and I have handled the thing and believe the record — born of a corpse. Whose corpse, what rite, what hour: the Nox kept their counsel and mine keeps theirs. What is written of it plainly is this: it is the proof of the Eternal City's high treason; it symbolizes their downfall; and it can do the one thing no made thing in all the Order's world is permitted to do. It can wound the Greater Will and its vassals.

The Fingerslayer Blade. Say the name slowly; it is the entire design in two words. Not finger-*harmer*. Not finger-*binder*. The banished people, under their handmade sky, kept whetted through the whole long insult of their sentence the single instrument in creation fit to kill the instruments of heaven — kept it, mark this, not as a weapon in an armory but as a *treasure* in a vault. A people's hope, shaped like a knife. They never used it. Whether they could not, or dared not, or were keeping it — my house has always believed *keeping it* — for the liege their creed awaits: another door with other heirs, and I lay my hand on it and pass.

There is a relic of theirs I should set beside the blade, for the two together make the whole portrait. In Nokstella, beneath the very throne of the city, their keepers held a talisman: the memory of their lost black moon, worked small enough to carry. The moon of Nokstella, the inscription runs, was the guide of countless stars.

The *guide*, love. Not the queen of the stars, not their warden, not their law — their guide. I have carried that word through every year of my legislating since, the way other sovereigns carry scepters. For here was a whole cosmology, older than the gold, in which the moon's office was neither to rule the wheeling lights nor to own them, but to walk ahead of them through the dark — and the Order that fell on those people found even that too much sovereignty to permit the governed sky. They kept the talisman under the throne. Mark the architecture of that choice as I marked it: not in the treasury, with wealth; not in the temple, with worship. Under the seat of power — so that whoever ruled the Eternal City sat, knowingly, all their reign, upon the memory of what ruling was *for*. When my own night was still a house of unnumbered rooms, I took my first article of law from beneath a buried city's chair: guidance above governance. A moon that leads is followed. A moon that grips is fled. I have been both in my time, and buried the second, and this movement is part of the grave.

And a woman without a fate could not so much as lift it.

There is a clause in the blade's own nature — thou feltest it in thy grip — that it cannot be wielded by those without a fate. Now count my inventory of that season: flesh, discarded; Great Rune, cast aside; my line of light deliberately slipped from every reader's book. I had made myself fateless with both hands — it was the masterwork of my freedom — and the masterwork disqualified me from the one blade my freedom's completion required. The heavens, love, keep a tidy sense of humor; they simply bill it to other people. I needed a bearer with a fate the blade would answer — a fate with weight and motion, aimed, of its own will, in my direction.

I have told thee what I thought the first hour I met thee. Now thou knowest what I was looking *at*. Not a courier. A qualification.

Set it on the scale against everything gentler I have said, for it is true alongside all of it: the marriage aboard this ship began as an armory requisition. Thou didst meet the specification. Then — this is the part no design of mine drafted — thou didst exceed it, and kept exceeding it, until the requisition form was somewhere at the bottom of a lake and I was standing at a tower window unfit for state business over a *nod*. I keep accurate books. They are not always flattering to the bookkeeper.

Thou wentest down through the crater my brother's death had opened, into the elder night. Of thy road — the ancestor-spirits, the hunting shades, the sacred ground the Nox keep holiest — thou hast told me pieces, and I hoard them. One piece I asked thee to repeat twice, and thou wilt remember which: that at the path's worst turning thou wast met by thine own shape, silver and hollow, wearing thee the way the tears wear everything — and that thou didst cut it down and walked on without a word to anyone until I asked. Thou hast never once named the thing an omen. Neither of us believes in omens. We are married; we may believe in them for each other. I have.

And in the vault under the sacred dark, thou tookest up their treasure — it answered thy fate; I felt the answer through half a country of stone, a cold ringing along the old treason-lines of the world — and thou broughtest it, past everything the deep sends against bearers of hope shaped like knives, up out of the first rebellion's grave, to the top of my tower. And put it in my hands.

*Ahh. It was thee. Not Blaidd, it seemeth.* My own words, and thou hast teased me with them since — the arch surprise, as though I had wagered on the wolf and lost. Hear their true freight at last: it was not surprise that thou hadst prevailed. It was that in my century of designing, through every draft of the plan I had drawn under my mirrored smith's lamp, the hand that delivered the Fingerslayer Blade had always, always been Blaidd's. Thou hadst not outrun my expectations, love. Thou hadst outrun my *design* — stepped, without knowing it, into my shadow's place in the architecture. And I stood holding the treasure of the Eternal City, complete at last, every piece in place, and what I felt under the triumph, cold and early, like frost in a month that should not bear it, was the first arithmetic of the eighteenth movement:

*if this stranger now stands where my wolf stood — where does my wolf now stand?*

I gave thee my thanks. I gave thee a statue's key to my own dead flesh — the next movement's business. And I went down into slumber with the blade beside me and that question unanswered in the dark, where it grew.

The Nox are still down there, love, under their handmade heaven, waiting as they have waited out the whole insolent age of gold. Their creed names no names; it says only: the coming age of the stars, and their Lord of Night.

I have begun that age. I do not know if I am what their long patience meant — whether the liege their armor promises is a moon-marked witch out of Caria or some truer sovereign their sky has yet to show them. I have learned to leave other people's prophecies unclaimed; I have seen what claiming does.

But I sail under a night no power stole and none may repossess. And if that is not their Lord, it is at least, at last, their *weather*. When the histories of the deep are written under real stars, I ask only this entry: that the debt ran downward. The banished built the first new sky. Mine is the second. Theirs was braver. They built it without a blade, without a demigod, without a hope in heaven.

Out of a whole age of night, they made a city. I only made a wedding.
