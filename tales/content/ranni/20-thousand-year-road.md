## XX. A Thousand-Year Road

Of thy last battle I will tell nothing except what I did during it, which no record holds and which thou hast never once asked, though thou wakest sometimes with the question on thy face.

I waited. That is all, and it is everything, for consider who waited: a woman who had spent a century proving she need wait on nothing — fates cut, Fingers slain, flesh discarded precisely so that no hour of any power's choosing could ever again be appointed for her. Free of every clock in creation. And there she sat in the roads between the stars, sovereign of the coming night, doing the one thing her whole architecture had abolished. Waiting. On thee.

For it was thine to do, love — that was the compact's last cold clause. I could make ready a sky; I could not win thee thy throne, nor would I have stolen the winning if I could. Past the ash and the thorns, in the ruin of the golden city, thou wentest down to the wreck of everything — the shattered ring, the tree's hollow heart, the old god's last shape of light — and what passed there passed between thee and the age, alone, as it had to.

And then thou madest thy choice. Attend, ages, if any age ever reads this: there were other hands held out at that threshold. Thou couldst have mended the gold — several golds; the world was thick with candidates for thy crowning, dreams and flames and orders enough to stock a market. Thou didst stand where every power of a dying epoch converged in offering, bearing the memory of every claim and wound the age had placed in thy keeping — and of everything on that counter, thou calledst *me*.

How thou calledst me deserves its own paragraph, for the instrument was no trumpet. At the threshold of the broken order, among all the marks and signs the age's factions had pressed on thee, thou soughtest the cold one: the sign of my summoning, blue as deep ice, that had waited for that hour since the design was young.

The cursemark was not its key. Let the record be exact, even where exactness costs me a beautiful symmetry. The sign answered the road we had completed: blade delivered, warning crossed, ring placed, my order heard. Thou calledst me with no wound of mine, but with thy choice.

Yet the wound belongs in the accounting. Thou hadst climbed to it and read what my freedom cost in the flesh that paid first. The heavens branded me a debtor. Thou didst read the brand as testimony, and the blue sign as a door. I have built an entire jurisprudence, somewhere in these twenty movements, on smaller inversions than that.

I came. Thou knowest how I came — not the doll, not the small cold hand from the pack; I came at the scale the hour demanded, the moon-marked woman entire, drawn up out of the night I had been building to stand at last upon the wreck of the order that had priced her. The tellers who were not there have painted it grand and terrible. Thou, who wast, know what I actually did first.

I looked at thee. The battle over; the beast of gold gone out; the age lying broken open like a geode around us — and the sovereign of the coming night spent her first moment of triumph checking her consort for wounds. The books may keep the grandeur. That look is the true coronation, and it crowned thee, not me.

Then I said the words. I have given them to thee across nineteen movements in their long form; the world got them short, and the world has been arguing about them since, and so — one last correction, testament, and then the correcting is done forever.

I swore to every living being and every living soul. An oath, love — the night opened with an *oath*, made by the woman who had cut every leash ever fastened on her, voluntarily fastening one more: herself to the world's whole living freight. That none of them be handled as I was handled. That the law over them be light, not hand. Now cometh the age of the stars, I said. A thousand year voyage under the wisdom of the Moon.

And then the sentence they have carried crookedest of all. Hear it as I swore it. To all of life I said: *the chill night is real, and it is mine to bear away — and thou mayest think of it as far, far away.* A promise of distance, love. Not a shroud drawn over the world — a burden drawn *off* it. The night that encompasses, the night that reaches — let the mistellings keep their grammar; thou wast there; the night I proclaimed was the night I was *taking with me*, out of every cradle and grave and law of the living, to the one place it can do its governing without its governing ever closing round a throat. Far away. Where the stars are. Where I now am.

And the fear, the doubt, the loneliness, the path into darkness — those words too they have hung on the world's neck, as though I sentenced the age to them. Hear where I placed their weight when I spoke. The fear was for *us*. The road into dark was *ours* — the voyage's freight, the price of the bearers, the three companions I had inventoried once already, years before, lying small in a coffin on an underground river. I never promised the world would feel no fear. I promised the world the fear of the *law* would leave with the law, and that the law's keepers would carry their own share out where the keeping happens. We are carrying it now. It is heavy exactly as often as thou hast seen me come to thy side of the ship at strange hours, and no heavier, because thou art there.

And then — thou wilt smile; I am going to enter it into the record anyway, because it is the record that matters most and no chronicle on earth holds it —

then I altered a word.

I had drafted that speech, love, the way I draft everything: years deep, every syllable costed. And the draft said — had always said, since long before I knew thy name — *my fair consort, eternal*. Fair: the word of the old courtesies, the word a queen uses at a distance, approving, gracious, sealed. The whole speech was built in the key of sovereignty, and *fair* was its final cadence: correct, cold, and safe.

But thou hadst come back to a tower with a war unfinished to ask a doll what her law was for. Thou hadst heard the order I envision — the only soul who ever asked, the only ears the whole of it was ever said into. And standing there on the wreck of the gold, with the drafted word in my mouth, I looked at the one who had listened, and the sovereign's cadence went the way of my fourth-try vanity in the rucksack, and what came out — unbudgeted, undrafted, the single unplanned syllable in a century of design —

*Let us go, together. My dear consort, eternal.*

Dear. It is one word, love. I have moved worlds with less. Nothing I have ever said cost more, and nothing was cheaper at the price: for *fair* is what a queen calls what she has chosen, and *dear* is what a woman calls what she cannot spare. The record of the age will keep my treasons, my rites, my night entire. Let this testament keep the truer scandal: that at the last, before the whole broken listening world, the witch of the leashes corrected her own draft — and married thee in the amendment.

Then the going. The old world does not keep pictures of it, and ours needs none; it is the room we live in. The rising dark; the towers, the graves, the juniper, the library, the forge with the name over it that only I can read, all growing small and precious and finally indistinguishable from the general blue of the world; and ahead — this, love, no movement of mine will ever render, and I have built skies — ahead, the open night. Our road. A thousand years of it, at the least accounting, and I have never once told thee what waits at its end, because — last honesty of the movement — I do not know. I know what I intend: the law hung high, the far cold made a keeping-place, the long work of teaching stars to hold what fingers held. Beyond my intention, the dark is dark to me too. I am not thy guide out here, whatever the wedding-songs say. I am only the one who knew the first step, and took it, and found — his was the discovery; I merely confirm it — that the first step is the whole of the knowing anyone gets.

So I will close this movement where the leash of my prologue comes at last to its transposition, and then I am done with movements, and owe thee only a coda.

I slew mine own flesh because the heavens mistook it for a leash. I have told thee all the tellings of that sentence now — the theft it required, the Night it cost, the faithful it spent, the night it bought. Hear the last telling. On this ship, in this dark, there is one tether left in all my cosmos: thy hand, in mine, on the rail, in the hours when the fear and the doubt and the loneliness come to collect. A leash, love — by every law of my old arithmetic, a leash: a binding, held at both ends, by which each may be pulled where the other goes.

I keep accurate books. I have entered it as a leash. I have entered it, also, as the single holding in a long acquisitive life that I acquired by *surrender* — the one bond no heaven fastened, no Finger tied, no design required: elected, ratified in a stone pantry, amended before the ruined gold, and renewed, without ceremony, every time thou takest the rail beside me.

The correction of the record is complete. The heavens mistook my flesh for a leash, and died of the error. Thou heldest out thy hand, and asked for nothing, and I —

Well then. Shall we?

I have always wanted to say it and mean only the road.
