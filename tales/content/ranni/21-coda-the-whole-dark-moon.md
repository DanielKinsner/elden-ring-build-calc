## Coda: The Whole Dark Moon

The telling is done. What remains is small, and I will say it small.

Here is what I did. I stole Death. I forged the knives. I gave them into aimed hands. I slew the body I was born into, and cast it away, and the same night a golden prince lost his soul, and half of that broken wheel is carved into flesh I once answered to. I armed one brother against a jailer and required the death of the other. I let a wolf pay for my freedom with his mind, and a smith with his life, and I took an old woman's face and a banished people's hope and a stranger's fate, and I built out of all of it a night, and hung the law where no hand reaches, and left.

That is the list. I have kept it short not to make it light, but because I have learned that a long confession is a place to hide. Nothing in this one is hiding.

Here is what I know. That the heavens' order was real, and its kindness was real, and its kindness was a hand that never lifted, and I would cut every cord of it again. That freedom is not innocence, and I have never once confused them. That the ones who loved me were braver than I, each in the coin of their own nature — a wolf against his marrow, a smith against his terror, a mother against her ruin, a stranger against a written warning — and that I am the beneficiary of courage I mostly did not deserve and entirely will not waste.

And here is what I refuse to pretend, now and at the end as at the beginning. I will not pretend to tell thee what I believed the knives would find; that door is shut, and I carry the key and the weight both, and they are one object. I will not pretend to know my teacher's name, nor whose face rides above my own with its one eye closed, nor what regarded me in my mother's garden, nor what waits at this road's end. A testament that ended with its mysteries solved would be a forgery of the very world it was sworn in. The world keeps locked rooms. I have kept mine honestly: in plain sight, with my hand resting on the wood.

One matter more, and then the small saying that ends it.

There will be tellers, in the ages this night is long enough to hold, who will say she left. That the witch, having won, abandoned the world she unburdened — took her law and her love and her cold and went where no prayer carries. And the tellers will not be lying, quite. I did leave. Leaving was the law's whole architecture; I have spent a movement teaching thee why the remove must include the remover.

But abandonment, love, is a word for the dark. And I did not leave the world dark.

Look up — whatever age thou art, whoever finds this testament in whatever keeping. Look up on any clear night. There is a moon. It does not warm thee; it never will; warmth was the old bargain, and its price was a hand about the throat. But it keeps the dark honest. It shows the roads without choosing thine. It moves the great waters and asks nothing of them by name. It is there every night of every year of every age — distant, exact, faithful — and it has never once come down, and it has never once gone out.

That is not a god over thee. That is not a mother's hand upon thee. That is a promise, hung at the one distance where promises keep.

Chosen distance is not abandonment. It is the only fidelity I had left to invent — the love that proves itself by never closing its hand. I learned it from a cup of ice on a sill; I studied it through the bars of a golden cage; I sealed it on the corpse of a god's fingers; I say it now, at the end of the testament of Ranni, in the smallest words the truth will fit inside:

I did not leave the world. I left it the night sky.

And I am in it — not gone, love; *far*, which this whole telling has labored to teach is a different country — riding the long dark between the stars of my own oath, with my dear consort beside me and a thousand years of road ahead, and of everything I have ever been called — witch, princess, traitor, liar, queen of the coming night — hear the one title I claim with my whole unleashed will, the one I chose, the one that was never fitted to me while I slept:

Not alone.

The two half-wheels were never mended; some breaks are the truth of the thing broken, and I carry mine whole in their brokenness. But the dark moon, love — the dark moon was never broken. It was only ever waiting, entire, behind the light it declined to borrow.

It is risen now. Let it keep the dark honest, and the honest company.

The rest is distance — and distance, at last, is ours.

*— Here ends the Testament of Ranni, the Whole Dark Moon.*
