## Prologue: The Leash

I slew mine own flesh because the heavens mistook it for a leash.

There. It is said, and said first, so that thou mayest never claim I buried it. Whatever else this telling is, it will not be a burial. I have discarded one body already; I am not in the habit of digging graves for the truth as well.

They tell the story otherwise, where stories are still told. They say: on a night with no moon, knives went into the Erdtree's fairest son, and a witch was behind it, and the world broke of grief. And I have read that account carved into weapons and whispered through keyholes, and I find it accurate in every particular but the ones that matter. It is a history written by an Order that numbers its dead in one column only.

So hear the correction, from the only mouth that owns it.

Two demigods died that night. One of them is speaking to thee now.

When they say I escaped the price, ask them — I give thee this question as a gift; use it on scholars, they startle beautifully — ask them why the receipt was carved into my own discarded skin. Thou hast seen it. Thou didst climb a tower turned wrong-side-up in the sky to stand over what I used to be, a corpse folded small atop the world with half a wheel cut into it. The other half went into a golden prince. We were priced together, he and I, on one broken circle, and no telling that forgets it deserves the name of history.

Understand what this telling is not.

It is not a plea. I have never once asked absolution — not of the Fingers, not of my accusers, not of thee — and I am too old to acquire the habit aboard a voyage with no confessor in a thousand miles of night.

It is not a defense. A defense implies a court whose authority I recognize. I recognize none. That refusal cost more than any court would have dared to fine me, and I paid it, and some of it was paid by others, in a currency I will count out before we are done. Thou wilt hear their names. I do not spend them quickly.

And it is not, my dear one, an explanation — not entirely. There are rooms in this account I will walk thee past and not unlock. I tell thee now that the doors exist. I will even rest my hand upon them as we pass, so thou shalt know precisely what I withhold and precisely that I am withholding it. That much honesty I can afford. It is the cheapest kind: the honesty of a woman pointing at her own locked doors. The expensive honesty comes later.

Why speak at all, then? A fair question. I have asked it of myself in the dark of this voyage, which is a dark with a great deal of room in it for questions.

Because thou camest.

I wrote a warning on a ring, in my own words, in my own cold hand: that the solitude beyond the night was better mine alone. I wrote it the way one writes to strangers — plainly, expecting obedience. Thou didst read it, and weigh it, I trust, for thou weighest everything; and then thou didst carry that ring through rot and void and dragonfire and put it on my hand regardless. I have slain the fingers of gods for less disobedience. Instead I find myself — the word still fits me strangely, like a gown cut for a larger life — I find myself glad.

A person who follows thee past thy warnings is owed one of two things: a widening of the distance, or the truth. I tried the first for an age. It did not take.

So. The truth, then — told once, whole, in my order and no other. Where the record speaks, I will speak with it. Where the record is silent, I will tell thee whether the silence is the world's or mine. Thou shalt have the theft, the rite, the Night and both its deaths; the doll and the long waiting; a wolf, a smith, a schemer; a brother under a locked sky; a blade born of a corpse; a wedding on the body of a god's hand; and a night I promised to keep far from the world, which thou hast chosen instead to sail into at my side.

I will not soften it. Thou didst not marry a soft thing.

Begin, then, where all leashes are first sewn: in a house that loved me.
