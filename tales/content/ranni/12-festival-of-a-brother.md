## XII. The Festival of a Brother

I must now thank thee, formally, for a death in my family. I have drafted this sentence for years, love, and there is no arrangement of it that sits. Hear it in its bones, then, unupholstered: my design required my brother's ending; thou wast the instrument my design had recruited; thou wentest, and he ended, and every good that has followed — the opened sky, the risen city, the age we sail — stands on that floor. A wife who prices everything must price this too, aloud, once. It is priced. It does not close. Now let me tell it in its order.

The road to Nokron ran first *down* — thou foundest that out before I could tell thee. Down a well's throat in the Mistwood, into Siofra, the river under the world, where the Eternal City's outriders once kept their watch. Thou camest back up changed in a small way I noted and filed: thou hadst seen the false stars. The banished people's answer to a stolen sky — constellations of lantern and craft, hung in the cavern dark, wheeling on no law but their makers' longing. Thou didst try to describe them to me and abandoned the trying, and I loved thee early for the abandonment. Yes: even a forged sky can be past words. Remember the falseness of those stars, and remember that they were beautiful anyway. Both facts govern the far end of this testament.

Blaidd was below by then, working the same riddle from the river's side. Thou foundest him at his post — mid-oath, probably; his patience swore like a dockhand, it was the one un-knightly seam in him — and between the two of thee the riddle gave up its shape. The way into Nokron was no gate. It was a wound: the city could be entered only as the calamity that sealed it had entered, from above. Something must fall. And nothing would fall, in all that arrested heaven, while the general my blood held the stars in his two vast hands.

Every road of mine, thou seest, had run to the same door for an age. Now my own recruits had walked it to its hinge. My wolf came up from Siofra and stood in my tower and said the thing Iji and I had each carried unsaid for years, because saying it set the price: *Then it's Radahn, my lady. The stars, or the general. There's no third heading.*

Said aloud at last, by the most honest mouth in my service. I gave the order. I will not launder it into "I consented" or "the design required." Thou wilt find those phrasings in the movement's earlier drafts, which is why this testament gets no earlier drafts. I gave the order.

What we learned next, the age had learned before us: that killing Radahn was no longer a matter of armies. Armies had been tried. The general had met the Scarlet Rot at some hour of the Shattering — the rot that unmakes from within, the slow red sister of everything I fled — and it had taken his mind and left his might. What held the southern wastes of Caelid was no longer my brother. It was my brother's *strength*, riderless, in my brother's body, wandering a battlefield it could not remember winning, eating — I will say this once, plainly, as the reports said it to me, because pity dressed in euphemism is contempt — eating the dead of his own old wars. The mightiest demigod of the age, reduced to weather with a name. And still — attend, for this is Radahn entire — still holding the stars. Mad, rotted, riderless, and the discipline of that one impossible arrest *held*. They call it his scourge-name's last proof. I call it what a sister may: the man had one grip left, and he spent it on his word.

Whatever his word was. To whom. I told thee: three versions, and no answer, and none coming now.

Nor did the design's threads run only through my household, and honesty compels the tangent. Thou wast steered toward that festival by more hands than mine. There was a sorceress — thou hast told me pieces; exiled from the Academy for hungers I will not adjudicate here, keeping her school in a ruin's cellar — who urged thee south to the Radahn Festival for reasons that were entirely her own: the stars, love. She was of the old persuasion, star-readers before moon-courtiers, and she wanted the sky unlocked as badly as I did, for purposes that had nothing to do with me and everything to do with fate's frozen ledger. I note her for a reason of record: the general's death was not one witch's requirement. Half the thinking world had business with that locked sky — the fate-starved, the star-taught, the merely trapped — and when the age finally sent its champions to Redmane Castle, they came carrying a dozen different hopes to the same grim errand. Mine was merely the hope with a wolf in it. History will braid all those threads into my design because my design won; that is how history flatters victors, by crediting them with everyone's rope. The record here stands corrected: I did not own that festival. I only needed it.

Then the Festival.

I had no hand in its making — set that down, for the chroniclers braid it otherwise. The Radahn Festival was Redmane work, his own war-children's mercy: the last soldiery of his following, penned in their castle by the rot's spread, resolved that their general should end as he had lived — in combat, against champions, under banners — rather than rot standing in a wasteland. An executioner's carnival. A funeral wearing armor. Their witch-hunter herald Jerren cried it across the south, and the age's blades answered, and among them — my design's whole weight riding in their saddlebags, unannounced — went a Tarnished of no house, and a wolf in black armor who had asked me for leave to fight beside thee. He put it as a soldier puts things: that the household's errand wanted seeing through. He meant — thou knewest him; thou needest no gloss of mine — he meant: *don't you go dying on me. For Ranni's sake too.*

I stayed in my tower. There is no verse of the ballads that puts me at that festival, and for once the ballads and the truth hold hands. I stayed, and I did the thing I am built for: I waited, awake — I had rationed the tide of sleep for weeks against that day — watching the southern sky through my smith's best glass.

Of the fight itself I will tell nothing. I was not there; thou wast; the scar on thy shoulder was there. It is thine to tell, some watch of this voyage when I ask thee — and I will ask thee, for I have heard every account but the one that matters.

What I saw was the sky.

I saw the stars *move*. Thou must grant me this paragraph, love; I have earned it across eleven movements of keeping my sentences cold. I stood at a tower window I had stood at for a hundred stalled years, and between one breath I do not take and the next, the oldest lock in my life's whole architecture opened. The fixed heaven shivered. The courses resumed. Fate's ledger, seized mid-entry the age long, lurched and began again to write — I felt my own line of light take up its interrupted sentence, and every reader and Finger and fate-crone in the Lands Between felt it with me, and knew the general was down. And out of the loosened dark, one star fell. South and east it went down, trailing its long bright verdict, and struck the Mistwood, and opened the earth — a wound, from above, exactly as the riddle had priced it. Nokron stood open. The door my brother's grip had held shut for a lifetime was open, and the key had been falling, arrested mid-fall, the whole age of his holding.

He had been holding back my future with both hands, and I will never know if he knew, and the not-knowing is his memorial in me. The stars, or the general. The heavens, in the end, did not make me choose. They made thee do it.

I have thanked thee. I do not thank them.

Now the crack. I promised thee, in the movement of my household, that my griefs would be shown as well as priced. Here is the only one I have to show for Radahn, and it is small, and it is the truest thing in this movement.

When the reports were all in — Blaidd home limping and proud; Iji's summary in its flat smith's hand; thy own account, which thou gavest me standing, sparely, like a soldier, though thou wast neither then nor ever mine to command so — when the tower was quiet again, I found among Iji's papers one line he had marked as a soldier's report, not fact. The clearing-crews said the general's old warhorse had been found beneath him after the field went still. True, embroidered, or only the mercy Redmanes needed their last image to contain, I cannot tell thee. I can tell thee that he rode the creature to his final battle. Rotted, mad, memoryless, a mountain of ruin on legs — and some deep unrotted stubbornness in him had gone on refusing, to the end of everything, to put his whole weight down.

I read that line, love, and I — who did not weep for the Night, nor for my flesh, nor for my mother's broken library — I set down the paper, and I was for some while unfit for state business.

He learned the bending of the world's very pull, when we were young, so that he need never abandon a beloved and unsuitable steed. Every power he ever took up after, he took up to that same measure: that nothing he loved should be crushed by mere bigness. The heavens gave that boy their stars to carry. I sent my design against him and it was right and I would send it again. Both truths, held. That is the house I am.

The horse's name I never learned. No shipped chronicle keeps it; perhaps none was ever set down; the age recorded my brother's every conquest and could not spare four letters for the one creature he arranged the world's own gravity around. So let this account keep what the chronicles would not — not a name, but the fact of him: that he was loved past all proportion, and was carried, and carried back, to the end.

The sky was open. The city was open. The design, after an age in irons, was *moving* — and thou wast already riding for the Mistwood crater with my wolf's blessing and my smith's new-forged edge on thy blade.

Southward behind thee, in a castle of mourning banners, they were building my brother's cairn. Below thee, in a dark that had waited longer than Caria has existed, the false stars of the banished were wheeling over the vaults of the one treasure I had built half a century of treason to reach.

Bring me the blade, I had said.

Thou didst bring me so much more than the blade, and the next movement must count it all.
