## VI. A Knife Made for Gods

Give me thy hands a moment. Not for tenderness — hold them out.

These hands have carried, unless I am misinformed, a slab of rock engraved with certain black traces, found far away in a storm-wracked ruin, and used — cleverly; I heard, and was proud, and said nothing — to turn the death-blade of a god's own beast back upon him. Thou didst wonder, surely, whose contingency thou hadst inherited. Whose foresight had left a counter-key to Death lying in the world for a stranger to find.

Mine. Bought on the very night I am about to spend. So thou seest this movement is not history to thee, my dear one. Thou hast already held its receipts.

Begin with the vault.

I told thee the Order held Death itself under lock. Now take the sentence apart, as I had to. When the golden law was raised — before my mother's moon, before Caria, before every living quarrel — its architects did one thing more audacious than all their planting and crowning together: they removed Death from the world's rule. Cut Destined Death out of the great Ring of law entire, so that the age of the Erdtree should be an age where nothing golden truly dies. And a rune of such power cannot be burned or buried; it can only be *kept*. So they made a keeper.

Maliketh. Mark the name; I say it with more respect than I say most gods'. A beast — the Black Blade, death's own edge given fur and fidelity — and, attend now, thou wilt know the shape of this: a *shadowbound* beast. Given to an Empyrean, as shadows are. Given to Marika.

Yes. The same instrument. The same grammar of gift and surety that sewed a wolf to my childhood sewed that great dark warden to the queen of everything, and the queen's sole use for her shadow — the record says it plainer than I ever could — was as a vessel: a living lock, to keep Destined Death where no hand could reach it. And the record adds, in the flat voice records keep for their worst sentences: *even then, she betrayed him.*

I robbed a jailer who was also a prisoner. I have never pretended otherwise. When I weigh my sins — and I do weigh them; this voyage is long and the scales travel with me — I set Maliketh's betrayed fidelity on the pan with the rest. He guarded truly. He was spent by his own mistress like coin. And a witch out of Caria came, in the shelter of a chained sky, to take from his keeping the one thing he existed to keep.

How?

No. Sit back down in thy patience; I watched thee lean forward. This door I do not point at and pass — this door I hold shut with both painted hands, and I will tell thee why, because my refusals in this testament come with their reasons or they come not at all. The world contains, at this hour, exactly one successful method for robbing Death, and it resides in one head, and the head is speaking to thee. Maliketh — poor faithful ruin — took the remnant of his charge, after my visit, and bound it inside his own flesh, that none might ever rob Death again. He learned the right lesson too late. I will not unlearn it for the record. Some knowledge is not made safer by being confessed to a spouse; it is made *pluraler*. A fragment was stolen. The word the histories chose is *someone*. Let someone stand. She has broad shoulders.

What I will give thee is the price of the how. It cost me years. It cost me a debt to persons I will come to presently. And it cost me — this I had not budgeted — the last of a certain innocence about my own reflection: for I found, when the fragment lay at last in my two mortal hands, cold past all my crone's chill and heavier than its size by the weight of everything it ended, that my first feeling was neither triumph nor terror.

It was recognition. As in my mother's garden. Something that regarded me, and asked nothing, and was not warm.

I have wondered since whether Death and my moon are kin. File that under a wife's speculations. It is the only unpaid-for sentence in this movement.

Now the forging. This I did, and this I will tell.

A fragment of the Rune of Death is not a weapon. It is a *jurisdiction* — a small portable province of ending, in a world whose law had banished ending. To make it kill, and kill what I needed killed — not men; men die of embarrassment; I required a blade that could find the life in a *demigod*, in flesh underwritten by the Erdtree itself — it must be worked. Divided. Bound into steel by a rite that law never anticipated, because law does not anticipate its own stolen organs.

I performed that rite. Write it in whatever hand the record prefers: the fearsome rite was mine. I took the fragment of Death and I imbued its power into knives — misshapen things, the smiths would call them, for they were shaped to no living smithing tradition; their geometry answered requirements I had derived, not inherited. Black knives. Godslaying knives, if the god's flesh could be reached. I worked in the dark my brother's arrested sky held open, with my smith's mirrors watching the watchers, and when it was done there lay on my table the only objects in creation the Golden Order could not survive an honest inventory of.

I remember standing over them a long time. Not in horror — I will not counterfeit a shudder for thy comfort; I was proud, with the whole cold pride of a made thing well made. But standing, and counting. There were more knives than one.

That is the last plain sentence I will spend on the arithmetic. Thou hast leave to count.

Now the hands that would hold them. Attend to my grammar in what follows. I have chosen every case-ending like a locksmith.

There exists an order of women — silver-armored under night-colored cloaks, sisters of a craft older than my treason — whose blood runs from the Eternal City below, and whose lineage, the whisperers add (I neither join the whisper nor hush it), stood *close to the queen of everything herself*. Numen. Old blood; outer blood; the blood of Marika's own far country. How such women and my conspiracy came into one another's orbit; who reached first; what was promised, and by whom, and to whom — thou feelest the doors closing as I speak, and I will not apologize. These are not my secrets alone. That is the difference, my dear one, between the door I hold shut with both hands and these: the forging was mine to tell. The alliance has other heirs.

Yet I will not leave the sisters as a grammar lesson, for I dealt with what I dealt with, and thou shouldst know the caliber of it. Their craft was older than my treason and better than my nerves. Armor of scale forged to make no sound — I have stood beside one at arm's reach, in a room I will not locate for thee, and heard nothing, *nothing*, not cloth, not breath, only my own heart being informative — and over the silver, cloaks the color of the idea of night. They did not speak unless speech was the errand. They did not look at me the way the age looked at me, as princess or prize or heresy; they looked at me the way surgeons look at a procedure. Whatever else is unresolved in their chapter — and I have told thee how much — resolve this: they were not hirelings. Hirelings can be bought off, and therefore priced, and therefore safe. These were believers, love, of some old and patient faith whose articles I was never shown, and a believer with a silent blade is the one instrument in creation that cuts entirely along its own grain. I supplied an edge that matched their grain. That is the confession hiding in this paragraph, and I will not dig it deeper: not *I commanded*, and not *I was commanded*. We coincided. Heaven itself could not subpoena a cleaner word.

What I will set on the record is this. The knives I forged did not stay on my table. They went into the keeping of those women. And the rite that made each blade a province of ending answered, thereafter, to purposes I will describe in the next movement with the exactness of a woman describing her own wound — for one of those purposes, thou knowest already, was carved into me.

Whose hand lent them, whose hand aimed them — I leave where the record leaves it. The record, I have found, has better lawyers than I.

And last — the insurance. Thou hast held it; I promised its provenance.

A conspirator who steals from a vault leaves a very particular creditor behind: the vault's keeper. I had robbed the Black Blade himself, and whatever else my rite might loose upon the world, one consequence stood certain as winter — some day, in some age, Maliketh, or what Maliketh serves, would come to collect. Against that day I wanted a counter-key: the stolen jurisdiction's traces, worked into a form that could *refuse* the death-blade — turn its own province back against it, a mirror for an edge as Iji's helm was a mirror for an eye.

I did not keep it myself. Study the choice; it is the most legible thing I did that year. A contingency in the plotter's own pocket dies with the plotter's first search. It wanted a keeper of my blood, inside the walls of power, with the nerve to hold damnation in a desk — and precisely no curiosity about its meaning. I have told thee of my second brother, and his single question. *What does it require?* On the night of the dire plot — the record's phrase, and for once the record and I agree on an adjective — I went to Rykard, and I rewarded him, says the same record, with those traces of the Rune of Death. Rewarded. I have never contested the verb, and I invite thee to notice what it concedes: that he had done something for me worth rewarding. There is a door for thee, set ajar. It is as far ajar as it goes.

He asked what it required. I told him: a drawer, and silence. He supplied both for an age, and the claw crossed the world by roads neither of us drew, and came at last — thou seest the shape of my faith in contingency — into the hands of the one person who would stand before Maliketh in the hour Death finally came home. I planned for a collector. I never planned thee. I have given up being embarrassed by which of my provisions the world chose to honor.

So closed the year of the forging. Count what stood ready, that last evening, in the shelter of the chained sky: knives of ending, in the keeping of silver-armored sisters; a counter-key, in a praetor's drawer; a smith in a mirror; a shadow, loved and untested; and a candidate the heavens still believed was ripening toward her crown.

Every blade I forged that year had two edges, my dear one.

I have only ever admitted to one. The other is the next movement, and I will not blunt it for thee.
