## VII. Two Half-Deaths

Thou askedst me once. Do not soften it now with thy tact; I keep accurate books. On the stair below the Divine Tower, with my first body's weather still on thy cloak, thou didst ask — not as Rogier asked, hunting a culprit, and not as the Order would ask, hunting a heretic. Thou askedst the way one asks a locked house about a light in its window.

*Did you choose him?*

I did not answer thee then. I made thee climb a while longer, and thou didst let me, which is one of the reasons this ring is on my hand. But a testament that walks past that question is a forgery, whatever else is true in it. So: this movement. The Night, whole. Both deaths, in their order. And at the center of it, the answer I will actually give thee, which is not the answer, and which I will not pretend is.

The Night, then.

I was not in the capital. Set that stone down first, for the ballads have me on every rooftop of Leyndell that evening, a silhouette against the gold, and it is theater. A woman whose own death is on the docket does not travel to watch the other entries. I was in Caria, in my tower, with the instruments of my part arranged before me and my smith's mirrors angled at the sky, and what I knew of the capital that night I knew the way the rite knew it — along the blades. Thou wilt understand shortly.

Somewhere in the golden city, silver-armored women moved through Marika's own halls with my knives under their night-colored cloaks. How they passed the wards of the most guarded palace under heaven; whose feet knew those corridors; whose word stilled which sentries — doors, my dear one. Doors with other heirs. I have named the women's blood already, and whose country it hailed from, and I watch thee do the arithmetic every time, and every time thou art too wise to say the sum aloud. Keep being too wise. The sum has no honest total — only a remainder, and the remainder is this: my knives did not walk themselves.

And in the deep of that night, one of those women — the record, which is better at names than mercy, calls her Tiche — put her black knife into Godwyn the Golden, first of the demigods, the Erdtree's fairest son. And the province of ending I had bound into the steel did its work.

Half of its work.

For the rite had two subjects. Now I will tell thee the second, and I will tell it in the first person, because it is the one death in all this history I am qualified to narrate from inside.

At the hour appointed — and I had appointed it; my own ending is the one entry in this ledger with my signature in the *ordering* column, uncontested, mine — I lay down in my tower, and I turned the rite's remaining edge upon the flesh I was born into.

I slew mine own flesh. Thou hast had the sentence since the prologue; here is its interior.

It was not violent. I had built better than violence. It was — cold, first: my crone's cold, the discipline, arriving not as sensation but as *procedure*, the body's ambitions cracking off it one by one like ice freeing a vessel's shape. I felt the Order's claims go. That is the part no scripture prepared me for — that dying, done correctly, is administrative. I felt grace's little rivers reverse and withdraw. I felt fate — stalled, nailed, my brother's whole arrested sky of it — lose its grip-point, the line of light that was written *Ranni* going slack in the readers' dark. I felt my mother's gift of body, my father's iron in the blood, the heavens' entire investment in a candidate, all of it discover, in one long frostfall, that the account had been emptied from inside.

And at the very last, on the far sill of it, something regarded me. I have promised thee no guesses beyond the one I flagged. I record the regard, and that it was not warm, and that I did not look away. I have never once looked away. It is my sole consistency.

Then I was small, and painted, and breathing nothing, in a shape kept for me three winters and a bequest ago — but that is the next movement's country. Hold the Night a while yet.

Two deaths, one rite, one instant. Now hear what the instant did, for this is carved where any pilgrim can read it, and thou hast stood over the carving.

When a demigod first dies in an age built to forbid it, the event leaves a mark — a cursemark, a wound in the law itself, and it should have taken, the scholars agree, the shape of a circle. One death; one seal; a wheel entire. It did not. Two demigods perished in the same breath — flesh here, soul there — and the mark that should have been whole broke into two half-wheels: one carved into my discarded body atop its tower; one into the prince below the earth. Ranni the Witch was the first of the demigods whose flesh perished. The Prince of Death perished in soul alone.

Sit with the grammar of that sentence, as I have sat. *In soul alone.* My half-death left a corpse and freed a will: the woman continued, the flesh did not. His was the mirror, and the mirror is an atrocity: the flesh continued. The soul did not. What lay in state under the Erdtree's gold, mourned by the age, was not a dead prince. It was a prince's body from which the prince had been subtracted — living remains, deathless and unmanned, golden and vacant, laid in root-riddled dark while the thing that had made him *him* went wherever my knives' province sent it.

I built the rite that made both halves possible. The wheel broke even around the instant I appointed; whether every weight upon it was mine to place is the door I have not opened.

Now the question. Thou seest I have walked the whole Night and left one room dark, and thou knowest which, and I said I would not pretend.

*Did I choose him?* Did the rite require a golden prince — his particular death, his particular half of my wheel? Did I name Godwyn to the silver sisters; did another name him and I consent; did I know whose soul my ending would be paired with when I lay down under my own knife; or did I lie down truly ignorant, one edge honest, and learn the second name as the world learned it, with the morning?

Here is my answer. Attend, for I will say it once, and the saying is the center of this testament.

I stole Death. I forged its fragment into knives that could find a demigod's life. I gave those knives into hands that had purposes of their own. I appointed my own ending to the same night — the same *instant* — and a woman who balances a wheel that finely does not get to plead surprise at what the other half weighed. This much the record holds, and I hold it with the record.

But what I believed those knives would find — what I knew, what I chose, what I permitted myself not to know, in that country between choosing and knowing where the worst of all verdicts live — *that, I keep.*

Not because it acquits me. Hear this with both thy ears, thou who followed me past a written warning: I keep it because it does not. There is no version of that room, lit, that leaves me cleaner than the door does. If I opened it and thou foundest ignorance, thou wouldst forgive me — and the forgiveness would be false coin, for ignorance at that altitude is a thing a woman *builds*, timber by timber, and I was the age's finest carpenter. And if thou foundest knowledge — thou wouldst stay; I have learned what thou art; thou stayedst past rot and void and my own cold warnings. Thou wouldst stay, and carry it, the length of a thousand years.

I will not put it on thee. It is the one freight of this voyage I did not invite thee to share. Every leash in this history, I cut. This weight I *chose*, and choosing it is the nearest thing to expiation I have ever permitted myself: that the question of Godwyn the Golden sleeps in exactly one keeping, mine, whole, unhalved — the only unbroken wheel I own.

Thou didst ask on the stair. I answer on the sea. The light in the locked house is lit, love. It will not be shown. Judge me with it burning.

What remains of the Night is epilogue, and belongs to the world's books more than mine, so I will give it as the world received it.

The capital woke to its first dead god. An age built on deathlessness looked into a bier and found the concept *ending* returned to it like an exile walking home out of the fog. The queen of everything — and here I state sequence, not cause; I was a corpse and a doll that season, and even undiscarded I could not have read that woman — the queen of everything, in an hour no chronicle dares narrate from inside, raised the hammer of her own far country and shattered the Ring of law itself. Her champion-self failed to mend it. The Rune fractured; her children took up the shards; and the wars of the Shattering began, in which every love I have named thee so far — brother, brother, mother — was in time fed to the same fire my Night had lit the long fuse of.

Of the mourning I will keep one artifact only, and I keep it because it is the one that has never once let me go. Among the swords made to commemorate the dead prince — and the gold made many; grief was the one commodity that age still minted honestly — there is a blade of remembrance into which was worked the prayer of a child. A young boy's prayer, humble, the smiths recorded, and it runs: *O brother, lord brother, please die a true death.*

Read it again. The age's whole enormous lament, love, and the only voice in it that understood what had actually happened was a child's. The princes and priests mourned a death. The boy alone mourned the *absence* of one — saw that what my rite had left in the bier was crueler than ending, and asked, with a child's terrible directness, for the mercy the adults had built an entire cosmology to forbid. Please die. Please be *able* to die. I have held many verdicts against my Night — Rogier's, the Order's, thine unspoken one, mine. That prayer is the only one I have no answer to. It asks nothing of me, accuses nothing, does not know I exist. It simply wants, purely, the one thing my balanced wheel made impossible — and a woman can armor herself against every accusation in the world, love, except a child asking heaven, politely, for her cruelty to be undone.

Whose child, the record hints and I will not. It does not matter to the wound.

The silver sisters scattered from Leyndell with the dawn behind them. One I have named died in the flight, shielding her mother, which I record without comment because there is no comment I have the standing to make. And under the golden city, in the root-veined dark, a soulless body began, very slowly, to disagree with the law that would not let it die. The disagreement would spread. Thou hast fought what it became. That bill arrives in a later movement; it is the one that never stops arriving.

Two half-deaths, then. One was mine to give, and I gave it, and I have never once wanted it back. The other—

The other, the world is still paying. And so, in the coin I told thee of, am I.
