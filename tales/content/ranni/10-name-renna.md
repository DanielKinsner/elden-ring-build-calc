## X. The Name Renna

What didst thou see, that night at the ruined church? I have never asked thee outright, and we have been married a war and a world's ending. Tell me some watch of this voyage. I hold my own account of the meeting, and I distrust it, because I have noticed a thing about first meetings: each party remembers the other arriving, and no one remembers arriving themselves.

Here is mine. Correct it when thou wilt. I promise to believe thee and record myself.

I came to the Church of Elleh by night, on business I had appointed before I ever heard thy name. Mark that; thy chroniclers will want our meeting fated, and I will not have it. Fate and I were estranged in those years by my own hand. What brought me down from my towers into Limgrave was an errand — a bequest to discharge — and thou wast its address, not its cause.

I did not come as Ranni. Ranni was a corpse on a tower and a rumor in a treason; Ranni did not travel. I came wrapped in the name I told thee of, the name with no owner — Renna — and in the look of a wandering witch: a hat, a season's dust, an old woman's easy stoop on a broken wall. Thou camest to the fire, dying-fresh, with the fog's grey still on thee, and I watched thee from the wall the way I watch everything, and thou —

Thou didst nod. To a strange crone perched in a ruin at midnight, as to a neighbor. I have unmade gods, my dear one, and I have been unsettled by exactly one greeting.

The business, then. I said to thee — and I have kept my phrasing, for I drafted it with more care than thou couldst have known — that I had been entrusted with a gift, for thee. By Torrent's former master. A bell, for the calling of spirits from ash unreturned to the Erdtree; and with it, to prove the bell's worth, the ashes of three wolves.

Every word of that sentence was true. Attend to the craft of it anyway. I told thee *what* without one syllable of *whence*: a former master, unnamed, unsexed, undated; a trust, unexplained; a witch, mislabeled. Thou didst take the bell — thou didst thank me, which the record should keep; courtesy from the dying is character, not manners — and thou didst not ask the question the sentence was built to survive.

Ask it now. Thou hast earned it, and this movement exists to answer what I can.

*Who was Torrent's former master?*

And here, love, I do a thing I have not done in nine movements: I leave a door exactly as I found it.

The bequest came to me with one name omitted: *a former master*. Whether that omission was a trust, a caution, or merely the shape of the message I received, I cannot improve upon it without inventing certainty. Thou hast seen what certainty does in the hands of those who want a history to close. I will not do their work for them.

So the door stays shut, and understand the shutting: not as proof that I guard a secret, and not as proof that no secret was given me. The honest record ends where the sentence ended. Thy steed's first rider had a life before thee. Its name is not mine to supply.

The wolves deserve their sentence too, before the horse has his. With the bell I gave thee ashes — the remains of three lone wolves, spirits that yet dwelt in what was left of them, ready to answer a summons and fight a stranger's battles for the length of a memory. Thou hast called them up a hundred times since and perhaps never once asked where such ashes come from. These came from wolves cast out — chased from their pack, the keepers of such remains record, who later found a nameless Tarnished that welcomed them as hunting companions. Outcasts, love, taken up by an outcast, faithful past death to the one soul who had wanted them. I did not choose that gift idly, and I did not choose it only for its power. I have never once given anyone an object that was not also a sentence, and the sentence in the ashes was this: *the discarded make the best companions. Ask me how I know.* Thou couldst not read it then. Read it now, on our ship crewed entirely by the discarded — a cast-off princess, a graveyard Tarnished, and somewhere in the hold of memory, three wolves nobody wanted. My whole order, love, assembled from what the gold threw away.

Now Torrent himself, since I have thee at his subject.

I watched him choose thee that night — for that is what it was; the whistle obeys the rider, but the *steed* obeys nothing he has not elected, as thou hast learned at cliff- edges since. He stood off in the dark past the fire in the way of his kind, more weather than animal, and he took thy measure with those old, old eyes, and then he was simply with thee, the way rain is simply falling: no ceremony, all verdict. I know only one other judge so quick and so seldom wrong.

I rode nothing to that meeting. A doll travels by arts I will not bore thee with. But I will confess into this record a small cold covetousness, watched from a broken wall: that spirit-steeds are permitted to choose their people, cleanly, once — and witches are not. We are chosen at, endlessly. By moons, by Fingers, by fates, by histories. The horse has the better constitution.

And one more thing moved through the Lands Between in those years, and this movement is its only place in my testament, so attend, and then let it lie.

There was another woman abroad. I never met her. Set that first and last: no meeting, no message, no compact — the chroniclers who wed our stories at their conjunction of convenience may take their trade elsewhere. But even a woman keeping to towers heard traces of her passage: a burnt-handed girl who walked with no body worth the name, one eye sealed — by whose will no honest record says — keeping company with a purpose received from her mother and not yet read to its end. Thou knowest of whom I speak. Thou didst travel beside her the length of a land, and I never once asked thee of it. What I know of her road, I learned from the marks it left and the silence with which thou carriedst it.

What was she to me? Nothing, by every record either of us will ever permit. A stranger on a parallel road. And yet — I am correcting the books, so let me correct my own — twice in those years I dreamt of a woman burning, and woke, and could not say whose face I had watched it happen to. Hers. Mine. There are shapes the heavens cut more than once, my dear one, when a design wants testing: the sealed eye, the discarded body, the purpose issued at birth like a uniform. She wore hers toward the fire. I wore mine toward the cold. If we had ever spoken — we did not, and would not; two mirrors facing each other show only a corridor, and neither of us had the constitution for corridors — if we had spoken, I do not know whether we would have clasped hands or drawn knives.

I know only this, and I set it down and shut the subject: when word reached me, late and by strange roads, of what she chose at the end of her road — thou wast there; I will not paraphrase thy grief — I sat a long time with my drawn-shutter eye toward the south, and what I felt was not triumph that my way had spared me hers, and was not pity, which she would have returned unopened.

It was recognition. Third of the three in this testament. I have told thee of the other two.

One near-miss belongs in this movement's record, because I have never stopped turning it over. The meeting almost did not happen. Thou wast under no obligation to rest at that church at that hour; the bell, had I missed thee, would have found its way to thee by duller roads — such objects manage; the world has channels for what must be delivered. And I would have discharged the bequest and gone home to my towers and my nailed sky, and thou wouldst have been, to me, a name in Blaidd's reports: the promising Tarnished, employed in due course, used well, paid fairly, and never once seen at midnight being courteous to a nobody. The design would have run regardless, love — that is the cold fact. It did not need the church. It needed the blade, the bearer, the fate; the nod was surplus to every requirement I had drawn.

And yet I cannot make the history run without it. I have tried, in the idle arithmetic of long watches: subtract the church, keep all else — the service, the festival, the city, the ring — and ask whether the woman at Manus Celes says *dear*. I do not believe she does. I believe she says *fair*, and means it, and sails alone, as designed. Every load-bearing stone of my life I quarried and set myself; I have told thee so for nineteen movements, and it is true. But the keystone, love — the keystone was a nod at midnight that no design of mine requested and no ledger of mine can price. Sovereigns hate to learn what I am about to write. Write it anyway: the plan accounted for everything except the hour it was seen through, kindly, by accident. That hour governs all the rest.

Why the disguise, thou wilt ask last — why Renna at all, for one bell and one night?

Three reasons, in descending order of the truth's comfort. Because a treason's first law is that Ranni does not appear; that is the reason of state. Because a bequest deserved discharging in a shape that could walk away from questions; that is the reason of honor.

And because — here is the cold one, love, and then this movement is done — because I wanted to see thee before thou couldst know to perform for me. Everyone the age would send thee thereafter came *announced*: maidens, Fingers, lords, witches, all trailing their titles like wedding-trains, all met by the Tarnished's public face. I came as a crone on a wall, and got the private one. One nod, to a nobody, at midnight, from a soul with no reason left for courtesy. I have made lords of less evidence. I would again.

I told thee we would likely not meet again. It was the worst forecast of a long career, and I have never been gladder to be bad at my work.
