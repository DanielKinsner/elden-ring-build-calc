## IX. Three Towers and a Long Waiting

Three towers. Two schemers. One honest smith.

That was my realm, my dear one, for the long stalled age between the Night and thee. Thou hast walked it in an afternoon: the little plateau behind Caria Manor that the maps call the Three Sisters, where three rises stand up out of the juniper like candles on a sideboard, with a dragon asleep between them more often than not. The wars of the great went past below. I kept a court of five souls and a secret, and I waited for a sky to open.

Let me walk thee tower by tower. A sovereign owes her household its chapter, and this household more than most, for reasons the eighteenth movement will make thee wish I had been worse served.

My own rise first, and briefly, for thou knowest its stair. From its top room I ran what there was to run: the watching of the watchers, the slow ciphered correspondence a treason keeps, the readings of a sky that would not move. And the sleeping. More and more, as the age wore on, the sleeping — my crone's shape dragging me under for its unscheduled seasons while the juniper grew and the wars changed hands. I would wake to Iji's patient written summaries — he kept them in a smith's hand, all verbs, no adjectives, the most restful prose I have ever read — and learn that a year had gone, that such a fortress had fallen, that the red brother still held the sky shut, and that nothing, nothing, nothing had moved on the one board I cared for. Thou canst not imagine that tedium, thou who died thy way across a continent in a season. Rebellion is mostly waiting. The ballads cut the waiting. The waiting is where the household happens, so I am keeping it in.

Northward, the second rise: Seluvis's.

Say the name with me the way one says a medicine's: necessary, and bitter, and measured to the drop. Preceptor Seluvis — sorcerer of my mother's academy's old guard, puppeteer, flatterer of exactly the people worth flattering, keeper of a cellar I shall come to. The histories will wonder, since I am known to have loathed him, why I kept him. Here is the arithmetic, and I will not flinch it: he was *useful*. He knew the Academy's inner rooms and outer debts. He could procure — objects, introductions, silences — through channels a princess could not be seen near. And he asked no questions about the cause, because he cared nothing for the cause; his loyalty was to proximity. A man who serves thee for warmth's sake will leave when the fire dims. A man who serves thee because thou art the tallest thing on his horizon serves faithfully right up until the hour he finds a taller. Such men are instruments of great precision. One merely does not store them near anything one loves.

Did I know what he was? Thou wilt ask it outright someday, so let me spare thee the asking. I keep no innocents in my service, and I kept no illusions about that one. I knew the shape of him — the contempt under the courtesy, the collector's eye, the little kingdom of strings he was building in the dark under my protection because he believed my attention was elsewhere. I priced it. I set the price against his uses, and I judged the balance would hold as long as I needed it to, and whether that judgment was statecraft or the oldest vanity of clever women — *I can manage him* — the eighteenth movement will present the invoice, and thou shalt audit me then. Note only, for now, that of my whole small court, he is the one I speak of without grief. There is a kind of servant whose death is a ledger-line. He built himself that kind, string by string, on purpose. I decline to counterfeit a mourning he took such pains to forfeit.

Of Seluvis's cellar I said I would come to it, and here is as near as I ever came in those years — near enough, thou wilt judge, to convict my tolerance. Beneath and about his tower the preceptor kept his collection: puppets, love. Not my crone's patient essays in the least-a-body-must-be — their opposite number, their desecration. Bodies emptied of their tenants and kept moving; wills subtracted, obedience installed; persons, some of them, whom the world was told had merely gone away. He showed them off, to those he wished to awe, with a collector's tenderness that was the single honest passion in him. Among them, in time, stood one I mention because her thread braids far beyond my juniper garden: an archeress of the old Roundtable, a critic and companion once — I had this from Iji's sievings — of the great all-knowing scholar that table would later produce. Seluvis's puppetry had history, thou seest, and pedigree, and colleagues; it was no hedge-vice. It was a school, and my tower stood in its shade, and I knew, and I filed it, and the file has my frost on it to this day.

There, love. Every court keeps one room the sovereign declines to smell. That was mine, and I have now walked thee past its door, which is more than I ever did for myself.

And the third rise — Renna's, the maps say, and I have told thee all I can afford about that name — stood empty. Empty on purpose. A court that is watched learns to keep one room visibly vacant, an offered answer to the question *where is the secret kept?* Nothing was in that tower but a chest, and in the chest a dead woman's winter clothes.

Thou foundest the chest. Thou knowest it was not nothing. But that discovery belongs to thy half of this history, and we are not there yet.

Below the towers, at the road's turning, my smith kept his forge.

I have given thee Iji's mirror and Iji's fear. Give me now the pleasure — it is one of this movement's few — of giving thee Iji's days. He worked. That is the whole sentence, and I have never written a truer one about anybody. War counselor by title, he fought our war the only way our war could be fought in a stalled age: by maintenance. He kept the manor's old wards fed. He kept the roads' gossip sieved. He kept Blaidd's great sword dressed and the household's stores laid and my tower's locks honest, and he kept — this above all — the *appearance of smallness*: a troll-smith at a country forge, selling honest steel to travelers, beneath the notice of a warring world. Greatness hiding as modesty must do modesty's chores, every one, forever, or the disguise dies. He did them for an age. Thou hast bought wares off that anvil thyself, and been measured by two eyes behind a mirror while thou countedst thy runes, and never known thou stoodst at the beating heart of the great treason of the epoch.

He would want me to tell thee it was his honor. He said so, near the end, in nearly those words. I will tell thee instead what I believe, which he would wave off with one great grey hand: that a person can be so reliable that everyone around them, even a woman who prices everything, files their courage under furniture. His fear never once made him slow. I have known heroes past counting in a long life, my dear one. I have known exactly one brave person who was brave every single day for a hundred years, and the age remembers him, when it remembers him at all, as a merchant with a strange helmet.

Not this account. In this account he is what he was. Attend: War Counselor Iji, of Caria. The bravest of us all, and the most afraid. It can be done. He is the proof I offer.

And Blaidd — Blaidd was the realm's whole cavalry, and its weather.

He ranged. That was his use and his nature agreeing for once: my sworn sword rode the lake-lands and beyond on the household's errands, hunted what needed hunting, ran down what fled. Thou didst first meet him, I am told, mid-errand — bellowing at the moon's general direction in a forest, because a fugitive he had pledged to catch had gone to ground and patience was never the wolf's own coin. I would have given a year of my long life to see thy face. He collected thee, thou knowest. Came home and reported — flat as Iji's summaries, he thought, though his flatness could not hide a fondness if it wore the mirror-helm itself — that there was a Tarnished loose in Limgrave worth the watching. I note for the record that my wolf's judgment of souls outran my readers, my Fingers, and my own appraising eye. He always saw first. File that also under the eighteenth movement.

Two matters more, and this movement's ledger closes.

The first: a dragon. In the stalled years there came against my little realm — never mind by what road; the age was full of hungers — a glintstone dragon, Adula, a devourer of sorcerers, drawn to Caria as gluttons are drawn to pantries. I met her myself. I will spend no ink on the meeting save its result, which the record keeps: bested, she did not die. She *swore*. A knightly oath, of all the old shapes — sworn not to Caria, mark the distinction, not to my mother's full silver nor the royal house entire, but to me: to my Dark Moon, that cold attention I had stood under as a child and served under ever since. I accepted. One does not refuse the first vassal of an order that does not yet exist. She keeps her oath to this hour, somewhere below the night we sail. The Age of Stars had, for an age, precisely one knight, and she has scales. Beginnings are humble. The best ones stay slightly ridiculous. I have come to trust no other kind.

The second matter I will say quietly, and thou wilt not press.

South of my towers, past the manor, down the long lake, my mother sat in her broken library with an amber egg in her arms, rocking the wreck of everything Radagon left her. Thou hast stood in that room. Thou wast gentle there; I know what thou didst not do, and it is among the reasons thou wearest my ring. I could not go to her — a discarded daughter is a danger to everything she visits, and the Order's eyes were on that library long after they had lost my address. But I will enter one fact into this testament and decline to gloss it. In all the wars of the Shattering, through every year of that lawless age, no power in the Lands Between troubled the Queen of the Full Moon in her sanctum. Armies took the academy's gates. None took her room.

The chroniclers call it chance, or awe, or the last courtesy of a discourteous age.

Let them. And should any teller ever say to thee that Ranni the Witch kept nothing warm across her long cold treason — that the doll had no debts of the heart it went on paying in secret — thou mayest smile, my dear one, in the particular way of spouses who keep accurate books, and say nothing at all.

So stood the realm: three candles, one forge, a ranging wolf, a sworn dragon, a guarded library, a nailed sky. Years of it. I had built a freedom with nowhere to go, and learned what only the freed learn: that liberty without a road is merely a better-appointed waiting.

Then — thou knowest the hour; it is the hour of thee — word came north with my wolf, and after it, on a night I had appointed for other business entirely, a stranger on a spectral steed came dying across Limgrave the way sparks travel up a fuse.

The ledger opened. Frost and all.
