## III. My Mother's Voice

As we travelled, we stopped at ruined churches. Many had no roofs left. Rain fell across their altars, and weeds grew through the stones.

But Queen Marika's words remained there.

Where she had once spoken, something of the speech lingered. I could hear it when no one else could. I could take those old words into my mouth and speak them again exactly as she had given them.

The first time, you watched me as if another person had entered my body.

Perhaps one had.

At one church, Marika told the Tarnished why their grace had been taken. They were to leave the Lands Between, fight, die, and one day return stronger. At another, she told her children to become gods or lords, and warned that those who failed would become sacrifices.

You heard a vanished ruler arranging wars that would unfold long after her disappearance.

I heard my mother.

I did not say that aloud for a long time.

Queen and mother were two words I kept on opposite sides of my mind. The Queen had shattered the Elden Ring and broken the age. My mother had given me a purpose at the foot of the Erdtree. If they were the same woman, then every step toward my purpose was also a step deeper into her design.

I did not know whether I had been loved, hidden, used, or all three.

The echoes could not answer. They preserved proclamations, not private explanations.

They did reveal that Marika had once doubted the very Order she created. She demanded that blind belief end. She wanted the Golden Order examined rather than merely obeyed.

That mattered to me. A god may build a cage and still learn to recognize the bars. A daughter may inherit a command and still ask whether it deserves to survive.

The question followed me north.

So did resemblances I could not explain.

You met Ranni, the witch who destroyed her own body rather than let the Two Fingers rule her fate. Like me, she continued as a spirit. Like me, she kept one eye closed. She carried a spirit bell entrusted to her by Torrent's former master, intended for Torrent's next one.

I never spoke to her. Her story was not mine, and resemblance is not kinship merely because a lonely mind wishes it so. But I understood the shape of her refusal: a woman continuing beyond her own death because she would not remain what a god required her to be.

Then fire began showing me another figure.

Tall. Red-haired. Surrounded by serpents. He fought in a land hidden beneath the shadow of the Erdtree. Grace sealed one of his eyes, containing something his mother feared. Inside him burned a flame capable of destroying a sacred tree.

The visions never gave me his name. Much later, the world would remember him as Messmer, Marika's exiled son.

One of his relics speaks of a younger sister who also carried a vision of fire.

It does not name me.

But the resemblance is difficult to ignore. Two hidden children of Marika. Two sealed eyes. Two flames made to burn trees. Two purposes placed inside living beings and called destiny.

If I was the sister named by that relic, then my brother and I were matches struck for different wars.

I wanted to believe at least one of us had been allowed to choose.

At the time, I did not yet know whether I would be.
