## VI. Kindling

The mountaintops are where the world stops pretending.

No more gold. Only white, and wind, and the bones of Giants making hills of themselves. The Forge waited above it all: a bowl of iron and stone holding the last ruinous flame at the top of the world.

Before we approached it, I said farewell to Torrent.

He had been my first friend. He had chosen you before I understood why, carried us across the entire Lands Between, and never once asked either of us to explain the parts of ourselves we could not name.

I pressed my forehead against his.

I thanked him for every road. I asked him to carry you through whatever came after me.

He breathed against my hands, which had no warmth, as if they did.

He knew. Steeds always know. It is people you have to tell twice.

At the Forge, I asked whether you were ready.

Every other time I touched you, I had asked permission. May I rest my hand upon you. Might I carry you to the Roundtable Hold. A dead woman minds her manners; borrowed hands borrow permission.

But there, at the end of everything, I reached for your hand and simply held it.

Your pulse was still there.

Stubborn. Mortal. Alive.

I thanked you for travelling with me. For the length of a world.

Then I stepped into the flame.

I will try to tell you what it is to burn, since I am the only narrator you will ever get on the subject.

It is not pain. Pain is a warning sent through flesh. I had only the memory of flesh.

The fire burned those memories instead.

It took the idea of my hands, then the long habit of a face. It found the vision of flame inside me and recognized it, flame meeting its portrait. I became heat, then light, then intention carried on the mountain wind.

I crossed the sky of the Lands Between as its own dawn.

And I took the Erdtree in my arms.

Gold burns gold, in the end.

The tree that had held its breath over a banked fire for an entire age finally exhaled. The whole world looked up from its little wars at once—every knight and beggar and abandoned god—to watch the sky's tree wear a crown of flame.

But fire alone did not finish the work. The thorns blackened and held. My burning opened the road to Destined Death. You still had to cross the storm beyond time, defeat Maliketh, and release the Rune of Death he guarded.

Only then could the burning become final. Only then could gods, trees, and ages end again.

That was my purpose. Not destruction for its own sake. An ending where the Golden Order had forbidden endings, so that something new could begin.

I had started our journey unable to remember the message I carried.

At the Forge, I wrote it across the sky.

It can end.

Things can end again.

And things can begin again.
