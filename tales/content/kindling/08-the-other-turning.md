## VIII. The Other Turning

In that world, you listened to the yellow-eyed whisper.

You descended beneath Leyndell, through sewers, graves, and every secret the golden city had pushed below itself. At the bottom, behind a sealed door, the Three Fingers waited.

You opened yourself to them.

The Frenzied Flame entered your flesh.

I felt the change before you confessed it: a wrongness in your grace, like a wasp trapped in amber.

I begged you to turn away.

I want the record to show that I begged, I who had asked so little of you.

I told you what the Frenzied Flame truly wanted. Not rebellion. Not mercy. The end of every boundary—every life, thought, memory, and difference—melted into one thing until nothing remained that could suffer, or love, or be.

And I set against it the entire case for existence, the whole testimony I had gathered from horseback.

Life persists, though it is battered.

Thought persists.

Births continue.

Even in a land this ruined, new things are still arriving, small and furious and unconsulted, and each one is a fresh argument against despair.

A Lord's first duty is to life. Melt the world and you are not its king.

You are only its stain.

You continued.

So I did the last free thing left to me.

I ended our accord.

The strength I had woven into you, I could not take back. So I took back the only other thing I had given: my company. My witness. My name for you.

And I left you with a promise.

If you became the Lord of Frenzied Flame, I would hunt you. I would deliver to you the Destined Death you had tried to remove from the world.

You reached the Forge without me.

You burned the Erdtree with the chaos inside your own body.

Then you returned to Leyndell and let the yellow flame consume everything.

The sky became fire. The boundaries between lives collapsed. Every voice joined one scream, and then even the scream was gone.

Afterward, I walked through what remained.

On a stone platform above the ash, I found a small golden ring burned black.

Torrent's whistle.

It broke apart in my hand.

Torrent had been the first creature to choose me, and the first to choose you. He had trusted us both. He had carried us toward every future you had just erased.

He had been the only soul in our story who was only ever kind.

Give me a moment.

Even ash.

Even ash.

Grief opened what the Golden Order had sealed.

My left eye opened.

The world entered it violet—the color of gloam, the first dark at the end of day. My golden eye had faded. My hair had darkened, as though the shadow of that ancient queen had finally reached the ends of me.

Was I her now? Had I always been? Or had Death, loosed into a masterless world, simply chosen the nearest open hand?

I no longer cared.

Grief burns questions for fuel.

Hear, then, the last vow of Melina: kindling that would not stay lit, maiden of no fingers, daughter of a locked door—sworn on a burned whistle to the Lord of Frenzied Flame, who was once my friend.

Your death was not destroyed.

It was only delayed.

I carry it with me now. I have become its road. And I am coming across the ash of everything we made together to give you the one gift I have left—the gift you tried to spare me, the gift that makes all other gifts mean anything.

An ending.

Wait for me.

You were always so good at that.
