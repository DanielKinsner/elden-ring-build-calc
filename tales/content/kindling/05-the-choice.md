## V. The Choice

Knowledge returned in pieces: the weapon my hands remembered; its words about kindling and Destined Death; the banked heat I had always carried; my mother's ancient war against the Giants; and now the sealed tree before us.

Far to the north, on the Mountaintops of the Giants, a forbidden flame still burned inside an ancient forge.

Marika had ordered the Giants destroyed because their flame could harm the Erdtree. One Giant remained, forced to guard the fire the Golden Order could not extinguish.

My purpose was to release it.

For a time, that knowledge frightened me more than death.

If a purpose is placed inside you before memory, how can you know when you choose it?

Perhaps obedience feels like desire when the command has been buried deeply enough. Perhaps a weapon can learn to speak and still remain a weapon. Perhaps my mother had made a match clever enough to praise the hand that struck it.

I could not answer that question in Leyndell.

Answers about freedom cannot be inherited. They must survive the chance to refuse.

So I returned to you and asked to travel with you again.

This time, there was no bargain hiding inside the invitation. I called myself your travelling companion. I told you the thorns would not open, that the Flame of Ruin could set the Erdtree alight, and that my purpose could guide you toward becoming Lord.

“Hello again, old friend,” I said.

Not maiden. Not guide. Not instrument.

Companion.

We took the road north together.

On the way, another voice tried to turn my doubt against us.

Shabriri wore the body of a dead man and spoke for the Frenzied Flame. He told you the world was too broken to repair. All suffering came from separation, he said, so the answer was to melt every life, every thought, and every difference back into one thing.

He also promised you could save me.

Take the Frenzied Flame into yourself, he said, and your maiden will not need to burn.

It sounded merciful because it turned my choice into a danger being done to me. It made you the rescuer and me the helpless cost.

But destroying every living thing is not rescue. A world without suffering because nothing remains capable of feeling is not healed.

It is erased.

I had watched births continue in the Lands Between. I had watched Boc grieve for his mother. I had watched Morgott die for a tree that never loved him. I had felt your pulse return after death.

Life was wounded, but it was still making new arguments for itself.

The Frenzied Flame would silence all of them.

So I asked you not to take it. I did not ask because I feared dying. I asked because I had judged the world for myself.

And still the harder question remained.

How does a match know it chooses the flame?

I had no clean proof. No one does. A will does not spring untouched from an empty world. Yours was shaped by exile, grace, hunger, and every hand that armed or wounded you. Mine was shaped by a mother, a fire, and years without a body.

But a design is not a verdict.

I could leave you at the rampart, and I did. I could return to fight beside you, and I did. I could accept the yellow flame's false rescue, and I refused.

My mother may have created the fire inside me. She did not choose what I saw on our road. She did not teach me why Boc mattered. She did not make me hear your pulse and want it to continue. She did not return for Morgott.

I did those things.

Freedom is not the absence of influence. It is the ability to examine what made you and decide what deserves to continue through you.

I had read the purpose my mother placed inside me.

I chose to sign it myself.

When you asked what the Forge required, I tried to make the truth small enough not to hurt you.

“Think not of the kindling,” I said. “I shall see to that.”

It failed. You had already done the arithmetic.

You did not seize the decision from me. You did not turn my body into a dispute between your grief and my will. You remained beside me and allowed the sum to be mine.

Call that trust. Call it friendship. Call it love in the broad mortal sense that refuses to own the thing it cannot bear to lose.

I will not narrow it so history can file us more easily.
