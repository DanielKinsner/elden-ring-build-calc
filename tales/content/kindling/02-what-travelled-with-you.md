## II. What Travelled With You

At each site of grace, you brought me the runes of the dead.

Runes are more than currency. They are traces of lives that have ended: memory, strength, and order shaken loose from a body. I could take those traces into my hands and weave them through you. Bone became stronger. Breath lasted longer. A weapon that once dragged at your arm began to answer it.

Before I touched you, I always asked.

“Share them with me,” I would say. “Your thoughts. Your ambitions. The principles you would follow.”

Partly, I needed to know what shape you meant to give the world if you reached the Elden Ring. Partly, I was testing you. Torrent had trusted you in a moment. I required more evidence.

And partly, though I did not admit it then, I wanted an excuse to feel your pulse.

I had hands, but no living body. I remembered warmth without possessing it. When my fingers closed around yours, your heart was there beneath the skin, stubbornly continuing after every sword, claw, fall, poison, and terrible decision you subjected it to.

You died often.

You also kept returning.

There was something almost offensive about the consistency of it. A knight would cut you down. Grace would gather what remained. You would stand again, swear at the knight, and walk back toward the same blade with a slightly better plan.

That was when I began to understand what Torrent had seen.

The Lands Between were full of powerful people who had mistaken endurance for entitlement. You were different. Death had not made you certain. It had made you curious. You explored paths that offered no crown. You stopped for people who could give you almost nothing.

There was a demi-human named Boc, hiding in Limgrave after being cast out by his own kind. He was frightened, ashamed, and very nearly convinced that the world had correctly judged him worthless.

You helped him anyway.

Later, when he followed you as a tailor, I watched him wait beside the grace for your return. I told you when he wished to speak. You always went to him.

He missed his mother. More than power, more than beauty, he wanted to hear her tell him once more that he was worth loving.

I understood purpose. I understood commands. I did not understand that kind of inheritance. My own mother had given me a task, but I could not remember her hand in my hair, or the sound of my name spoken with tenderness. When I reached backward, I found only gold.

Gold is bright. It is not always warm.

Watching you with Boc taught me something no throne ever could. A world is not saved only in battles between gods. Sometimes it is saved when one wounded creature tells another, truthfully, that he is beautiful.

I began collecting moments like that.

The frightened girl asking you to carry a message into a castle you had every reason to avoid. The wolf-man howling above the ruins. A living jar wedged in the earth, roaring about glory while you struck him from behind. The sorcerer too proud to admit he needed help. The dying. The lost. The ridiculous.

You kept stopping.

Not always. You were no saint, and the Lands Between did not reward sainthood. But often enough that a pattern formed. Often enough that I began to ask questions just to hear what you would say. Often enough that when I carried you to the Roundtable Hold, I no longer thought of you as cargo.

Somewhere between one grace and another, I stopped testing you.

I still told myself our accord explained why I remained.

I have always been skilled at giving fear a more dignified name.
