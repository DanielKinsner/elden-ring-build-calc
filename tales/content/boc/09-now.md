## IX. Now

You've been standing there a while, m'lord. You spoke twice. I heard it. I heard the sound of my name in your mouth, which I've not heard before, not once, in all the roads, and I'd not have chosen this for the first time, but I'll take it.

You looked at me.

I want to say that properly, because it's the thing I asked the road for, out of the bark, a year of it, to every boot. Why won't anyone look me in the eye. And you've done it. You came in past her and past the little ones and you stopped, and you got down, the way you got down on the moss, and you put your face in front of this face, and you looked, and you didn't look away.

I don't know what you saw. That's the joke of it, isn't it. I've a new face and I've not seen it. I can see the hands. The hands are, I'd say, alright. I'd say they're the hands of someone who's never had to pick string off the drowned. But the face I'll never see, and you're the only one who has, and you can't tell me, and I couldn't hear it if you did.

Was it worth it? You'd ask that, if you asked things. I'll answer, because I've time for one more answer and that's the one.

I don't know. I don't know, m'lord, and here's the thing: I've stopped needing to. It's the strangest thought I've ever had, in this head or the other. It always mattered. It was the only thing that mattered, from the collar to the rampart. And I knelt here with your face in front of mine and thought it doesn't matter now, and it didn't, and I didn't know what to do with my hands.

You've gone to the light. I saw you go. Don't feel bad about it. You've earned a sit down more than anything alive.

It's going, now.

The little ones were right. It goes from the hem up. That's the only way I've got to say it, and it's the right way; it's what I'd have said about a coat. The road's going first. The lake's already gone; I know I crossed it but I can't see the water. The plateau's going, the gold grass, the crossroads. The gate. I can feel the cliff going, the lake under mist, the whole of the world I sat and looked at for a day, coming loose at the seam and folding back.

I'm keeping what I can. I'm keeping it by saying it, which is the only way I've ever kept anything.

The tree. I'm keeping the tree, the big one, the gold poured up, and I'm keeping that you'll have it. That's not going. That's stitched.

The moss, and the boots coming back up the passage, and you crouching down.

Ten mushrooms.

The sea in a stone.

It's the cave now, m'lord. It's going into the cave. I can feel it, the dark coming up the passage from the fire, and it's taking the chiefs, good, let it, and the heap, and the word that means out, let it have that, I never wanted it. It's taking the fire. It's at the mouth now, where the light comes in grey, and there's a big one sitting there with her head up and a coat across her knees that she's making for nobody.

It's going to take her. I know it is. I'm not a fool; I saw the little one look at its hood.

But I'll tell you what I've done, and then I'll stop, because there's not much of me left to do it with. I've gone through everything I had, the whole of it, road and cliff and gate and rampart, and I've picked the one thing I'll hold when the rest has gone, and I've put it at the front, where m'lord was, where it'll be the last to go. And it's not the needle, though I thought it would be. And it's not your face, though it should be.

It's her voice. Saying the word. The one nobody else had.

And I'll tell you the truth, since it's the last one: I can't tell any more whose voice it is. Hers, or mine saying it in hers, or yours, the way I've filled in yours all these years, kind, and not laughing. It's all one voice now. It's just a voice that says the one thing. I don't know if it's true. I know I was told it. That's enough. That was always enough, and I'm sorry it took me an egg to find that out, and I'm not sorry, and I'm not.

Boc the seamster, m'lord. At your service.
