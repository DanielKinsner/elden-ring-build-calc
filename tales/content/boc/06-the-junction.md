## VI. The Junction

The plateau's another country. You'll know that; you've been to all of them. But I'd not known there was a top to the world and then a further top, gold grass and gold stone and the tree so big now it wasn't a tree, it was the weather. There's a lift up to it, a great iron thing that goes up the cliff face, and I'll not tell you how I got up without it; the rules are that I'm small and I'm ashamed of half the cracks I've used.

There's a crossroads at the top where the roads meet under the tree, and a gold light at it, and I knelt there. I'd started kneeling by then. I don't know when. It started as sitting, on the cliff, and became the other thing somewhere about the gate, and by the plateau I'd go down on both hands and put my face toward the ground when I heard the boots, and it's not that I'd decided to. It's that the body finds the shape that fits the thing it's feeling, and that was the shape.

And I had a thing to ask you, and I'd carried it up the cliff, and I said it before I lost the nerve.

"Master. May I ask you something."

You stopped. You always stopped; it was the least of you and I've never got over it.

"Would you mind," I said, "if I called you lord."

I told you why. I'd heard it on the road, from the book-folk and the soldiers and the dead: that you and the others like you, the ones with the gold in their eyes that had come back over the sea, that you were after the throne. The big one. Elden Lord, they said it like a curse or a prayer depending on who was saying. And I said, "Well. I know that you will be the one."

I did know. I'd known since the mushrooms. And I said the other thing, which was the true thing under the first: "And you'd make just the manner of kind-hearted lord that I'd wish for."

That's what I wanted, m'lord. Not a lord. A kind one. I'd had chiefs. I'd had a heap, and knives held low, and a word that meant out. I wanted there to be somebody at the top of everything who'd take ten mushrooms off a demi-human like they were coin, and I wanted to be allowed to call him by the name of what he was, so that when the world got put back, there'd be one small ugly thing in it who'd known first.

"So please," I said. "If you would. Allow me to call you lord."

You nodded.

I don't know what I'd expected. Not that. I'd expected the thing you do, which is stand, and let me fill in the rest. But you nodded, once, down, and I said, "You don't say," and I said, "Thank you, thank you," and then I said a thing three times because once wasn't enough to hold it. "M'lord. M'lord. M'lord."

I said, "Please, become Elden Lord." I said, "And please. Let I, Boc the seamster, remain at your side."

And then I did the thing I'd been doing without knowing, the hands and the face to the ground, but I did it at you this time, for you, on purpose, and I held it, and when I looked up you'd copied it. You'd gone down on your hands in the gold grass at the crossroads under the tree in a demigod's coat with a golden seam in it and put your face toward me.

Don't. I said it then and I'm saying it now. Don't do that. You're not to bow to me. Get up.

You did it once more, later, at the rampart, when you thought I wasn't looking, to see if you'd got it right. You'd got it right.
