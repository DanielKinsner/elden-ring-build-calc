## XVIII. The Bridge at Stormveil

The light in this room is the color the water in the channel went at the end of the light's hour, and I know what came after. Now thee.

I have looked at thee for the whole of this telling the way I looked at the tree: from underneath, without turning. I am going to turn now, and tell thee the first time I saw thee, because thou wilt remember it as a bridge in the rain and a hammer coming out of nothing, and I remember it as the night the road stopped working.

Hear why that bridge. The storm castle sits on the cliff between the south and everything else; whoever comes up out of the graveyards of the south with grace in them and a mind for the tree comes, in the end, to that bridge, because the road goes over it and the castle squats on the road and the grafted lord in the castle, whatever else he was, was a gate. I had learned to watch gates. I watched the storm gate because it was the first place a Tarnished new-come over the fog had to be more than road-fever to pass, and the ones who passed it were the ones who would come, eventually, up my road to my wall. So I went out there. In the fur. I will not tell thee the manner of the going; the Order's gold does a thing in the hands of what it does not recognize that I have never had a name for, and I went out in a shape that could come back, and the coming back is a thing thou hast seen and I will not explain. I went out to the bridge and I waited for what came up out of the south.

Thou camest up out of the south with a lamp lit in thee that I could see from the far end of the bridge.

I said what I say. Thou hast it by heart, I expect; I have heard that the men over the fog made a jest of it, and I do not mind. Foul Tarnished, in search of the Elden Ring. Emboldened by the flame of ambition. Someone must extinguish thy flame. Let it be Margit the Fell. And I came down the bridge in the rain with the staff and the fur and my horns bare, and I put out my hand, and the gold came into it, and I struck.

Here is the fight from my end, because thou hast only ever had it from thine.

Thou wert nothing. I say that as the first thing I thought and not as the last. A Tarnished of no size, in a dead man's armor, with a weapon that any of the riders would have laughed at and a way of moving that told me thou hadst never seen a thing like me before, which was true of everyone, and a habit of standing too close, which was true of the dead. I put thee down. The hammer, I think, the first time; thou wilt know better. And thou didst go out like a lamp, and I said over thee what I say, that thou shouldst put these foolish ambitions to rest, and that the tree is firmly rooted, always, and I turned to go back to the tree.

And the lamp lit again.

I had seen it before. Do not think I had not; the grace that had been given back to the warriors' bones did not stop giving, and a Tarnished put down on the road got up again from the nearest place the gold had touched and came back up it. I had put the same men down three times, four, and stacked them at last on the fifth. It was the work. But there was a thing about thee that I did not have a word for on the bridge and have found one for on this floor, and it is this: the others came back angrier. Every man I put down came back with more in him of the thing I had put him down for, more heat, more claim, and by the fourth time he was a candle in a gale and easy. Thou didst come back the same. Exactly the same. The same lamp, the same size, the same too-close stance, as if the dying were a thing that had happened to the road and not to thee. I put thee down again. And again. And thou didst come up the bridge again in the rain with the same face and I felt, for the first time in an age of that work, a thing I will call what it was. I was afraid. Not of dying. Of a flame that did not learn.

Then thou didst begin to hurt me.

Hear the words I said when it happened, because I chose them and they are the first true thing I said to thee. Well, thou art of passing skill. Warrior blood must truly run in thy veins, Tarnished. I said it as scorn; I say everything as scorn, I have not had the practice to say things otherwise. But hear it under. Warrior blood. I had spent my life under a city that shouted my father's name, and my father had gone out through the gate with his warriors, and the warriors had died abroad, and their blood had been given back its grace and had come over the fog, and here was some of it, on a bridge, cutting me. I fought my father's warriors' children on that bridge. I have thought since that I fought them as he had never once fought me, which is to say face to face, with the horns bare, and that they gave me more of his attention on that bridge than he had given me in the whole of his lordship, and that I did not know it, and said warrior blood as an insult.

I will not give thee the whole of the fight. Thou hast it in the body, as I have. I will give thee the end.

Thou didst win. I want to say that without ornament, as I said the gate held. A thing of no size in a dead man's armor put the Fell Omen down on the bridge of the storm castle in the rain, and I went to one knee with the staff, and I felt the fur heavy with water and my own blood, warm, that I had not felt outside the stick in an age, coming out of a cut on the arm where the seal did not reach, and I looked up at thee, and thou wert looking at me. Not away. At. The horns and the tail and the old shape, and the lamp in thee still lit, and thou didst not look away, and thou didst not die.

I said: I'll remember thee, Tarnished.

I said the rest too; I said that the flame of ambition leadeth thee astray, and I called on the hands of the Fell Omen to snuff out thy flame, and thou wilt have heard it as the thing a beast says when it has lost and means to come back. It was. And then I went. Not died; thou knowest that, thou hast seen the manner of the going, the gold taking me back to the tree the way the light goes off the water when the hour ends. I went back to this room and stood in it in the hood with a cut on my arm under the sleeve and the city asleep and the seats behind me, and I put my hand on the trunk of the staff and felt the blade in it warm, and I understood that something had happened on that bridge that the road had not been built for.

Here it is. I have told thee I killed men on the road because for the length of a fight I was looked at as what I was. I told thee I did not know that was what I was doing until the bridge. This is the bridge. Thou didst look at me as what I was, and I lost, and thou didst not die, and I could not put the look out. The whole arrangement of my life had been that a thing that saw me died of it, one way or the other; the knight on the wall, the champions on the road, my brother in the channel with my face. And here was a thing that had seen me, whole, in the rain, and was going to go on living with that in it, up my road, toward my wall, with the lamp lit, and there was nothing I could do that would put it back the way it had been. I had said I'll remember thee as a threat. Standing in this room with my hand on the stick I found that it was not a threat. It was a fact. I was going to remember thee whether I wished it or not, and I did not wish it, and I did.

I have one more thing to say about the bridge and then I will go to the road.

Thou wert graceless. I said so, later, in this room, in my first words to thee, and I meant it as I mean everything, twice at once. I meant what the Order means: the Queen took the grace from my father's warriors, and thou art of their line, and the thing that guided thee up the bridge was a grace given back to the dead, not the grace of the chosen, and a man who lives by a returned thing is a man who was once without it. Graceless. And I meant the thing I had never said to anyone and was saying to thee as an insult because it was the only way I had of saying it at all. I meant: thou art what I am. A thing the light fell on and did not enter, once, that came up the road anyway. I called thee by my own name. I did not know that I had.

I said I would remember thee. It was a threat. It was also the first true thing I said to thee.
