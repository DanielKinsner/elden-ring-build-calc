## XIX. The Outer Road

I counted the days between. I had nothing else to count; the heap did not grow, in those days, because the road had gone quiet the way a road goes quiet when something is on it that the others are waiting to see the end of.

Word came up. It comes up to a veiled lord the way it came down to a shackled child: through grates, in other men's mouths. The grafted lord in the storm castle was dead. A Tarnished of no size had gone through his gate and his hall and put him down among his grafted limbs, and taken his piece of the ring, and gone on. I heard it from a bishop who thought it good news, because the grafted one had been a traitor, and I let him think I thought so too. He told me, as a thing worth telling, that the grafted lord had cried out at the end, not to the tree and not to the Queen, but to my father; that he had called on great Godfrey, by name, to bear witness. I said nothing. I had not called on anyone in an age. I have thought on this floor about the difference between us, that the grafted one called and I did not, and I have not finished thinking about it. Then another piece. Whose I will not say, because I did not know then and I am not going to claim now what came to me only as a rumor of a rumor; the moon's country, or the red east, or the mountain, one of the seats behind me had lost its claimant and thou hadst the claimant's gold. Two pieces of the ring in one small hand, coming up the road.

And here is a thing I did not make and could not alter, because it is one of the things about my own house that I did not decide.

There was a rule on my gate. Not mine. The Order's, older than my keeping, set into the road to the capital the way the barbs are set into the trunk: the way was closed to any Tarnished who did not carry two pieces of the ring. The fingers over the fog said it, I am told, to those who asked them; the road itself said it to those who did not. I stood behind a rule I had not made, in a city I had not been given, and watched the Order measure, with its own gold, which of the things coming up the road was fit to reach me. I have thought since that the Order was choosing, even then. Not me. It had never chosen me; it had never been asked to. It was choosing what would come through my door, and it chose thee, and I was the last thing on the far side of its choosing, and I did not know that yet. I only knew that a thing with two pieces of the ring was coming and that the gate would open for it whether I stood there or not.

So I went out one more time.

The road below the outer wall. Thou knowest it; it comes up through the plateau past the heaped field toward the gate, and there is a stretch of it, before the wall, where a man on foot is a long way from anything. I went out there in the fur, in the day this time, or the end of it, and I waited at the side of the road, and here is what I told myself I was doing, and then what I was doing.

I told myself I was going to finish it. That the bridge had been rain and luck and a lamp that did not learn, and that on open ground with the wall at my back and the whole of my strength I would put it out for good, and the road would go back to being a road. That is what a hunter tells himself. Here is what I was doing. I wanted to see thee again before thou camest through the gate. I wanted to see whether the look was still in thee, the one from the bridge, the one that did not go away, and I wanted to see it on my own ground, where I could tell myself afterward that I had been the one looking.

Thou camest up the road. I stepped into it. I said: I see thee, little Tarnished.

Hear the word. Little. I had never in my life used a small word for anyone; I had learned my speech from proclamations, and proclamations do not have small words in them. It came out of me on the road and I heard it, and I went on, because a hunter who stops to hear himself is dead: Smould'ring with that wretched flame of ambition. Tremble now, with fear. For the hands of the Fell Omen will soon be upon thee. To put out thy little flame. Little, again. Twice, in the space of a breath, to a thing I had come out to kill. I have turned that over on this floor and I will give thee what I have. It was not contempt. I know my contempt; I have used it on kings. It was the word one uses for a thing one has decided is not going to be able to hurt one, and it was a lie, and I told it to myself in the only voice I had.

Thou didst put me down on the road as thou hadst on the bridge. I will not tell it twice; it was the same fight with the ground drier and the lamp the same, and at the end of it I was on one knee in the fur with the wall behind me and thou wert standing over me with the look, and I understood that it was not going to be put out on any ground, mine or thine, in rain or in dry, and that I had come out to learn that and had learned it. I went. Thou knowest the manner. Back to the room.

And then I stopped hunting.

Understand what that was; it did not look like anything; a veiled lord in a hood standing in a room is what I had looked like for an age, and I looked like that still. But the fur stayed on its hook. The riders rode without me. The road went on being quiet, and I did not go out onto it, and I stood in this room with the seats behind me and the barbs behind the seats and waited for a thing with two pieces of the ring to come through a gate the Order had told me it would open, and I did not know whether the waiting was the last of my keeping or the first of something I had no word for. I have a word for it now. I have had time. It was the nearest I ever came to faith, and I had it for a thing coming to kill me, and I have decided that this is what faith is for the kind of thing I am: not that the tree will open, but that the thing on the road will arrive.

There was a shape in the sanctuary above this room. I will tell thee of it because thou hast fought it and I have not, and because it wore my father's shape.

Gold. A shade of him, the height of the statue, with the lion and the axe, standing in the hall above the roots where the stair from the city comes up, and I did not make it, and I did not know what made it, and I never once went up to it. It was there when I came up the stair, or it came, as the barbs came, in a time I cannot fix. It fought what climbed to it. I heard it fight thee; the whole room shook with it, and I stood here with my hand on the staff and listened to a thing in my father's shape try to keep thee from me, and I did not go up to help it, and I did not go up to stop it, and I did not go up to look at its face. I had a statue for that. A thing that has imagined a father does not go up and look, any more than it goes in to the mother's room; I let it be, and thou didst put it down, and came on.

I heard thee on the stair. I heard the doors. I had time, between the last door and this one, to do a thing, and here is what I did, because it is the last thing I did with my hands before I did the rest with the sword.

I went to the seats. I had never sat. I did not sit then. I put my hand, in its glove, on the arm of the great one, once, the way one touches the shoulder of a man one is about to leave in a room, and I took the hand away, and I turned to the door thou wert coming through, and I put the hood back so that thou wouldst see the horns when thou camest in, because I had decided, on the road, when the word little came out of me, that whatever happened in this room would happen to my face.
