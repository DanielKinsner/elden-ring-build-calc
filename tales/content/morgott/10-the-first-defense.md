## X. The First Defense

I can hear the great gate from here. Not now; it has been open since thou didst come through it. But I held it long enough that the stone of this room still carries the shape of the sound it makes when it is shut against something, and I want to tell thee about the first time it was, because the Order has cut two lines about it into a monument on the field and the two lines are true and they are not what happened.

The First Defense of Leyndell. A sovereign alliance rots from within. Traces yet remain of bloody conspiracy.

They came in the first years. Thou must understand what the world was then; thou hast walked it after, when the fires had gone down to coals, and it is not the same. The ring was broken and the pieces were in the hands of the Queen's children and the children had gone mad with them, every one, the record says, and had taken countries and raised hosts and named themselves what they pleased. A general in the red east. A praetor on the mountain. A witch in the moon's country. A grafted thing in the storm castle, whom I will come to. And there were lesser lords under and beside them, with pieces of the ring in their keeping or claims on pieces, and some of these looked at the capital and saw a chair with no one in it, and for a while, a short while, they were one thing. An alliance of lords. Sovereign, every one; that is the monument's word and it is exact, because there is no such thing as an alliance of sovereigns, only a delay. Which of my brothers and sisters lent it banners, and which only watched, I do not know; the monument does not say, and I will not.

I saw the banners from the outer wall. I will not tell thee whose, because I have promised thee the truth and the truth is that I knew them only from the drains, as sounds, and the banners were sights, and I could not match them. A great many banners. Siege-work behind them, engines, the sort of thing that takes a season to build and a day to burn; thou hast seen the bones of it on the field outside the wall, half-sunk in the ground now with the corpses of a later war on top. And I stood on the wall in a hood with my staff and the sentinels on their gold horses on either side of me and thought: they have come for the chair. They have come to sit in it. And I found that I did not hate them for it, then. I have hated men for less since and I have had time on this floor to wonder why the first ones got off so lightly, and I think it was that they were the first people I had ever seen who wanted the same thing I wanted, and I had not yet learned that this is the exact thing one cannot forgive.

The gate held. I want to say that without ornament, because the city says it with a great deal. The inner wall of Leyndell has never been breached, not by them and not by anyone since, and the reason is not me, or not only; it is that the soldiers of this city were built for walls the way a keystone is built for an arch, and they stood on them. I stood with them. I fought on the wall, in the hood, at night mostly, when the shape of a thing is harder to make out, and I learned in those nights what my father's lion had been for. There is a thing that comes up in the blood when the wall is under assault and the enemy is close enough to smell, and I had put mine in a stick, and I found that the stick was warm in my hand, and I held the stick tighter and fought with the gold instead. I will tell thee about the gold when I tell thee about the hunting. Here I will only say: the gate held, and I held it, and I was more careful with my own hands in those nights than I have ever been with anything.

And then the alliance rotted.

From within. The monument says so and I will not improve on it. I did not see it happen; no one on the wall did. There was a night when the camp under the walls, which had been a single thing for a season, a single mass of fires and engines and banners, began to fight itself. We heard it before we saw it: the sound of a host discovering its enemy is inside its own lines. Fires went out and came back in the wrong places. By morning half the banners were down and the other half were pointing at each other, and the engines were burning, and a great lord of the grafted line, a thing called Godefroy that had made itself out of other men's limbs in the manner of its house, was taken in chains at the foot of the wall by one of my knights, an honorable man named Kristoff who worshipped the old dragons, and brought inside.

Bloody conspiracy. Traces yet remain. I have gone out to that field, since, in the hood, at night, and I have seen the traces, and I will not tell thee what I made of them, because the third thing the monument does not say is who. Someone put a knife in that alliance from the inside. Someone sent word, or gold, or a woman with a veil, and the sovereigns who had come to sit in my chair turned on each other before they could reach it. It was not me. Set that with the rest, because thou wilt have heard it said that the veiled king was cunning, and I was not; I was a thing that stood on a wall. But I have never been able to say that it was not done for me. There were people in the capital, and above the capital, and under it, who wanted the chair kept empty as much as I did, for reasons that were not mine, and one of them, or several, reached out of the city that night and rotted an army. I have lived my whole reign inside a house that was defended by hands I never saw. I know the feeling. I was raised on it.

Kristoff I gave the tree.

Hear what that is. The Order has an honor, the highest it gives to a man who is not a god: burial in the tree. To be taken into the roots and become part of the gold. The bishops keep the rite, and the rite is the lord's to grant, and I was the lord. And so I, whom the tree would not take alive and will not take dead, stood in this room in the hood and spoke the words that gave an honorable knight the thing I would never have, and did it gladly, and have done it since for others, and this was the first time I understood what kind of king I was going to be. I was going to be the keeper of a gate I could not go through. I was going to hold it open for others with both hands and my horns under a hood.

The rot was inside the walls too. I found that out the way one finds out about damp: by the smell, then by the wall coming away in the hand.

There were men in the capital who did not want a veiled lord. There were men who did not want any lord, who had liked the empty chair. There were, I believe, though I never proved it, men who knew what was under the hood, or guessed, and there were more of them as the years went on; I will come to one. And there had been, before any of them, a night when women in veils walked into the Queen's own house and put knives into her son, and no sentry had seen them pass. I had the torches made. I have told thee for whom. I will tell thee now what they did: they let a man on the wall see a veil for what it was. A flame with a prayer in it that showed the shape of a thing hidden. I gave them to the guardians of the tree in the name of the tree and the Grace-Given Lord, and I stood on the wall at night and watched my own men carry a light that could have shown them, if they had turned it on me, exactly what they were guarding. None of them turned it on me. I have never known whether to be grateful for that.

And I made a hand.

A lord needs one. Not a hand like the ones that fed me; a hand that does what the lord cannot be seen to do. The knights of the city were the city's, and the sentinels were the tree's, and I needed men who were mine, and I found them where I had been found, which is to say among the things the world had thrown away. There was a knight called Oleg. He had been one of two, the Wings of the Storm they were called, and he had been banished, from where and for what I will not say because I did not care then and do not now. I noticed him. That is the record's word and it is the right one; the veiled lord noticed a banished man, the way the shard had noticed me, and took him in, and set him to work. He slew a hundred traitors as the Lord's hand. A hundred. The record kept the count, and I kept the record, and when he died I gave him the tree, and he is in it now, a banished man in the gold, put there by an Omen. His brother-in-arms I asked too. Engvall. He refused. He said, through the man I sent, that he would rather keep watch over a masterless castle for the rest of his life than come to a master who would not show his face, and he did, and he is remembered as a hero of the fringes for it, and I have never blamed him. He was the only man who ever refused me for the right reason.

So. The first defense. An alliance of sovereigns at the gate, rotted from within by a hand I never saw. A grafted lord in chains and an honorable knight in the tree. Torches on the wall that could see through a veil, carried by men who never turned them on the veil beside them. And a hand made out of banished men, killing traitors in the dark for a lord who was, by the Order's own law, the most banished thing in the city.

I made my hand out of men the city had thrown away. I did not see the pattern. Thou mayest.
