## XVII. The Shape in the Cathedral

Far down, under the floor, under the smell and the song and the bell, there is a sound I have heard before. I do not think thou canst hear it. I hear it because it has my breathing in it.

I told thee I went down the stair once as king. I went down it in the hood, at night, with the staff and the shard and a torch of the kind I had made for the walls, and I went past the halls where the horned ones were, and they heard me and did not come out, because a thing in gold with a light is the shape of what kills them, and I let them think it. I did not stop at the halls. I did not stop at the gaol. I went down to the hall with the shape at the end of it, the one the Omen would not enter, the cathedral that someone had built down there for the forsaken, and I went in, because the seal I meant to set had to be set behind its altar, at the top of the way that goes lower, and there was no other way to reach the place.

There was a shape at the altar.

I will give this to thee exactly, and I do not have the words, and I am going to use the ones I have and thou wilt have to make do, as I did. It was an Omen. Unclothed, in the old way, the way we were before there were hoods; horns, and the whole shape, and the height of a door, and it stood at the altar with its back to me the way I stand with my back to the tree. And when it turned, it had my face.

Not like mine. Mine. The one I had not seen since a shaft of light with the water going dark on it and the fire staying. Older, as I was older. Marked, as I was marked. And in its veins, faint, the way the gold is faint in a shackle, the red.

I said his name.

I told thee I had said it to no one since the stair. I said it there, in the hood, to a thing at an altar under the city, and the thing did not answer. It looked at me. It did not speak; I do not think it had speech, or it had it and would not spend it on me. It did not come at me. I had the staff in my hand and the shard warm against my ribs and I was ready for it to come, and it stood, and looked, with my face, and there was nothing in the look. Not hate. Not what he had had in the channel, the pity, in my voice. Nothing. It was the look of a thing that has been left in a place to do a work and is doing it.

I do not know what it was. Put that on the pile, plainly, where thou canst see it, because I have heard the men over the fog make a great deal of it and I would have thee know that the one man in the world who stood before it did not know either. It may have been him. He had gone down into the dark below the standing water, years before, to a mother who wanted his wound; he may have come back up, or left something of himself at the altar of the forsaken's cathedral on the way, the way one leaves a coat on a hook. It may have been a thing made in his shape by the mother in the hole, to keep the way to her. It may have been the dark itself, that had had the two of us in it so long that it had learned our face. I found his shackle. In the pipes near the hall, in the water, the small gold-worked iron with his measure on it, faint, forgotten, as mine was faint; he had taken it off, or it had fallen off, or it had been left there as I have said. I did not take it. I left it in the water. It was not mine to move.

And I set the seal behind it.

Hear what that is, because I have had an age to hear it and it has not gotten easier. I walked past the shape with my face, to the altar, and behind the altar to the mouth of the way that goes down to the thing that laughs, and I set the Order's gold across it in the words the Order used for me, and the thing with my face stood between me and the door while I did it, and when I turned to go it had not moved. It let me pass. It watched me go up. And I understood, on the stair, in the hood, with the torch going out, that I had just used it as a lock. Whatever it was. Him, or his coat on a hook, or the dark wearing us. I had put my seal on the fire below and my brother's shape in front of the seal, and anyone who came down that way toward the madness would meet him first, and I had not asked him, and I could not have, and I left him there.

Now the name. I said I would tell thee what I call the silence when I get to his place in the sentence of traitors, and this is his place.

I have called the others by their titles and their crimes. The general who is mad, the praetor who is blasphemy, the princess who is theft, the twins, the grafted one. I can say them. They are on their seats in my mind as they are on the stone. I cannot say him there, and I have told thee it is not refusal, and here is what it is. A traitor is a thing that had a place and left it. The five had places; they had seats, and pages, and my mother's sentence on the field was said to them, and they heard it and went out and made a war, and that is treason, and I have judged it as such every night of my reign. He had no place. He had a shackle with his measure on it and a stair he could not climb and my face, and he went down instead of up, and whatever he found there, whatever he became, whatever the thing at the altar is, he did not leave a seat to do it. There was no seat. One cannot betray a house that has already put one under the ground. So I have five names and a silence, and the silence is not mercy, though thou mayest call it that. It is the one place in the sentence where the word does not fit, and I will not force it, because I was raised on words forced onto things they did not fit, and I know what that makes.

I have thought, on this floor, about the two of us as locks. He is at the bottom, in front of the fire below, whatever he is. I was at the top, in front of the tree. Two horned things with one face, set at either end of the same dark, keeping the same city from burning from either direction, and neither of us asked, and neither of us ever spoke to the other again after the stair. The city never knew it had two. It did not know it had one. And I do not know whether the thing at the altar knew what I was setting behind it, or cared, or was there for its own mother's reasons that had nothing to do with the tree at all. I know what I did. I do not know what he did. That is the whole of what I can tell thee about my brother, and I have told it.

The sound is still there, under everything. My breathing, in something else's chest, at an altar, waiting. I have never gone back down to find out whether it stopped.
