## XXI. Just As I

I did not die on the floor. Thou knowest that; thou didst come and stand over me. I got as far as the step. I will tell thee that plainly because the city's account of me will have me dying on my feet with the sword up, and I did not; I dragged myself the length of the floor on my hands with the tail behind me and my blood going out along the way, and I got to the step of the great seat, and I put my back against it, and I could go no further, and I had never once in an age been that close to it. Thou camest. I looked up at thee. And I said the last thing I said alive.

Tarnished, thou'rt but a fool. The Erdtree wards off all who deign approach. We are... we are all forsaken. None may claim the title of Elden Lord. Thy deeds shall be met with failure, just as I.

I said that as a verdict. I am going to say it again as the other thing, one sentence at a time, as I promised thee in the prologue, and then I have nothing left to give thee but the room.

Thou'rt but a fool. A fool is a thing that does not learn. I had called thee that on the bridge, in my mind, before I had the word; a flame that did not learn. And I lay against the step and looked up at the thing that had not learned, that had died on my hammer and my sword and my fire and had come back the same size each time, and I understood, with the blood going out of me, that a fool who does not stay dead is a kind of king. I know the kind. I had been one for an age, in a hood, standing in front of a chair, not learning. I called thee fool the way I had called thee graceless, by my own name, and this time I heard it.

The Erdtree wards off all who deign approach. Thou hast had this one: the word, the nights, the gate with my hand on it. I will add only what I saw when I said it: thy face, over me, and behind thy face, past the seats, the trunk with the barbs on it, that I had never once turned to in an age and was looking at now because thou hadst laid me down facing it. I said it looking at the barbs. I said it to them as much as to thee. I was telling the tree, at last, in the only voice I had, that I knew.

We are... we are all forsaken.

I said it twice. I did not mean to. Thou wilt have heard the catch; the men who record such things will have written it down as a dying man's breath failing, and it was not. It was the word coming up. I had put it in a stick with the blood, on the field of heroes, and the blood was loose now and running out of me across the floor and the word came out with it, in her voice, from the field, and I heard it in my mouth and stopped, and then I had to finish the sentence, because a sentence begun in this room must be finished, and I said it again with the rest.

We. I told thee I would tell thee who. Not the demigods; I had never once said we of them, not in an age of saying their names. I meant the two horned things under the city. I meant the one on the step and the one at the altar in the dark. I meant thee, on thy feet over me with the lamp lit, who had been made nothing over the fog and had come up the road anyway; and I meant every thing the light has ever fallen on and not entered, every image with a prayer carved under it, every heap. I meant that my mother had said on a field that whatever failed to become aught would be forsaken, and that I had spent a life keeping her tree to prove her wrong, and that I was lying at the foot of her chair with my blood on its step, and that the sentence was true, and that it had always been about me, and that I was, at the last, inside it, as I had wanted to be inside beloved, and the word that let me in was the other one.

None may claim the title of Elden Lord.

I said may. I have thought since about the word. Not can; I did not say that no one was able. Not will; I did not say that no one would. I said that no one was permitted, and I want thee to hear whose law that was. It was mine. The Order had barred the tree with barbs and the god had gone away and the fingers had fallen silent, and all of that is the record's, but the law that no one may, the permission withheld, was the thing I had made out of my own life and set on this room like a seal. I had never permitted myself. And so I lay on the step and told thee that no one was permitted, and it was the last act of my reign, and it was the same act as the first, and it was a man who was never allowed to want a thing making a law that no one might.

Thy deeds shall be met with failure, just as I.

Here is the last one, and it is the one the men over the fog have made the most of, and I will give thee what I meant and then what I mean.

I meant that thou wouldst go to the tree, as thou didst, and find the barbs, as thou didst, and that the thing I had never permitted myself would refuse thee too, and that I would be proved right from my grave. That is what a dying king means when he says it. And under it I meant the thing I had promised thee at the third gate that I would not say, and I will not say it now either; the gate is sealed and my hand is on it. I will say only this, which is not the thing behind the gate but the thing beside it. I failed. Not at the barbs; I never touched them, and thou canst make of that what thou wilt. I failed at the sentence on the field. I had spent an age trying to become aught; a keeper, a lord, a king, a hand, a horror, a name on a seal. And I had become none of them, because every one of them was a hood, and under every hood was the thing that had been made nothing before it could fail at anything, and it was that thing lying on the step. Just as I. I was telling thee that thou wouldst fail to become what thou hadst come to be. I was telling thee that I had.

Then I died. There is no more to say about that than about the blow. The body stops keeping the record and the thing that goes on is the thing that is talking to thee now, on the floor, and I have told thee I do not know what it is. It is not grace. I would know grace; I spent an age watching it not come. It is something that would not leave the room. The Order has ghosts in it; thou hast met them, the ones who sit in chairs and speak the same line to every passer. I think I am one. I think I am the ghost of a man who would not leave a door, and I think I will stay until the thing I was keeping the door for comes through it, and I do not know what that is. I had thought it was thee. Thou camest, and killed me, and went, and I am still here. So it is something else.

There is a smell in the room now that was not in it when I began. I know what it is. I have spent my life keeping it away from this floor. I will not name it yet.

I called thee by my own names three times, lying here: fool, and graceless, and forsaken. It was the only time in my life I said all of them to a face that was looking at me, and I was dying, and I said them as scorn, because I had no other voice.

None may claim it. I said may. I have thought since about the word.
