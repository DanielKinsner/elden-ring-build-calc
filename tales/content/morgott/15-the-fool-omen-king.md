## XV. The Fool Omen King

Something is singing. I can hear it through the floor, the way I have heard everything through floors, and I do not know who it is, and I will not guess; a voice in the streets in an hour like this could be anyone the city has left. But I know the song. I heard it first from a window of this palace on a night when I could not sleep, which was most nights, and I have heard it since in the barracks and the taverns, and I will give thee the part of it that is mine.

There is only one tree, and only its branches, that bathe in true rays of gold. Not the fool Omen King. Nor the rank malformed twins.

Fool Omen King. Hear how the city said it, because the city said it while it also said Lord of Grace, and I have spent an age inside the space between the two.

And hear the other word in it. Twins. The palace never once said that word aloud in an age, and a tavern sang it. The city always half knew. Whoever made that song was safer than the knight I am about to give thee, who saw one horn under a hood and kept it; the song saw two and sang, and no one thought to ask it how.

The city loved me. I will say that once and let it stand, because it is true in the way the noble's speech is true: correct in every particular and false as a whole. It loved the tall old man in the hood who spoke the tree's words and kept the walls and rang the bell for the tree's hours and gave the tree to its heroes. It said his reign was great. It said that in the years of the war, while the world outside the walls went to rot and fire and the moon's cold, the capital revelled in eternal grace, and it was not lying; the streets were gold, and the sentinels were gold, and the great dragon lay dead on the rampart where my brother's war had left it, kept, for the view, and the bells rang, and the people who had been chosen by grace went about being chosen. They called the man in the hood their lord. They wanted audiences with him. They wrote his name on the seals of the doors they wanted kept shut, with the word they had given him after it, so that a man coming to a sealed door in the sewers or on the north road would read Sealed by Morgott the Grace-Given and know that the sealing was holy.

And it hated me. Not the man in the hood. The Omen. It hated the thing under the city that bellowed, and it hated the grotesque Fell Omen on the road who stacked the corpses of heroes, and it sang, in its taverns, about the fool Omen King who did not bathe in the true rays of gold, and it never once, in all the years, put the three together. The city had three of me and loved one and hated two and did not know it had only one. I have thought about that on the wall until the thought wore smooth. A veil is not a thing that hides a face. A veil is a thing that lets a city have as many faces as it needs.

One knew.

I told thee I would come to him. I will not give thee his name; it is the only name in this telling I have kept back on purpose, and I keep it because he is dead and did not choose to be in a king's account. A knight of the city. A good one, I think, in the way the city means good; he had the wall at the eastern gate, and I had passed him on my rounds for years, and he had bowed as they all bowed.

It was in the years of the second war. The assault came at the end of a day, which they did not often do, and so I was in the hood and the gold and not the fur, and the day went into dark with the fighting still on the parapet, and the torches were lit, mine, with the prayer in them, and by their light the men on the wall could see through any veil that came at them. Something came over the parapet that was not veiled. Large; one of the champions' beasts, or one of the champions; I did not have time to know. It took two of the soldiers off the wall in the time it takes to say so and it came at me, and I had the staff in one hand and the gold in the other and the thing was too close for either, and I met it with both hands. The horns do the rest, in a fight like that. A thing with horns lowers its head. The hood went back. Not far. Far enough.

He was beside me. He had come up the wall to the place where his lord was, and he was an arm's length from me when the thing died on the stone between us, and I straightened, and the torch was in his hand, and its light was on the side of my face. He saw the horns. He saw the old shape under the gold where the hood had pulled the cloth from the shoulder. And he saw me see him see it. I put the hood up. I remember the exact weight of the cloth going back over the bone. And we stood there, two men on a wall at night with a dead thing between us and the torches burning with a prayer in them that had been made to show the hidden, and neither of us said a word. He bowed. I nodded. He went back along the wall to his gate, and I went down the stair into the city with the gold cloth over my face and did not sleep that night or the next, and on the third day he was at his post, and bowed.

He did not tell. I want that on the scale, in his favor; it is the only entry on the scale that is someone else's. He could have gone to the bishops the next morning and ended the veil and the lord and the city's peace with one sentence, and he did not, and I never knew why, and I did not ask, and I did not have him killed. Mark that too. I had a hand made of banished men who slew traitors in the dark, and I did not send it to him, though I lay awake and thought about it more than once. I let him be. He let me be. We passed each other for years after, in corridors, on the wall, once at a burial where he stood in the second rank and I spoke the words; he bowed as the others bowed, and I nodded as I nodded to the others, and under the bow and the nod there was a thing the size of the city that neither of us touched. I have thought, since, that he was the only man in Leyndell I ever had a thing in common with, and that the thing was me.

He died in the last war. Not by me. I heard, after, from a man who had been near him, what he said as he went, and I am going to give it to thee because I promised the truth and because I have carried it since as one carries a stone in a boot. He said: kill our lord. He said: he is a stain upon all things golden. He said: kill Morgott, kingly impostor. King of deceit. That was what the years had made of what he saw on the wall. Not a secret kept for a lord. A stain kept for a city, until he could not keep it any longer and let it out with his life.

I have had worse titles. I have had truer ones.

Hear that as I mean it. Impostor is not the wrong word. I was a thing in a hood pretending to be a thing the tree had sent; I have told thee I let the name stand, and I did not correct the spelling, and a man who lets himself be called Grace-Given when the stone says graceless has earned impostor from anyone who catches him at it. And it is not the right word either, and I will tell thee why, and then I will leave it. An impostor takes a thing that is someone else's. I took a chair no one was in and stood in front of it. I took a name no one was using and answered to it. I took a city no one was keeping and kept it. Thou canst call that deceit; he did, and he was owed the word. But the thing I was pretending to be was not another man. It was a man at all. That is the whole of my imposture. I pretended to be a man, in a hood, for an age, and the city was glad of it, and one knight saw the horns and called it a lie, and it was.

There is a sentence I said to the seats every night of my reign, by title, quickly, the way I gave the bishops their audiences. The general who is mad. The praetor who is blasphemy. The princess who is theft. The twins. The grafted one. Traitors. I knew them as the city knew them, by report and by monument and by the word the bishops used; I have never sat with one of them at a table, and I have never heard one of them say my name, and I said the sentence so often that it had no meaning left but the sound, and thou heardest it from me in this room at the end, and I will take it apart for thee when we come to that.

It has five names. Thou wilt have noticed. The sixth I did not say to the seats and did not say to thee, and it is not one of the three gates, because it is not a thing I refuse. It is a thing my mouth will not do. I will tell thee what I call that when I tell thee about the shape in the cathedral.

The song is still going, under the floor. Fool Omen King. I have never minded that one. Of all the names the city gave me it is the only one that was said by people who would have said it to my face.

Kingly impostor, he said. I have had worse titles. I have had truer ones.
