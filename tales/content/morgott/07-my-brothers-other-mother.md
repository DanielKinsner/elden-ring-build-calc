## VII. My Brother's Other Mother

Mohg.

There. It is said. I have said it to no one since the day I came up the stair, and I have heard it said of him by others in every tone the tongue has, and I have let them say it and said nothing, and I find, now, that it comes out of me in the voice I had when we were the same height. That is what a name does when it has been kept. It keeps the age at which it was last used.

He listened down. The water stood, in the lower channels, and stank, and held things. I have not told thee that he went there, at the end, not to look but to be looked at, because I did not understand that until he came back.

He was gone a long time. Days, I think; there is no day under the city, only the light's hour, and the light came and went several times with the shaft empty beside me. I did not go after him. I have asked myself why, on ramparts, at night, and the answer I give myself is that the shackle held me to the stair and he had gone away from it, and the answer is true and is not the reason. The reason is that I knew where he had gone, in the way that one knows where one's own hand is in the dark, and I did not want to see it.

He came back with fire in him.

Not on him. In. He came up out of the lower channels into the light's hour and stood at the edge of the shaft, and he did not come into the light, and I saw that the veins of his arms were lit. Red, under the skin, the way a lamp is red through a hand held over it. The horns had it too, at the roots, where mine got hot. His eyes had it. He was smiling, and it was my smile, and I had never seen my own smile before and I have not liked it since.

I asked him what he had found. I give thee what he said as I have it; I have turned it over so long that the edges are worn, but the shape is his.

He said he had gone down past where the water stood, into the dark that the other Omen would not enter, and that at the bottom of it there was a mother. Not the Queen. He said the word for the Queen the way one spits. A mother of truth, he said, without a form, who wanted only one thing of the things that came to her, and the thing she wanted was a wound. He said he had stood before her and she had looked at what was in his blood, the accursed thing, the heat, the shame the tree had put under the ground, and she had not looked away. She had wanted it. She had desired it the way the tree desired gold. And the blood in him had answered her and caught, and now it burned, and it would go on burning, and it was the first time in his life that anything had asked him for exactly what he was.

I said that it was a thing in a hole.

He said that a thing in a hole was what we were.

I said that the tree was the mother, whatever it had done, because it was the tree, because everything came back to it, because the light came down the shaft from it and had come down every day of our lives, and that a thing that wanted our wounds was not a mother but a mouth.

He said that the tree had never once wanted anything from us except that we not exist, and that I had made a religion out of being un-wanted, and that if I wished to spend my life sitting in a puddle of someone else's light pretending it was meant for me he would not stop me, but he would not watch it, either, not any longer, because it made him ashamed, and he had found something that did not.

I said that the thing in the hole had made his blood into a lamp for its own use and he called that love.

He said yes. He said it was love. He said she loved what we were, the mire, the horn, the heat, all of it, without asking it to be anything else first, and that this was what the word meant, and that I would never in my life hear it from the thing I sat under, and I knew it, and sat there anyway, and that he did not hate me for it. He said he pitied me. He said it in my voice.

I did not answer. Thou knowest by now what I do when he is right.

But here is what I saw while he was speaking, because it is the thing I have never been able to get out of my eyes. The light's hour was ending. The gold on the water was going. And my brother stood at the edge of it, with my face and my horns and my shackle on his wrist and the fire in his veins, and as the light went off the water the fire in him did not go with it. It stayed. It was the only light in the channel. And I understood, with the whole of my body, that he had gone into the dark and come back with a light of his own, and I had sat in a borrowed one for years and had nothing, and that the thing I had decided to be my own lion about was already, in him, loose.

He went down again after that. Not at once; there were more days, and more words, most of which I will not give thee because they were only the same words worn thinner. He came to the stair once. I remember it because he never came to the stair. He stood at the line where his shackle went tight, beside me, and looked up it, and said that if I ever got to the top I should tell the Queen that one of her sons had found a mother who would have him, and that the other was still waiting on the step. Then he went down, past the standing water, and I did not see him again in that dark, and the next time I saw anything with his face on it I was a king and it was not him, or I do not think it was, and I will tell thee about that when it comes.

Now the night I promised thee. It was that night. I have never told anyone that the two are one thing and I am telling thee.

I had a staff. I had fished it out of the water years before, a length of old hard wood with the marks of a tool on it, from what I do not know; something the city had been done with. I had used it to walk, because my tail, which none of the other Omen had, threw my balance, and a staff helped, and because I liked having a thing in my hand that had been made by someone for a purpose. I took it to the shaft, in the dark, with the light gone. I took a shard of slate that I kept for cutting. And I opened my arm over the wood.

The blood came out hot. It was not burning, as his was; it had not been asked for by anything. But it was not cold either, and in the dark I could see it, faintly, the way one can see a coal that has been out for a while. I let it run onto the wood. And I said the prayers I had learned from the outside of the grate, in the shape of the mouths above, aloud this time, and between the prayers I told the blood what I had decided. I told it that it was not mine. That is what recanting is, if thou hast never done it: it is telling a thing that came out of thee that thou hast never known it. I told it that it belonged to the dark it had come from and not to me, and that I was going to hold it down as the lion holds my father down, and that it would go into this wood and stay there, and that if the tree ever wanted it, the tree could come and take it, and until then it would be sealed.

It went into the wood. I do not know how to say that so that thou wilt believe it and I am not going to try; thou hast held the blade, and thou knowest what it is. The blood ran into the grain and did not run out. It set. Over years it set harder, and took an edge, and the edge was of no color that I can name, because it shifted, the way the light on the water shifted, and I understood that I had made a thing that was half the tree's light and half the mire's heat and that it was going to be the only weapon I ever had that was truly mine. I kept it in the staff. I closed the wood over it. I walked on it. I was the only man in the world who used his own blood as a walking-stick, and I did it for an age, and no one ever knew what they were looking at when they saw the veiled king lean on his cane.

Set us side by side, once, and hear it plainly.

My brother went down to a thing that wanted his blood and he gave it, and it lit him. I stayed by a thing that did not want mine and I took it out of myself and shut it in a stick, so that I could go on sitting in its light without the heat showing. He did to himself what a lover does. I did to myself what the Order does. I put the shameful thing where it could not be seen. I have known that since the night I did it and I have never once been able to make it mean that he was right, and I have never once been able to make it mean that I was.

He went down. I went up. Neither of us went out.
