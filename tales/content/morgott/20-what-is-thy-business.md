## XX. What Is Thy Business With These Thrones

The fight is the room I lie in. I do not have to remember it; I have only to look, and the floor tells me where I stood, and the seats tell me where I bled. The light on the floor has gone white at its edges while I have been telling thee this. I note it, and go on.

Thou camest in. I came out of the gold in front of the seats, as I had come out of it on the bridge and the road, and I had the hood back, and I looked at thee, and thou wert the same size. I had known thou wouldst be. I said the words. I will give them to thee whole and then take them apart, because I have taken them apart on this floor a hundred times and I would have thee see the pieces.

Graceless Tarnished. What is thy business with these thrones? Ahh... Godrick the Golden. The twin prodigies, Miquella and Malenia. General Radahn. Praetor Rykard. Lunar Princess Ranni. Wilful traitors, all. Thy kind are all of a piece. Pillagers. Emboldened by the flame of ambition. Have it writ upon thy meagre grave: Felled by King Morgott! Last of all kings.

Graceless. I have given thee that one; it was my name for myself in thy direction, and I did not know it.

What is thy business with these thrones. A question. I asked thee a question, the only one I ever asked thee, and I did not wait for the answer, because I had been asking it of the empty seats every night for an age and had never once gotten one, and I did not know how to stand in a room and wait for a reply. I did not ask what thy business was with the tree, or the ring, or the chair. I asked about the thrones. All of them. I had kept them empty for an age and I wanted to know, before I killed thee, whether thou hadst come to fill one.

Ahh. Thou wilt have heard that as a beast's breath. It was the sound I made every night when I got to the roll of them. I made it in front of thee because I had made it so often alone that it had worn a groove, and the groove was there, and my breath went into it.

Godrick the Golden. The grafted one called himself that, and I gave it back to him in the voice the city uses for a thing it has decided to laugh at, and of all the names on the roll his is the one I said with the most in it, because he was the only one of them who had been in this city, in this house, and had run from it, and had made himself lord of a castle out of other men's arms rather than stay in the one place that was his by blood and keep it. He was of my father's line. He was, the city said, the last of it. He was the nearest thing I had to a kinsman above the ground, and he had fled among the women, and I put his name first and I put Golden after it, and that was the whole of what I ever said to him.

The rest of the roll thou hast had. The twins, the general, the praetor, the princess. Titles, as the city said them. And then the silence where the sixth should be, which thou didst not hear, because a silence in a roll of names is heard only by the one who is not saying it.

Wilful traitors, all. The verdict. I had said it to the seats until it was only sound.

Thy kind are all of a piece. That is the one I have thought about most on this floor. All of a piece. I said it of thee and thy kind, the Tarnished, the ones who came up the road with the lamps, and I meant that ye were all the same thing, all ambition, all pillage, and that killing one was killing all. And the thing under it, that I did not hear until I lay here, is that I had spent my life being told that my kind were all of a piece, the horned ones, the things under the city, one bellowing mass that a viscount could ask to have expunged, and I had climbed out of that sentence by being the one that was different, and I stood in this room and put thee in the same sentence and called it justice.

Have it writ upon thy meagre grave. Felled by King Morgott. Last of all kings.

I have given thee the grave. I have given thee felled. Hear last. I meant three things by it and I will give thee all three and thou canst choose. I meant that there would be no king after me; that I had kept the seats empty for an age and would go on keeping them empty from the far side of thy grave, and that the age of kings in this city ended at me because I had decided it would. I meant that I was the last of my father's line that the city would not name, standing; the golden brother dead, the grafted one dead by thy hand, the sixth in the dark, and only the fool Omen King left on his feet in the house. And I meant, under both, the thing I had never said. That I was the last because I was the least. That the Order's kings ran from my father in his gold down to me in my hood, and I was where the line ended, in the dark, in the old shape, and last was the kindest word for that.

Then the staff.

Thou hast seen it, so I will only tell thee what it was to do. I raised the stick I had walked on for an age, and I broke it. The wood came away, and the thing I had put in it came out. My blood. Set, as I told thee, over years, into an edge of no color that I can name, warped, the way a thing is warped that has been held in a shape it was not meant for. I had never drawn it. Not on the bridge, not on the road, not on the wall; on the wall I had fought with the gold. I drew it in this room because the gold was not going to be enough and I knew it, and because I had decided on the road that whatever happened here would happen to my face, and the face was not whole without the blood. I held the only weapon I had ever made in my hand and it was the thing I had recanted, and I went at thee with it.

I will give thee the fight as I have it: in the body.

The gold came when I called it, as it always had. A hammer out of nothing, a spear, a great blade, daggers thrown like a fistful of light; the Order's weapons, that answered a horned mouth without knowing it. I used them all. I used the old shape too, the tail, which none of the other Omen have, the oldest thing on me, the thing that had made me lean on a stick since I was a child, and I swept the floor of this room with it and caught thee with it more than once, and I felt, each time, a thing I had not felt since the fur: that I was fighting with all of what I was, the gold and the blood and the tail, in the room where I had spent an age pretending to be only the gold. I put thee down. I said over thee what I say in this room, that the curse should seep to thy very soul, an apt reward for thy brash ambition; and I meant it, and the lamp lit again, and thou didst come back through the door.

And then the blood caught.

I do not know how else to say it. There is a place in a fight where a thing that has been held down for an age comes up on its own, and the sword in my hand, that had been my recanted blood set hard, began to burn. Red. The way his veins had burned in the channel with the light going off the water. It ran down the edge and it did not go out, and I struck with it and it left fire on the floor of the room, my fire, on the roots, before the seats, and I heard myself say the thing thou heardest.

The thrones. Stained by my curse. Such shame I cannot bear. Thy part in this shall not be forgiven.

Hear it now, from the floor. The seats had been clean for an age. I had kept them so; it was the work. No claimant had sat in them, no traitor had reached them, no ambition had so much as touched their wood. And here at the end, with the only true weapon I had in my hand, the thing that stained them was me. My blood, on fire, the thing I had put under the ground with my own hands and sealed in a stick, was on the floor of the throne room in the sight of the tree, and the shame was not that thou hadst brought it there. The shame was that I had. The one thing I had kept the seats clean from was the thing I was, and I could not fight thee without letting it out, and I could not bear it, and I said so, and I blamed thee, because a man with fire coming out of his own blood in front of his mother's chairs has to say something and I had only ever learned one kind of thing to say.

There was a woman.

I will give thee this once and not explain it, because I cannot. There was a moment in the fight, near the end, when thou wert not alone. A woman stood beside thee made of smoke and light, with one eye shut, and a blade in her hand that knew this room; I cannot say how I knew that, only that the way she held it was the way one holds a thing in the place it was made. She fought me. She fought well, as if she had been born at the foot of the tree, and I did not know her, and I do not, and I am not going to guess, because I have already told thee what I think of a life spent guessing at a family. She was there, and then the fight was past her, and I have put her on the pile with the rest of what I do not know, and I would ask thee, if thou knowest, not to tell me. I have enough.

I fell.

Thou wilt know the blow; I do not. There is a point where the body stops keeping the record. I know that I was on the floor with the sword under me and the fire going out along the edge, and that the seats were behind me, where they have always been, and that I had kept them for an age and they were clean, every one, except where I had bled on the step of the great one, going down.

I fell. The seats were behind me. They were clean, except for me.
