## Coda: Long and Hard

Someone in the sanctuary is crying out. A spirit; one of the Order's, the kind that sits in a chair and says its one thing. It is saying: he is returned. The Lord has returned at long last. Our very first lord, to brandish the Elden Ring once more.

I know the step. I have never heard it. I know it.

He comes into the room the way the statue would come into it if the statue could walk: the height of it, the width, the axe, and the lion on his back with its old eyes open, looking at everything, as it has looked at everything for an age from a man's shoulders. He is not gold. I had thought he would be gold; the shade above was, and the statue is veined with it, and the whole of the city's memory of him is cut in it. He is not. He is a man, grey at the edges, with his eyes the color of the ash on the seats, and I understand, before he has taken two steps, why. The Queen took the light from his eyes at the gate. He died abroad. He came back over the fog with the rest of them, with grace given back to his bones and not to his eyes, and the eyes are what I am looking at, and they have no gold in them.

Neither have mine. Neither had they ever.

He sees what is on the step. He comes across the floor through the ash and I cannot rise and would not, and he kneels. Mark that, because the city will make a song of it and the song will be wrong in the particular that matters. He does not stand over me. He kneels in the ash of his wife's tree in front of the chair he once sat in, and he puts his hands under what is left of me, and he lifts it. Hands. Thou knowest what the word was for, in this account: the hands that fed us without looking, the hands of the Fell Omen that put out the lamps, the hand I made of banished men, the hand I never raised to the barbs. These are not those. These are under me. I did not know a hand could be under a thing. I had only ever known them above.

He says: It's been a long while, Morgott.

My name. In his mouth. I do not know who gave it to me, or where; I have it as one has a scar. He has it. He says it the way one says a thing one has carried; not learned on the stair just now, not read off a seal; carried. I do not know from where. That is his gate and I said I would not open it for him and I will not now, with his hands under me; whether he knew of us at the top of the stair, whether he counted us among the kinfolk and could not take us, whether he learned it over the fog from a shade or a rumor or the tree itself when the tree burned, I do not know, and he is not going to tell me, and I find, with the ash coming down between his face and mine, that I do not need it. He has the name. He has had it a long while. That is what he said.

Then he says the other thing, and I am going to give it to thee in his words and then I am going to stop, because there is nothing after it that is mine.

Long and hard didst thou fight.

I had. No one had said so.

Thou hast the Order's account of me, the sentence on the stone above this room, the one I said I would not have softened by any hand. That I was not loved and loved regardless. Hear it now, from the floor, with the hands under me, on the lowest floor there is. It is true and it has the order wrong. I was not loved and I loved. That is how it went; that is the order the years came in. And here, at the end, with the tree burning and the city grey and the seats stained by the one thing I had kept them from, the other half arrives. I am loved. A man with my name in his mouth and no gold in his eyes has come across the ash and put his hands under me and told me I fought well, and it is the first time, and it is the last, and I cannot return it. Not because I will not. Because there is nothing left in me to return it with. I loved for an age with nothing coming back and it made me what I was, and now the thing comes back and there is no one on the step to receive it but a shape in the arms of the man who carries a lion, going to gold.

It is starting. I can feel it in the horns first, which is where everything started: the gold coming into the old shape, at last, not as light on it but as itself, taking it apart. The tree is taking me. Burning, with the barbs on it, with no god behind it and ash on its chairs, it is taking the thing it would not look at, into the roots, in my father's hands. I do not know whether that is grace. I would know grace; I spent an age watching it not come. This is not that. This is only the tree, at the end, doing with me what it does with the dead, because I am dead, and I am at its foot, and there is nothing left in the Order to say that I may not be.

He is turning to thee. I can hear it; his voice is going past me now, over the ash, to the one who came through the door. He is calling thee warrior. He is saying thou wert spurned by the grace of gold. He is saying it the way he said my name.

I want to say the word. The stone's word, the one I have kept off my tongue through the whole of this account because it was the Order's and I would not use the Order's word for the only thing I ever did that was not the Order's.
