## Prologue: Writ Upon a Meagre Grave

A first-person Elden Ring literary fan work, written for audio. Prologue, twenty-two movements, and a coda.

Have it writ upon thy meagre grave. I said that to thee. I said it with the whole of my breath and a sword in my hand, and I meant every word of it, and I was wrong about only one. The grave is mine.

So carve this instead.

I am lying where I fell. Thou knowest the place; thou didst make it. The floor of the room before the seats, with the roots of the tree under the stone and the stone under my back. Thou art gone. I felt the seal on the north road give when my heart did, the way a gate feels a bolt drawn on the far side, and I know which way thou hast gone because there was only one way left for thee to go. I will come to the seal. I will come to all of it. But understand first what this is, because I have judged a great many things in my time and I know the shape of a judgment, and this is not one.

It is not a plea. I have never made one. Not to the Queen, not to the tree, not to the men who called me king while I stood in front of a chair I never sat in. I did not learn the form of a plea in the place where I was raised, and I am too old to learn it now, on a floor, with no one in the room.

It is not a defense. A defense is offered to someone who may pardon. There is no one here who may.

And it is not the Order's account. The Order keeps its accounts in gold, on stone, and it has had a long time to write mine. Thou hast seen what it wrote. It wrote Grace-Given over a gate I sealed and Omen King over a body I could not hide. It wrote both of those things and never once put them in the same sentence, because to do so would have required the Order to look at me, and looking at me is the one thing it never did. I will put them in the same sentence. That is what this is. Nothing more. A man who was named twice, saying both names in one breath, so that at least once they are heard together.

Why speak at all. I asked it of the room, and the room gave me the answer it gives everything, which is the sound of the tree breathing. So I will answer for it.

Because thou didst not stay dead.

I have put out a great many flames. I was good at it. I was, I am told, a horror to those who carried them, and I took the word as a title because it was the nearest thing to a title I was ever offered by anyone but myself. I put thine out on a bridge, and on a road, and thou didst come back with it lit each time, and when at last thou didst stand in this room I looked at thee and understood something I had spent an age refusing to understand. A flame that will not stay out is not ambition. I had been calling it ambition for so long that the word had worn smooth. It is something else, and I do not have a name for it, and I am going to spend what is left of me looking for one.

So. This is what thou wilt have of me, and in what order, because I have always been particular about order; it was the only thing I was ever permitted to be particular about.

Thou wilt have the stair. Thou wilt have the water, and the word they had for us, and the man who took his kin and left, and a brother made of light I saw once through a grate. Thou wilt have the tree from underneath, which is the only way I ever knew it until I stood before it old. Thou wilt have my twin, and what he found in the deep, and what I found instead. The night of knives. The empty seats. The name I did not choose and did not refuse. The gate I held and the road I hunted and the city I kept, and beneath the city the thing I also kept, which I will not pass over, because I have never in my life passed over a thing to make myself look better and I am not going to begin with an audience of one.

There are three gates in this telling I will show thee and not open. I will tell thee which they are when we reach them. I have sealed enough things in my time that I know how to make a seal honest: thou shalt see it, and thou shalt know it is mine.

And then thou wilt have the bridge, and the road, and this room, and the words I said to thee lying here, which I said as a verdict and which I will say again, slower, as something else.

I do not know how long I have. Something is happening to the light. I have lain in this room long enough to know its hours and this is not one of them; the gold is going the wrong way. I think thou hast done what I told thee could not be done, or art doing it. I think I will hear it before the end. That is a strange thing to wait for. I have waited for worse.

Begin, then, where they put me: under the city, before I had a name that anyone above it could say.
